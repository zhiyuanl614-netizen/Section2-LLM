Robust optimal self-scheduling of potable water and power producers under
uncertain electricity prices

H I G H L I G H T S
• Robust optimization based self-scheduling of fresh water and power producers is presented.
• No need to probability density and membership functions for modeling price uncertainty.
• Both risk-neutral and risk-aversion generation schedules are optimally obtained.
• Sensitivity of water-energy nexus schedules to budget of uncertainty is analyzed.

A B S T R A C T
Recently, seawater desalination and power generation units are optimally co-scheduled because of inter-
connection between electricity and water networks. A typical water-energy hub grid consists of the conventional
thermal power plants and the combined water and power (CWP) generating units. In the CWP plants, the waste
heat of the ﬂue gases exhausted from the power generation process is utilized for water treatment. If the water
and power generation company aims to maximize its daily proﬁt, the electricity price uncertainty will aﬀect the
optimum operating point of the generating units. In other words, the ﬂuctuations of the energy prices cause the
power generation patterns of thermal and CWP units to change. Hence, this paper implements a robust opti-
mization strategy on water-power nexus model to handle the uncertainty of the electricity price with no need for
its probability distribution and membership functions. The lower and upper bounds and the forecasted prices are
used for solving the robust mixed-integer non-linear program and making the risk-averse decisions against the
uncertainty. It is indicated that the proposed approach is suitable for the price taker water-power cogeneration
companies, which seek the optimal schedule of their thermal and CWP units for a certain operating period.
1. Introduction
In recent years, 40% of the global population face with the water
scarcity, environmental pollution, and the limited capacities of the
fossil fuels based power generation stations [1]. The higher energy
consumption in developing countries such as Iran has caused the huge
volume of the pure water is utilized by the conventional steam turbine
power plants [2,3]. In other words, the desalted water is utilized in the
energy procurement processes such as thermally driven power plants
and the electricity is consumed by the gross water treatment units and
the fresh water distribution equipment such as pumps [4,5]. It is fore-
casted that the global water and electricity demands are supposed to
increase by 50% and 80% in 2050 [6,7]. In the tropical areas, the water
scarcity and the energy crisis usually increase due to the temperature
rise, which may lead to the load-generation imbalance and the forced
outages of power and potable water distribution lines [8]. Therefore,
the water and energy networks should be co-operated in order to
minimize the fuel cost of power only units and cogeneration plants
[9,10].
Many researchers have proposed the innovative designs of the re-
newable energy resources based interconnected water and power gen-
eration systems and optimized their performance from the energy, ex-
ergy, economic, environmental, eﬃciency points of view. Jabari et al.
[9] presented a water-heat-power nexus model and optimized its

tems is related to the water extraction process. Although the waste
water recycling utilizes a large portion of the wind power, the com-
partment of the ground/surface water is beneﬁciary from the waste
treatment. Some factors such as the old types and high corrosion of the
constructed pipelines, maintenance of pumps and valves, and the me-
chanical damage cause the underground water system leakage [13]. To
solve this issue, the pressure of the pumping stage should be arisen,
which makes the water leakage worse, as well as more electricity will
be consumed by the water pumps [14]. The water leakage detection
tools reduce the energy requirement of the water distribution systems
[15]. Authors of [16–18] proved that the ocean thermal energy
conversion based binary cycle generates the electrical power and de-
salted water. Authors of [19] studied the potential of the wave energy
powered desalination plant to solve the potable water scarcity of the
mid-latitude areas such as Canary Islands. Oﬀshore compressed air
energy storage (OCAES) [20] and undersea pumped storage system
[21] could be integrated with marine, tidal and ocean wave based en-
ergy production facilities to increase eﬃciency and output power.
In [22], the biogas-fueled Brayton cycle is coupled with a humidi-
ﬁcation-dehumidiﬁcation (HDH) desalination unit to supply the energy
and fresh water demands of the industrial consumer. The uncertainty of
loads and climatic condition could be modeled using the information
gap decision theory to make the robust or risk-aversion and the op-
portunistic or risk-taker decisions and minimize the capital investment,
maintenance and the operating costs of the multi-generation system
[23]. In addition, bioethanol [24] and biodiesel [25] could be used
instead of biogas to improve the overall eﬃciency of the cogeneration
system. The optimal design of the grid-tied and oﬀ-grid solar PV/diesel
generator/battery/inverter power system for coupling with seawater
desalination unit is provided by Ghenaei et al. [26]. It is recommended
to make its performance more robust against the uncertainty of the
solar irradiance. For instance, molten salt thermal storage should be
installed for saving heat at hot hours and discharging it for desalting
water and generating power at night or cloudy sky condition [27]. In
[28,29], organic Rankine cycle (ORC) is used for generating heat and
power for membrane distillation and reverse osmosis cycles, respec-
tively. The output brine of the reverse osmosis process is reheated by
the surplus heat exhausted from the ORC to treat more fresh water from
the membrane distillation procedure. It is found that integration of
membrane distillation with ORC coupled reverse osmosis unit improves
both economic saving and environmental aspects associated with nat-
ural gas utilization. In comparison with membrane distillation ap-
proach, the implementation of HDH [30], forward osmosis [31], and
multi-stage ﬂash desalination [32] to the ORC-reverse osmosis com-
bined cycle could result in better emission footprint, eﬃciency, and
power consumption, respectively.
Authors of [33] proved that the integration of Brayton, multi-eﬀect
desalination and vapor compression cycles using a dual-pressure heat
recovery steam generator leads to produce 85 kg/s pure water, 30 MW

they can coupled with thermally driven vapor compression and multi-

s seawater.
In the literature review, it is found that there are many valuable and
interesting works on design, performance assessment, and optimization
of water-energy nexus models. However, the robust optimization of the
cogeneration grids under the uncertainties associated with water and
power demands, energy market prices, and renewable energy resources
have not been presented by scholars. Therefore, this paper proposes a
risk-constrained optimization framework for CWP generation systems.

• A robust optimization technique is implemented on water-energy

power only plants and gas-ﬁred thermal generating units. In the gas
turbine power generation cycle, the high-temperature waste heat is
exhausted from the ﬂue gases and used for seawater puriﬁcation.

proﬁt of the Genco, which is equal to the revenue obtained from
selling energy minus the total operating cost of CWP units and
power only plants, is maximized as the objective function. The op-
eration cost of the thermal power plants consists of their fuel cost,
start-up cost, and shut-down cost. The linearized fuel cost calcula-
tion model, ramp up and down rates of power only units, minimum
up and down times of thermal power plants, generation capacity,
and the power-water ratio of the CWP units are incorporated in the
robust optimization problem.
• The uncertainty associated with the electricity market prices is
modeled via the robust optimization algorithm. In the robust model,
the lower and upper boundaries of the energy prices are used for
making some stochastic scenarios. The forecasted electricity prices
are ﬁrstly considered to solve a deterministic or risk-neutral opti-
mization problem without considering uncertainty. Then, the sto-
chastic scenarios are selected within the price interval to solve the
risk-aversion robust problem.
• The sensitivity of the daily proﬁt to the budget of uncertainty are
discussed. The budget of uncertainty changes from 0 to 100% for a
1-day study horizon. For example, if the budget of uncertainty is
considered to be equal to 50%, the cogeneration company max-
imizes its proﬁt by considering that the actual price values in 50% of

2. Problem formulation
In a combined water and energy production grid, the electricity
price is aﬀected by both generation and consumption sides. Therefore,
the energy market price is a ﬂuctuated parameter. In the literature,
several scholars have used the scenario based stochastic programming
approaches [51,52], Monte Carlo simulation [53], fuzzy method
[54,55], artiﬁcial neural network [56] to model the uncertainty of the
energy rates. The main objective of the current research is to robustly
schedule the water-energy nexus model against the uncertainty of the
electricity market prices using a robust optimization algorithm. Ac-
cording to Fig. 1, it is supposed that a water and energy cogeneration
company, which composed of some thermal units and CWP plants, is
linked with the main electricity grid. In the CWP production facilities,
the low-grade waste heat of the ﬂue gases exhausted from the power
generation stage, is utilized for seawater desalination applications.
Hence, the freshwater is considered as a byproduct. According to ob-
jective function (1), the Genco is a proﬁt maker and desires the max-
imum revenue obtained from selling energy and the minimum oper-
ating cost of CWP and thermal units over a T-hour study horizon [57].

4. Case study and discussions
In this section, a standard combined drinking water and power
generation system is studied to prove the capability of the proposed
method in modeling the uncertainty of the electricity market prices.
This benchmark cogeneration network is linked with the local power
system. It consists of ﬁve power only units and two CWP plants. The
start-up, shut-down, ramp-up, and ramp-down characteristics of the
thermal units as well as their power generation bounds are presented in
Table 1 [58]. The constant factors, power and water generation capa-
cities, and the power-water ratio of the CWP units are reported in
Table 2 [58]. The presented robust optimization model can be im-
plemented on each plant-level CWP and thermal units, while minimum
and maximum levels of water and power generations, ramp up and
down rates of thermal units, power-water ratio of CWP producers, and
other constant factors reported in Tables 1 and 2 are available for these
units.
A mixed integer non-linear programming problem is coded under
general algebraic mathematical modeling system (GAMS) and solved by
convex over and under envelopes for nonlinear estimation (COUENNE)
[60]. The forecasted electricity market prices are presented in Fig. 3
[61]. These values are determined using the historical data and the
price forecasting methods [62]. The lower and upper boundaries of the
energy prices are estimated based on the predicted values and shown in
Fig. 3. The maximum deviation of the actual energy price from the
forecasted one at each hour t is reported in Table 3. Firstly, the budget
of uncertainty, Γ, is considered to be equal to zero for solving the base
optimization problem without modeling the market price uncertainty.
The diverse values of the control parameter Γ are then intended for
developing the risk-constrained self-scheduling problem. The numerical
results of the mentioned cases are compared to show how the system
operator makes the best decision on the performance of thermal units
and CWP plants under the uncertainty of the energy market prices.
In the robust optimization of the water-energy nexus model, it is
assumed that the budget of uncertainty changes from 0 to 1. This means
that the electricity market prices in 0–100% of the whole study horizon
may be diﬀerent from the forecasted values. For example, if the budget
of uncertainty, Γ, is equal to 0.5, the aggregator of the generating units

5. Conclusion
In this paper, the day-ahead robust self-scheduling of the conven-
tional combined potable water and power generation system was pre-
sented. It was assumed that the power only units and the CWP plants
were co-dispatched to supply the freshwater and energy demands of the
benchmark network. The main objective of the CWP units is to generate
electricity, but the waste heat of the high-temperature ﬂue gases is
recovered for seawater puriﬁcation. In other words, the freshwater was
considered as a byproduct. An aggregator of thermal units and co-
producers was responsible for maximization of daily proﬁt obtained
from selling electricity. Meanwhile, the uncertain energy prices aﬀect
the expected revenue and the total proﬁt gained over the study period.
Therefore, a robust non-linear programming methodology was pro-
posed to incorporate the variable nature of the electricity market prices
in the water-energy nexus model. Firstly, a deterministic or risk-neutral
optimization problem was solved to ﬁnd the optimal generation

Brayton power generation cycle, gas consumed by the gas load centers,
gas transmitted via the pipelines, gas extracted from suppliers/gas

desalination plants. In the natural gas-fresh water-power nexus model,
