This paper addresses the development of portable and hybrid solar-powered membrane distillation
(SPMD) system for producing freshwater in remote arid and/or coastal areas like in Saudi Arabia. It is a
stand-alone system that only uses renewable solar energy to operate. Thus, the system is highly suitable
to be implemented in remote arid and coastal areas without infrastructures or connection to the grid
(water and power), but blessed with abundant solar irradiation. Because the system is portable, it can
also be used for emergency case like a natural disaster (e.g. tsunami). The system uses Vacuum Multi-
Effect Membrane Distillation unit from Memsys to desalinate the seawater or brackish water. The
SPMD system is a hybrid system that combines thermal collectors and photovoltaic (PV) panel. The
thermal collectors convert solar irradiation into thermal energy to drive the Memsys process, whereas PV
panel converts solar irradiation into electrical to power electrical equipment. The overall operations of
the system were governed by Programmable Logic Controller. The innovation of this system is the design
of a hybrid solar desalination system that is portable and reliable. The system can also be considered as a
“green” and sustainable desalination technology. To study the performance of the system, small-scale
tests have been conducted at the Engineering College e King Saud University, Saudi Arabia. The sys-
tem ran successfully by only utilizing solar energy. The total volume distillate output during the test was
approximately 70 L with an approximate conductivity of 4.7 mS/cm. The average distillate output rate was
11.53 L/h with the maximum of 15.94 L/h at noon time, whereas the distillate ﬂux was in the range of 1.5
e2.6 L/m2 h.

1. Introduction
The increasing population and rapid industrial growth around
the world have signiﬁcantly increased the freshwater demand. Not
to mention, the water pollution that has decreased the availability
and quality of natural freshwater resources (rivers and lakes)
(Macedonio et al., 2012). These two factors have led to the fresh-
water shortage all over the world and has become one of the
biggest problems of the world (Nematollahi et al., 2013; Fath et al.,
2008). This problem is more severe for Saudi Arabia and other
Middle East and North Africa (MENA) countries, where natural
freshwater resources are very scarce (Fath et al., 2008).
One of promising alternatives to solve the world freshwater
shortage is desalination process. Desalination technology con-
tinues to evolve for meeting the social and economic needs of
freshwater (Safavi and Mohammadi, 2009). There are some con-
ventional desalination technologies that have been widely used in
large-scale desalination plants including Multi-Stage Flash (MSF),
Multi-Effect Distillation (MED), and Reverse Osmosis (RO), Vapor
Compression Distillation (VCD), etc. (Fath et al., 2008; Leblanc and
Andrews, 2007). For Saudi Arabia and other MENA countries,

plants have a high capacity to provide large cities with freshwater.
However, villages or small isolated communities in remote arid
and coastal areas with less freshwater demand (up to 10 m3/day)
and without infrastructures or limited access to the grid (water
and energy/electricity) do not proﬁt from these plants. Remote
arid and coastal areas are usually characterized by a dry climate
(low rainfall), freshwater scarcity, and abundant brackish or
seawater resources (Banat et al., 2007a). Additionally, remote arid
and coastal areas with their freshwater scarcity are usually
accompanied by high solar irradiation, like in the MENA countries.
The domestic water supply for communities in these remote areas
is usually from ground water and typically is brackish water. It is a
type of water with a level of salinity between seawater and
freshwater, which usually contains high level of dissolved salts
such as CaCO3, Mg(OH)2, NaCl, and likely microbiological con-
taminants. This type of water is unpleasant to drink and can cause
irritation of the digestive system and also skin problems (Banat
et al., 2007a; Hogan et al., 1991). These conditions encourage the
utilization of solar energy as the driving force for saline or brackish
water desalination system. Therefore, solar desalination process is
preferable and more economical to be applied in these remote
areas (Fath et al., 2008; Abdel-Rehim and Lasheen, 2007; Tzen
et al., 2004).
Additionally, although the conventional desalination processes
(e.g. MSF, MED, RO, VCD, etc.) have been proven to be technically
and economically feasible (Fath et al., 2008), the increasing interest
in desalination trends have been pushing the energy demand for-
ward. Not to mention the energy demand by other sectors such as
industries, transportation, etc. Such desalination processes require
a large amount of energy (e.g. heat demand or high-pressure de-
mand) and also high operating cost (e.g. scaling and fouling
maintenance). Saudi Arabia itself has numerous large-scale desa-
lination plants that using conventional desalination technologies
and most of them are driven by fossil fuels (directly or indirectly)
(Hagens, 2011). The problem is that the energy demand continues
to rise and multiplying while fossil resources are depleting and will
cease to dominate, which certainly lead to an energy crisis. Energy
and water resources are the two major problems in the world
(Blanco et al., 2009). Therefore, utilization of renewable energy to
drive seawater desalination system is a promising alternative
method to solve the water and energy problems simultaneously
(Chang et al., 2010). By using renewable energy sources in desali-
nation process will save conventional energy (i.e. fossil fuels) to be
used for other applications, reduce the environmental problems
(e.g. reduce CO2 emission, global warming, etc.) and obviously, free,
sustainable, low-maintenance energy source (Nematollahi et al.,
2013). Apparently, solar energy has the highest potential among
all the renewable energies (Blanco et al., 2009). Furthermore, solar
energy and water demand are highly related to each other, which is
the water demand increases in the summer season where the po-
tential of solar energy also increases. This makes the utilization of
solar energy become more attractive (Tzen et al., 2004). Therefore,
suitable technologies must be developed to permit the utilization of
solar energy to drive seawater or brackish water desalination
process.
Basically, solar energy can be utilized to provide the required
energy for desalination process either in the form of thermal en-
ergy or electricity (Banat et al., 2007a; Al-Kharabsheh and Gos-
wami, 2003). Fig. 1 shows the classiﬁcation of solar desalination
technologies. As shown in the ﬁgure, solar desalination system can
be classiﬁed into two types: direct and indirect system. The direct
system utilizes solar energy to produce freshwater directly in the
solar collector itself, such as a simple solar still. In other hand, the
indirect system integrates two subsystems: solar energy harvesting
(either thermal energy by using thermal collector or electricity by
using PV panel) and desalination process either phase-change
process (e.g. MSF, MED, MD) or membrane process (e.g. RO, MD).
Some conventional desalination plants (e.g. MSF, MED, RO, VCD)
can be coupled with the indirect solar energy system (i.e. thermal
collector and/or PV panel) to partially provide the thermal energy
and/or electricity for the plants (Banat et al., 2007a). These plants
are mostly centralized desalination plants which have a high ca-
pacity to provide large cities with freshwater. However, as
mentioned in the previous paragraph, remote arid and coastal areas
with less freshwater demand and without infrastructures or
connection to the grid (water and energy/electricity) do not proﬁt
from these plants. Therefore, these areas need a small/medium
scale and stand-alone solar-powered desalination system to pro-
vide the population with freshwater.
One of desalination technologies that is suitable to be applied in
remote arid and coastal areas is a membrane distillation (MD). The
concept of membrane distillation (MD) was ﬁrst introduced in the
literature in 1967. Membrane distillation (MD) is a hybrid process
that combines both thermal distillation and membrane process and
it is best described as a trans-membrane evaporation process
(Safavi and Mohammadi, 2009; Banat et al., 2007a; Mericq et al.,
2011). The MD process uses microporous hydrophobic membrane
for the separation. Types of polymer that are commonly used for
MD membrane are polypropylene (PP), polytetraﬂuorethylene
(PTFE) or poly(vinylidene ﬂuoride) (PVDF) (Fath et al., 2008; Safavi
and Mohammadi, 2009). Due to its hydrophobicity, the membrane

is only permeable to the water vapor, thus separating the (pure)
water from the saline water. Because only the vapor phase can pass
through the membrane, the mechanical stress on the hydrophobic
membrane decreases, thus the lifetime of the membrane increases.
Generally, the driving force of the process is water partial pressure
difference due to the temperature difference between the two sides
of the microporous hydrophobic membrane (Mericq et al., 2011).
The main advantages of MD process over other desalination pro-
cesses are its simplicity, less sensitivity to feed salinity, small ten-
dency of fouling, the capacity to utilize low-grade thermal energy,
needs only small temperature difference to run, high quality of
product (Safavi and Mohammadi, 2009; Hausmann et al., 2012;
Chang et al., 2014). Additionally, MD is most suitable for stand-
alone operation in remote arid and coastal areas due to its ability
to adapt the changes in feed ﬂow rate as well as the temperature
(Blanco Galvez et al., 2009). In MD, the vapor passes through the
membrane by several mechanisms. Based on the mechanism, the
MD can be classiﬁed in four conﬁgurations: direct contact MD
(DCMD), air-gap MD (AGMD), sweeping gas MD (SGMD), and the
one that is used in this work, vacuum MD (VMD) (Mericq et al.,
2011). Fig. 2 shows the schematic diagram of four different con-
ﬁgurations of MD system.
Recently, solar-powered membrane distillation (SPMD) system
that combines solar energy with MD process has become popular.
Banat et al. (2007a) reported the development of a (small-scale)
autonomous solar-powered membrane desalination unit (SPMD)
called “compact SMADES”. The unit was for autonomous operation
in arid remote areas. The unit has four main components: a ﬂat
solar thermal collector, PV panel, feed tank and spiral-wound air-
gap MD module. The energy for the unit was provided entirely by
ﬂat solar thermal collector and PV panel. The feed stream was
directly heated by passing through a 5.73 m2 ﬂat solar collector and
directed to the MD module as a hot stream. The MD module had an
effective membrane area of 10 m2. The unit produced distillate
approximately 120 L/day with a quality of less than 5 mS/cm. The
unit had some advantages: no need for external source of elec-
tricity, no additional water for cooling, and environmentally
friendly. However, this design also had disadvantages, e.g. the ﬂat
thermal collector must be corrosion-free, and the unit did not have
a thermal storage, thus it can only run effectively when the solar
irradiation is high. Banat et al. (2007b) continued to develop the so-
called “large SMADES” unit. The unit was an improved version of
the previous “compact SMADES” unit. The collector area was 72 m2
and the hydraulic loop of the solar collector consisted of a thermal
storage tank of 3 m2, thus it could be used for an extended opera-
tion time of the MD module. The collector was also separated from
the seawater loop of the MD module (two-loops system), thus it
was unnecessary to use anti-corrosive material. The operation of
the system was governed by a control unit. The MD module was a
spiral-wound air-gap MD made of PTFE. There were four MD
modules operated in parallel with an effective membrane area of
10 m2 in each of module. It was reported that the maximum
distillate ﬂux was approximately 1.5 L/m2 h of membrane surface
area.

Additionally, this solar-powered membrane distillation (SPMD)
system is highly expected to give some potential contributions in
the socio-economic and environmental aspects of the Kingdom of
Saudi Arabia. In socio-economic aspects: 1) The system exploits
renewable solar energy instead of fossil fuels for its operation.
Therefore, the oil reserves in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia can be
saved. The system also will help to reduce the subsidiary load of the
Kingdom from the power and water sectors, thus will save the
public funds which can be used for other developments of the
Kingdom. Additionally, such system can be considered as a sus-
tainable desalination technology for the future of the Kingdom
when no oil reserves remain. 2) The rapid increase of the world
population on 2050 will increase the demand of energy and water.
Instead of striving for limited oil resources, the Kingdom has the
opportunity to shift to an exploitation of renewable energy. 3) Due
to its portability and independency (stand-alone), the system is
suitable for operation in villages or small isolated communities in
remote arid and coastal areas with difﬁcult access to water and
power grid. The system only needs enough solar irradiation and
availability of seawater/brackish water sources for its operation.
Moreover, the system can be further developed/designed to pro-
duce extra power for daily life. Therefore, the system can support
the development of isolated communities in remote arid and
coastal areas in the kingdom. Additionally, the system can also be
used for emergency case like natural disasters (e.g. tsunami)
because the system is easy to be deployed anytime, anywhere, and
as many as possible. 4) Due to its modularity, the system is easy to
be scaled up and mass produced, thus will help the economy of the
kingdom especially in the water sector.
Moreover, in environmental aspects: 1) The system uses solar
energy, which is an environmentally friendly renewable energy
source (i.e. zero CO2 emission) (Fath et al., 2008). Hence the system
can help to reduce the global warming effect caused by CO2 emis-
sion and airborne pollutants from fossil fuels that are mostly used
as energy sources in most of desalination plants in Saudi Arabia,
which means “Zero emission”. 2) Chemical pre-treatment is usually
used to avoid fouling, scaling and corrosion of the conventional
desalination plants (e.g. MSF, RO, etc.). They may appear in the
brine and potentially harmful to the ecosystem. The use of Memsys
V-MEMD will help to reduce the environmental impacts because it
has a low tendency toward fouling and scaling (Koschikowski et al.,
2009), thus requires less or even no chemical pre-treatment of the
feed water (Koschikowski et al., 2009); and also corrosion free
because most of the parts are made of plastic, which means “Clean
Technology”.
2. Description of the system
The solar-powered membrane distillation (SPMD) system is a
stand-alone, portable, and hybrid system that works on the zero
energy concept. The zero energy concept means that the system
does not require external energy sources or electricity from the grid
system, thus it is more self-sustainable. The system consists of
three major components or subsystems: solar-thermal system,
solar photovoltaic (PV) system, and Memsys V-MEMD unit. These
components are integrated or interconnected as shown in Fig. 3.
Details of each subsystem are described in the next subsections.
Controlling and monitoring of the SPMD system are governed by
two Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs). The main PLC is

Since the system only utilizes renewable solar energy, the sys-
tem can operate in remote arid areas and coastal areas like in the
Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, where seawater or brackish water is
available, and also blessed with abundant high solar irradiation.
Therefore, the SPMD system can contribute to the socio-economic
aspects of the Kingdom. Due to its portability, the system can also
be deployed anytime, anywhere, and as many as possible, such in a
case of emergency like natural disasters (e.g. tsunami). During de-
livery of the container (SPMD system) to the destined sites, all the
equipment can be put inside the container without exception. Fig. 6
shows photographs of the installed system. A typical 20 ft container
is used to put all the equipment, except the PV panel; whereas the
thermal collectors were mounted at the top of the container, which
resemble a roof. Therefore, they can give a shade to the container.
This design is considered to be new or innovative and different with
other SPMD systems (Banat et al., 2007a, 2007b; Chang et al., 2014;
Koschikowski et al., 2009). Fig. 6a and b shows the top view of the
system and inside the container, respectively. The estimated area
required for one system is approximately 62 m2. The layout of the
system can be seen in Fig. 6c. The end of the container is used as the
seawater tank that can store up to 10 m3 of feed water (either
seawater or brackish water). The inner sides of the tank are coated
with an anti-corrosion paint to prevent corrosion of the surfaces
that are in contact with seawater/brackish water. Aside from being
used as the feed to the Memsys V-MEMD unit, the seawater/
brackish water is also used to cool the cooling water (for the
condenser) via heat exchanger HE2 (see Fig. 4).

mode. i.e., feed water is added to the seawater tank in batches. A
magnetic ﬂoat level switch with three ﬂoating sensors is installed
to monitor the seawater level in the tank. For operation, the level
must be above the lowest ﬂoating sensor. A portable ultraﬁltration

2.1. Solar-thermal system
Solar-thermal system consists of ﬁve major equipment: thermal
collectors, thermal storage tank (T), heat exchanger (HE1), thermal
collector pump (P01), and Memsys pump (P02) (see Fig. 4 and
Table 2). The type of thermal collector used in this system is an
evacuated tube collector with CPC reﬂector (Compound Parabolic
Concentrator) namely CPC 1506, which was manufactured by Linuo
Ritter International Ltd., Germany. The evacuated tube thermal
collector consists of three main components which have been fully
pre-assembled: evacuated tube, CPC mirror/reﬂector, manifold/
collecting box. Fig. 8a exhibits a 3D model of a single evacuated
tube. A single evacuated tube consists of two concentric borosili-
cate glass tubes that are half spherically closed on one side and
fused together on the other side. The advantages of using borosil-
icate glass are high light transmittance, low emission rate, and low
expansion rate (Pei et al., 2012). For vacuum insulation, the space
between the glass tubes was evacuated and subsequently plugged.
In order to effectively harvest solar thermal energy, the external
surface of inner glass tube is coated with highly selective coating
material (thermal absorber layer). The coating material used is
aluminum nitrite which has very low emission and very good ab-
sorption of thermal energy. The heat absorbed by the inner glass
tube is conducted to the U-type copper pipe through an extruded
aluminum pipe holder (see Fig. 8a) and the water inside the U-type
pipe can be heated to a higher temperature.
Another important component is CPC mirror. Fig. 8c exhibits the
cross section of the evacuated tube and CPC mirror. The CPC mirror
is a highly reﬂecting, weather-proof mirror that is used to increase
the efﬁciency of the evacuated tubes. A particular CPC mirror ge-
ometry ensures that unfavorable direct irradiation angles and
diffusion irradiation will fall to the absorber coating. This will
signiﬁcantly increase the thermal energy harvesting of the evacu-
ated tubes. Additionally, it has been reported that CPCs can reach
higher temperatures compared to ﬂat-plate collectors (Umair et al.,
2013). The last component is manifold/collecting box, which is
described in Fig. 8b. A manifold/collecting box is used to distribute
the cold “heat transfer ﬂuid” (freshwater) into a set of U-type
copper pipes inside the inner glass tubes of the evacuated tubes and
collect the hot freshwater and carry it through the mainstream. The
distribution and collecting pipes (manifold) are insulated and
located inside the collecting box. The advance or return connection
can take place alternatively on the left or on the right. The small U
pipe (with direct ﬂow-through) inside each evacuated tube is
connected to the distribution and collecting pipe in such a way so
that each evacuated tube demonstrates the same hydraulic resis-
tance. The technical characteristics of the thermal collector are
presented in Table 3.

2.3. Memsys V-MEMD unit
The portable SPMD system uses a Vacuum Multi-Effect Mem-
brane Distillation (V-MEMD) unit supplied by Memsys (Germany).
The Memsys process is a hybrid desalination technology which
combines the advantages of the efﬁcient thermal multi-effect
process with Vacuum Membrane Distillation (VMD) process into
a small modular conﬁguration. As shown in Fig. 2d, in VMD process,
negative pressure (vacuum) is applied to the permeate side (that is
below the equilibrium vapor pressure) to maintain the driving force
(Camacho et al., 2013). Due to vacuum condition, evaporation can
happen at low temperature resulting in the increase of evaporation
rate, which will facilitate an optimum ﬂux (Al-Kharabsheh and
Goswami, 2003). Additionally, the key success to the Memsys
process is the use of the superior multi-effect process, which
signiﬁcantly reduces the need for a thermal energy supply. Most of
the Memsys components are made from food-grade polypropylene.
This has advantages in terms of weight, reliability, the elimination
of corrosion and scaling, and large-scale cost-efﬁcient production.
The
Memsys
process
works
at
moderate
temperatures
(approximately 50e80 C) and negative pressure; therefore, the
entire pipeline can also be made of plastic. The microporous
hydrophobic membrane is made of polytetraﬂuorethylene (PTFE)
with a pore size of approximately 0.2 mm. This type of membrane
has been widely used in other membrane distillation systems (see
Table 1). In the Memsys, a single sheet of the membrane has a
dimension of 33.5 cm  47.5 cm. Fig. 12 shows photographs of the
hydrophobic membrane, PP foil, single stage, and a complete
Memsys module. The Memsys process occurs in the frames, which
are made of PP. There are two types of frame, membrane frame (see
Fig. 12a) and foil frame (see Fig. 12b). Membrane frame has two
sheets of hydrophobic membranes that attached on the two sides;
the area of each membrane sheet is 0.16 m2, so the area of one
membrane frame is 0.32 m2. The foil frame also has two sheets of
foil that are made of PP with the same dimension as the membrane
sheet.
The membrane frame is used to separate the water vapor from
the salt water via evaporation process, whereas the foil frame is
used to condense the water vapor to produce distillate. The
membrane frames and foil frames are friction-welded together to
form a single stage. Several stages are stacked up and vacuum
sealed using silicon gasket to form one module that contains all of
the different functionalities necessary for the membrane distilla-
tion process (see Fig.12c). As mentioned in the previous section, the
Memsys process is governed by its own PLC, which is B&R
controller. To monitor and control the desalination process, an HMI
has been installed in the Memsys unit. Fig. 12d shows the HMI
screen display of the Memsys process. The process can be run in
Auto or Manual mode.

evaporation point of water is reduced and the process can run
with temperatures between 50 and 80 C. By evaporating water
from the steam raiser, the amount of water decreases and re-

Overall, the Memsys Vacuum Multi-Effect Membrane Distilla-
tion (V-MEMD) unit itself has some advantages (Koschikowski
et al., 2009; Lange, 2011):
1) Energy efﬁcient through multiple recycling or recovery of
thermal energy, similar to traditional MED
2) Robust, because the system is automatically adjusted by the use
of PLC and also small tendency for fouling and scaling.
3) Low operating pressure allows the use of thinner piping and
reduces leakage problems and pump failure compared with the
RO process.
4) Low investment cost, because most of the parts are made from
plastic materials. Also, due to its modularity, the Memsys can be
easily scaled-up to meet the demand, further decreasing initial
investment requirements. The system can also be easily mass
produced, which will contribute to the socio-economic aspects
of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
5) Low operating cost, because low-grade thermal energy can be
efﬁciently used. Also, the electricity required for the pumping is
relatively low since the Memsys operates at low pressures, and
there is no need for expensive water pre-treatment.
6) Sustainable, because the low temperature requirement makes
renewable solar energy is suitable to be combined with the
Memsys.
7) Environmentally friendly, because hydrophobicity of the Mem-
sys membrane reduces or even eliminates the needs for chem-
ical pre-treatment of the feed water. Apparently, a simple
mechanical ﬁltration of the feed water is sufﬁcient for the
Memsys process. Also, the combination of Memsys with solar
energy will make the Memsys as a “clean” desalination tech-
nology, which will contribute to the environmental aspect of the
Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
8) High quality of the distilled water, because only the water vapor
can pass through the Memsys membranes, blocking the salt
minerals, bacteria, and organic elements including dangerous
elements such as arsenic and boron, which sometimes can pass
through the RO process.
9) Wide variety of applications because of its modular design and
the ability to handle salt solution with a high concentration and
also different solutions.
The last point is very important aspect to compensate the
advantage of reverse osmosis (RO) process, especially for small
scale unit.

4. Conclusions
A portable and hybrid solar-powered membrane distillation
(SPMD) system has been designed and developed. The current
study was conducted at College of Engineering, King Saud Univer-
sity, Riyadh e Saudi Arabia. The hybrid system works on the zero
energy concept which does not require external energy, and only
utilizes solar energy for its operation. The Memsys V-MEMD unit
has been successfully integrated with the solar-thermal collector
and the solar-PV system. In this study, a small-scale test based on
one-day operation of the system was conducted in manual mode.
The heat pump was operated to increase the distillate production
by preheating the brackish water before fed into Memsys V-MEMD
unit. The test results showed that the portable SPMD system ran
perfectly by only utilizing solar energy and without external energy
from the grid. The average distillate output rate was approximately
11.53 L/h (Test on 18 Feb 2013) with the maximum level of 15.94 L/h
(at noon time). The total volume of distillate output on that day was
about 70 L with an approximate conductivity of 4.7 mS/cm. This
distillate volume can be considered as a low output. However, due
to its modularity, the capacity of the system can be easily increased
to meet the water demand by increasing the number of the Memsys
“effect stages”. The magnitude of the distillate ﬂux was in the range
of 1.5e2.6 L/m2 h, which is comparable to other research studies.
Other tests were also conducted to study the performance of the
thermal collector. The solar energy converting efﬁciency (in per-
centage) of the thermal collector was quite low. The mean value of
the solar energy converting efﬁciency from the two tests was
approximately 33.6%.
