Optimal Coordination of Water Distribution Energy
Flexibility With Power Systems Operation
Konstantinos Oikonomou, Student Member, IEEE, and Masood Parvania
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper proposes a model for optimizing the
energy ﬂexibility of water distribution systems (WDSs) in day-
ahead power systems operation. The water distribution system
operators (W-DSOs) are considered as energy-conscious entities,
who run the proposed WDS operation model to optimize the
operation of pumps and tanks for minimizing the operation cost
of local WDSs, given the expected water demand and electric-
ity prices of the next day. A WDS energy ﬂexibility model is
proposed that is used by W-DSOs in order to calculate and offer
the feasible ﬂexible energy capacity to the power system oper-
ator. The proposed WDS operation model takes into account
the hydraulic operating constraints of water networks, thus
ensuring deliverability of the WDS energy ﬂexibility. Further,
a network-constrained unit commitment model is proposed that
integrates and optimizes the energy ﬂexibility of WDSs in the
day-ahead operation of power systems. The proposed model is
implemented on a 15-node WDS that is powered by a 6-bus
power system and the results are presented to study the impacts
of optimized WDS energy ﬂexibility on power and water systems
operation.
Index Terms—Water distribution systems, energy ﬂexibility,
mixed-integer linear programming, unit commitment, demand
response, water-energy nexus.
I. INTRODUCTION
W
ATER distribution system (WDS) facilities, including
water and wastewater treatment plants, are energy-
intensive infrastructures that account for about 3-4% of the
total electricity used in the United States [1]. Approximately
80% of this total electricity is used for pumping and distribut-
ing water and wastewater with the remaining to account for
water treatment [2]. Further, drinking water and wastewater
utilities are typically the largest energy consumers of munici-
pal governments, accounting between 30-40% of total energy
consumed [3]. In California, water facilities consume 20% of
the state’s total electricity [4], out of which, urban water usage
constitutes more than 70% of the electricity associated with
water supply and treatment [5]. As population grows, elec-
tricity demand associated with water supply and treatment is
projected to increase.

Traditionally, water and power systems have been designed
and operated as two uncoupled systems. However, in reality
these systems are mutually interdependent [6], [7]. Water is
utilized, often in large amounts, in energy sector for mining,
fuel production, hydropower, and power plant cooling. On the
other hand, energy is an indispensable component of the water
facilities as electricity is used for pumping, treatment and dis-
tribution of water. This interrelationship is often referred to as
water-energy nexus and couples these critical infrastructures
upon which human civilization depends [8].
A. Background on Water-Energy Nexus
Predominantly, the water-energy nexus has been studied
with respect to the policy and regulatory challenges and its
interactions with the food supply, climate change, economic
growth, and water rights [9]–[11]. Recent research has also
investigated the impact of climate change on the operation of
hydroplants and water reservoir management [12]. From the
modeling perspective, the water-energy nexus has been stud-
ied using multiple technical and economic approaches, mostly
looking at the co-production facilities as the coupling com-
ponents between the water and power networks [13]–[19].
In [13], the coupling between the power and water systems
is studied in terms of utilizing co-production facilities (e.g.,
hydroelectric and thermal desalination plants) assuming that
all the facilities are connected into a single node in the power
system. Additional work has also investigated the role of bat-
tery storage units in the optimal dispatch of co-production
facilities [14]. In [15], the link between water and power
systems is studied through the operation of desalination units.
Robust optimization has been applied to hydrothermal genera-
tion scheduling problem in [16], without modeling constraints
of the power transmission networks. Ignoring power transmis-
sion network constraints is widely observed in works where
the primary scope is to schedule the operation of pumps in
water system facilities [17], [18]. In a different approach, the
water-energy nexus has been modeled as a physics-based inte-
grated engineering system in [19] where a set of algebraic
equations was derived to describe the energy input-output rela-
tions between the electricity, water and wastewater systems
using bond graph models. Although there has been a recent
research focus in regards to investigating and modeling the
water-energy nexus [9]–[19], less attention has been given
to account for the energy ﬂexibility that water facilities can
introduce to the operation of power systems.

The large energy consumption of water facilities along
with their ﬂexible components such as water pumps and
tanks make them suitable candidates for energy efﬁciency
and optimization applications. A joint effort between power
system operators and the water facilities they serve would cre-
ate a great opportunity for enhancing the ﬂexibility of power
systems and increasing the energy efﬁciency of water indus-
try [20]. A model is developed in [21] for optimizing the
demand response and regulation capacity that a WDS can
offer in power systems operation. The other efforts in techni-
cal literature are mainly focused on passive energy efﬁciency
and price-based load management strategies in water distri-
bution facilities [22]–[24]. From the power system point of
view, the energy efﬁciency programs could improve the system
operations, reduce operation costs, and possibly reduce emis-
sion from the electricity sector [25], [26]. Several pilot energy
efﬁciency programs that include loads from water facilities
are conducted and their economic impacts on power systems
operation is studied [27].
The water distribution system operators (W-DSOs) have a
natural incentive to act energy-conscious, where the operation
coordination with power systems could reduce their elec-
tricity costs [8]. However, W-DSOs may be also concerned
about the negative impacts of modifying the operation of their
systems on the reliable delivery of water to the consumers.
This would require a comprehensive model to optimize the
dynamic energy ﬂexibility of WDSs considering their under-
lying hydraulic constraints. However, the majority of technical
literature models the water-energy optimization problem at
supply side neglecting the power ﬂow limits of power trans-
mission networks and the hydraulic constraints of WDSs.
Therefore, the dispatch decisions of these models may not
be supported by the constrained power and water network
characteristics.
B. Contribution and Paper Structure
This paper investigates the water-energy nexus in the con-
text of WDS operation, and elaborates WDS modeling as part
of integrating the ﬂexibility of its energy-intensive compo-
nents (pumps) and storage plants (tanks) in power systems
operation. More speciﬁcally, this paper proposes a model,
shown in Fig. 1, to integrate and optimize the energy ﬂexi-
bility of WDSs in the day-ahead operation of power systems.
In Fig. 1, a day-ahead WDS operation model is proposed
that utilizes the expected water demand and electricity prices
of the next day to optimize the operation of water pumps
and tanks for minimizing the operation cost of WDSs. In
order to ensure the deliverability of the scheduled energy
ﬂexibility, the proposed day-ahead WDS operation model
incorporates a detailed formulation of the water distribution
network and its associated hydraulic constraints. However, the
energy ﬂexibility scheduled by W-DSOs may not necessar-
ily be optimal from the power system operator’s viewpoint.
Therefore, a network-constrained unit commitment model is
proposed to co-optimize the energy ﬂexibility of WDS with
day-ahead schedule of generating units considering the power
ﬂow constraints of the transmission network. The WDS energy

energy ﬂexibility limits by which the feasible capacity range
of the WDS energy consumption ﬂexibility is determined. In
summary, the main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• This paper develops a model that integrates and optimizes
the energy ﬂexibility of W-DSOs in day-ahead power
systems operation and ensures the deliverability of the
scheduled energy ﬂexibility by considering the hydraulic
constraints of the water distribution networks.
• A WDS energy ﬂexibility model is proposed that offers
the feasible energy ﬂexibility capacity range of the WDSs
to the power system operators. The proposed energy
ﬂexibility model enables the power system operators
to optimize decisions on utilization of WDS energy
ﬂexibility in order to minimize the operation cost of
network-constrained power systems.
• In order to ensure the computational efﬁciency of the
WDS operation model for large-scale WDSs, this paper
proposes a technique to linearize the nonlinear terms
that eventually converts the nonlinear model to a mixed-
integer linear programming problem that improves the
computational efﬁciency of the proposed model.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: the proposed
WDS operation model is formulated in Section II as a nonlin-
ear optimization problem. A linearization method is developed
in Section III that casts the proposed nonlinear WDS opera-
tion model into a mixed-integer linear programming problem.
Section IV presents the proposed WDS energy ﬂexibility
model, as well as the proposed network-constrained unit com-
mitment model for integrating the WDS energy ﬂexibility in
day-ahead power systems operation. The simulation results
on a 6-bus power system and 15-node WDS are presented
in Section V, and the conclusions are drawn in Section VI.

III. LINEARIZATION OF THE NONLINEAR FUNCTION
The proposed WDS operation model (1)-(16) is a nonlinear
programming (NLP) problem, with the nonlinearities repre-
sented by the pressure head loss constraint (9), and the water
pumps’ operation constraints (13) and (14). Solution of the
NLP model, however, may be time-intensive and complex in
large WDSs, and a feasible global optimal solution may not
be always guaranteed. In order to address this issue, this sec-
tion develops a model to approximate the nonlinear functions
in (9), (13) and (14) with piecewise linear functions.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed a model for integrating the energy
ﬂexibility of water distribution systems in power systems day-
ahead operation. An energy water distribution system operator
(W-DSO) is considered who optimizes the operation of WDS
ﬂexible components for minimizing its energy costs, while
respecting the hydraulic operation constraints of the WDSs.
A computationally efﬁcient mixed-integer linear programming
(MILP) model is proposed that linearizes the inherent nonlin-
earities of the WDS operation model, and efﬁciently solves the
proposed problem. The W-DSOs, then, utilize the proposed
WDS energy ﬂexibility model in order to calculate and offer
the available energy ﬂexibility to the power system opera-
tors, who optimize ﬁnal decisions on WDS energy ﬂexibility
contribution in power systems operation.
The proposed WDS optimization model is implemented on
test 15-node WDSs that are supplied by a 6-bus power system.
The simulation results indicate that implementing the proposed
model signiﬁcantly reduces the energy cost of W-DSOs. In
addition, integrating the WDS energy ﬂexibility reduces the
operation cost of the power system, reduce the peak electricity
demand, smooth out the load proﬁle, and reduce the electric-
ity prices. These beneﬁts are maximized when the available
WDS energy ﬂexibility is optimized by the power system oper-
ator. The studies also show that the beneﬁts for power systems
are more prominent when more W-DSOs agree to offer and
coordinate their energy ﬂexibility with the power system oper-
ator. The results indicate that the proposed MILP model has
led to computationally efﬁcient solutions that are in accept-
able range of the original NLP model solutions. Future works
may include expanding the proposed model to a stochastic
optimization model, which would account for the uncertain-
ties in the wholesale energy prices, as well as the uncertainty
of water demand forecast of WDSs.
