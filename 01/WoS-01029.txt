A Stochastic Game Model for Evaluating the Impacts
of Security Attacks Against Cyber-Physical Systems
Hamed Orojloo1 • Mohammad Abdollahi Azgomi1
Received: 4 May 2016 / Accepted: 7 February 2018 / Published online: 12 February 2018
 Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2018
Abstract A quantitative security evaluation in the domain of cyber-physical sys-
tems (CPS), which operate under intentional disturbances, is an important open
problem. In this paper, we propose a stochastic game model for quantifying the
security of CPS. The proposed model divides the security modeling process of these
systems into two phases: (1) intrusion process modeling and (2) disruption process
modeling. In each phase, the game theory paradigm predicts the behaviors of the
attackers and the system. By viewing the security states of the system as the ele-
ments of a stochastic game, Nash equilibriums and best-response strategies for the
players are computed. After parameterization, the proposed model is analytically
solved to compute some quantitative security measures of CPS. Furthermore, the
impact of some attack factors and defensive countermeasures on the system
availability and mean time-to-shutdown is investigated. Finally, the proposed model
is applied to a boiling water power plant as an illustrative example.

1 Introduction
Cyber-physical systems (CPS) are deﬁned as computing and communication
elements that interact with physical processes [1]. These systems are known as the
foundation of modern critical infrastructures, such as smart grids, power, chemical
and water plants, transmission and distribution systems, and manufacturing to
improve the quality of life. In these systems, cyber elements are used to monitor and
control the physical processes.
These systems are usually comprised of sensors, actuators, controllers, and
communication networks. In these systems, each physical entity is monitored by a
set of sensors. Sensors measure some physical phenomena, such as temperature,
pressure, humidity, or the rotating speed from the physical world. The observed
measurements are transferred through communication networks to the controllers,
which control the computations [2]. Based on the received measurements, a
controller issues the control signals to the actuators. Finally, the actuators apply the
received control signal to the physical process under control.
Due to the potentially severe consequences of the security disturbances on CPS,
the security of these systems has a strategic signiﬁcance. Intentional and accidental
disturbances in these systems may lead to equipment damage (e.g., overstress of
equipment and violation of safety limits in general), production damage (e.g.,
targeting product quality and production rate), and compliance violations, including
safety hazards and environmental pollution [3].
The fact is that providing secure systems is a challenging task. This is because
adversaries only need to ﬁnd a single penetration path into the system, while the
security professionals of the system must identify and confront all vulnerabilities
and security breaches in the system. Moreover, the eradication of the deliberate and
inadvertent human inaccuracies and mistakes in providing software and hardware
systems is impossible. Furthermore, detecting and eliminating all the vulnerabilities
of systems, especially in complex systems, is not feasible. Quantitative security
evaluation can provide insight on the security level of systems. It is valuable for risk
management activities and helps to make security decisions in designing, operating,
and maintaining systems.
In comparison with security violations in the information technology (IT)
domain, the knowledge-level of attackers is the most important prerequisite for
conducting a successful cyber-physical security attack [4]. Consequently, in order to
carry out an attack aimed at disrupting a physical process, attackers need to know
the failure conditions, signal processing and the control principles of the target
system. However, without a suitable knowledge-level, adversarial actions against
CPS may only have minor nuisance effects.
The main goal of security enhancement actions in the IT domain is to protect the
stored and transmitted information and maintain the availability of services as much
as possible. Whereas the primary target of security hardening activities in CPS is to
protect the operations [4].
The primary focus of this paper is to provide a solvable security model for CPS
representing how attackers are likely to carry out attacks against these systems and

estimating the likely consequences of the conducted attacks. To do so, a stochastic
game model is presented. In the proposed model, each state represents the current
position of the interaction between the system and the attacker. In each state, a two-
player non-zero-sum game is formulated to investigate the dynamic interactions
between the attacker and the system. We also discuss how to determine the value of
the game parameters.
One of the main characteristics of the security of CPS is that the penetration of
the attacker into the system is not the same as disrupting the physical process under
control [2]. The proposed model captures this important issue and divides the
security modeling process of CPS into two phases of intrusion process modeling and
disruption process modeling. The main target is to provide a comprehensive
approach to quantitatively evaluate the security of CPS and capture the behavior of
the system in response to the attacker actions. By solving the proposed game model
in each state, we can measure the attack probabilities and the detection probability
of the attacks.
By investigating the dynamic behavior of the system under attack and using the
obtained values from solving the game, the quantitative analysis can be performed
in terms of some suitable metrics such as mean time-to-shutdown (MTTSD),
availability, and attack probability. Finally, the proposed model is applied to a
chemical plant as an illustrative example.
In this paper, we extend the model proposed in our recent work [5] in several
ways. First, the defender strategy is to decide whether to invest in security or not.
Second, the attack cost is taken into account and the attacker’s payoff in each
strategy is considered to be mainly dependent on the detection probability of attack.
Third, the attacker’s and the system’s payoffs in the disruption phase are considered
to be dependent on their payoffs in the penetration phase. Fourth, we have
performed comprehensive experiments to quantify the security of CPS. As a result,
the calculated payoffs and therefore, the game results, are more accurate.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, some related works are
mentioned. In Sect. 3, the system model is provided. In Sect. 4, the proposed model
is described. In Sect. 5, an illustrative example is given. Finally, in Sect. 6, some
concluding remarks are mentioned.
2 Related Work
In this section, some previous works on modeling and evaluating the security of
CPS are surveyed and compared with the model presented in the subsequent
sections of this paper. Game theory has been widely employed for studying the
security of computer and networked systems. For instance, Sallhammar et al. [6]
modeled the security using continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) and used
game theory to estimate the probabilities of the attacker’s behavior.
Wang et al. [7] combined stochastic games with Petri nets to propose a
probabilistic security modeling and evaluation paradigm for a security assessment
of an enterprise network called stochastic games net. Spyridopoulos et al. [8]
employed the game theory to present a defense framework against the denial of

Game theory was recently applied to analyze the security attacks against CPS.
We now focus on the presented game-theoretical approaches. Zhu et al. [10]
considered a linear dynamic system subjected to attacks conducted by correlated
jammers. They primarily focused on proposing a game-theoretical approach to
model the interdependency between adversaries and operators. To do so, they have
proposed a receding-horizon Stackelberg control law for operators. Then, they have
assessed the resulting closed-loop stability and performance of the system under
attacks.
The authors in [11] proposed a game-theoretical approach to capture the attack
and defense in CPS. In their formulations, they considered different beneﬁt
functions, such as linear, negative exponential and S-shaped. Then, they determined
the outcome of each game.
Backhaus et al. [12] proposed a game-theoretical model of human interactions in
CPS. They modeled the interactions between a cyber adversary and an operator of
the electrical grid supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) system as a
game model. The outcome of the considered interactions and social welfare of these
outcomes are estimated using the proposed model and subsequent analysis.
A dynamic game-theoretical method to model the interactions between the cyber
level policy making and physical level robust control design is presented in [13].
The model captures the cascading failures in which one harmful event propagates
failures in the system. Then, a set of coupled optimality conditions is provided to
characterize the pure strategy equilibrium of robust control design and cyber
defense policy.
A game-theoretical approach to model the behavior of attackers in control
systems is proposed in [14]. They considered jamming attackers whose target was
the communication channel between sensor nodes and assessors. They presented a
decision making process between an adversary and sensors with an energy
limitation assumption. Finally, they obtained the optimal strategies of the adversary
and the system.
Sanjab et al. [15] proposed a game-theoretic approach to analyze the interactions
between one attacker and one defender over a CPS. They assumed that the attacker
conducted cyber attacks on a number of cyber components of the CPS to maximize
the potential damage to the physical system. On the other hand, the system operator
chose to defend a number of cyber components to minimize the potential harm to
the physical side.
Shan et al. [16] proposed a multi-period multi-target game. At each period, a
limited amount of defensive resources over multiple targets was allocated by the
defender, and some attack probabilities to those targets were assigned by the
attackers. They considered four different scenarios. By solving the game in each
scenario, we can identify the optimal allocations. Shan et al. [17] also proposed a
novel hybrid model to study homeland security. In this model, a centralized

Now, we review some other approaches for modeling the security of CPS. The
authors in [18, 19] provided a survey of infrastructure security for smart grids and

radio frequency (RF) jamming, and signal blocking.
The attack graph structure and the multiple-criteria decision making (MCDM)
method are used in [22] to propose an approach for security assessment of
communication networks in power grids. The attack graph is used to present a
security analysis model and the MCDM method is used to assess the vulnerability
factors obtained by the attack graph based model.
The authors in [23] presented a language for describing the possible attacks
against CPS and their consequences. The main concentration of the proposed
language is to determine and describe the features that should be described, when
describing the attacks and countermeasures against CPS. Although, they have not
dealt with the security evaluation process, the authors believed that the proposed
attack description language can be used for security assessment purposes.
Krotoﬁl et al. [24] proposed a vulnerability analysis model based upon timing
attacks. They showed that to drive a CPS to an undesirable situation, a cyber-
physical attack aimed at jamming the communications to a device must be
conducted at proper time. Otherwise, the conducted attack may have a limited
impact on the physical process.
Cardenas et al. [25] investigated the impacts of DoS attacks on sensor
measurements. They also proposed a new anomaly detection mechanism for
cyber-physical attacks. The authors in [26] studied the impacts of network
parameters, such as communication delay, packet losses, control logic scheduling
time, and the network background trafﬁc on the consequences of attacks conducted
against CPS. Their experimental results demonstrate that the communications
parameters (e.g., communication delay) have a limited impact on the result of an
attack and the task scheduling parameter has signiﬁcant impacts on the resilience of
physical processes.
Hahn et al. [27] proposed a multi-layered security analysis framework for CPS.
They investigated how various cyber attack phases can perturb the cyber, control,
and physical layers of the system and eventually affect physical operations.
Mitchell et al. [28] proposed an approach for modeling and evaluating attacks
and defensive actions for CPS. Their model is based on stochastic Petri nets (SPNs).

considered a component attack-defense model in which the components may be
attacked and defended. They assumed that the infrastructure requires minimum
numbers of cyber and physical components to function. They demonstrated in their
studies that the Nash equilibrium, under uniform costs in both cases, is
computable in polynomial time, and it provides high-level deterministic conditions
for infrastructure survival.
Rao et al. [33] proposed a game theoretic method to formulate a resilience
problem for infrastructures composed of discrete components. They considered an
infrastructure survival probability term as the parameters of the utility functions and
a cost term in terms of the number of components attacked. As case studies, they
applied this approach to models of cloud computing infrastructures and energy grids
at different levels of abstraction when both have a large number of components.
Rao et al. [34] proposed a discrete product-form game by using a resilience

correlations are between cyber and physical components using ﬁrst-order differ-
ential condition, and between cyber and physical sub-infrastructures using the
conditional probabilities. By solving of the game, they provided the sensitivity
functions that clearly represent the dependence of the infrastructure resilience on
cost terms, correlation function and sub-infrastructure survival probabilities.
The authors in [35] proposed a game model for survivability analysis of cyber-
physical infrastructures. In their approach, the interactions between the systems are
considered between the systems, and between the components with the system.
They solved the game and derived Nash Equilibrium conditions. Finally, they
estimated the sensitivity functions to determine the infrastructure survival proba-
bility according to the failure correlation functions, partial derivatives of cost terms,
and survival probabilities of component systems and their partial derivatives.

This paper employs stochastic game theory to present a model for security
evaluation of CPS. In comparison with the mentioned research, the proposed model
and the employed game parameters are new and none of the mentioned models can
do the same evaluation. The proposed model, not only captures the behavior of the
attacker and the system in the intrusion phase, but it also considers their interactions
when the attacker conducts an attack aimed at disrupting the physical process.
Our ﬁrst objective is to quantitatively evaluate the security of CPS in terms of
some system-based and adversary-based quantitative metrics such as MTTSD,
availability, and attack probability. Our second objective is to show how different
attack factors and defensive countermeasures inﬂuence the security of CPS. The
quantitative results can provide insights on the security level of CPS and help to
make security decisions.
3 System Description
3.1 The System Model
This section presents the formal and informal deﬁnition of CPS. The behavior of a
CPS must be based on timely data about the state of control parameters and the state
of its physical environment [24]. The fact is that real-time information is temporally
accurate but only for a limited duration. Generally, the dynamic behavior of a CPS
can be described using a set of state (or process) variables. As an instance of state
variables, we can refer to the position of a valve. The data received about the state of
a physical entity for a state variable at a particular instant is referred to as an
observation [36]. For instance, the measured information about the pressure inside a
tank by a sensor is an observation.
Process variables in CPS can be categorized into two groups of measured control
variables for sensor and control signals, respectively. Every process variable has a
set-point determined by system experts [36]. The primary goal of controllers is
maintaining the state variable values closer to the determined set-points. This is
done for reducing variability (for ensuring a consistent quality of product),
increasing efﬁciency (accurate maintenance of optimal production conditions), and
ensuring safety (ensuring safe operations) [24].
The values of the measured variables (for instance, the temperature of a vessel),
must be periodically sampled by a set of sensors and sent to the controlling system.
We can refer to the constant duration between two successive sampling points as the
sampling period. In fact, the controlling system compares the measured variable to
the corresponding set-point and computes the error term. The produced error term
forms the input of a control algorithm for calculating a new value of the control
variable.

3.2 Attacks Against CPS
The ﬁrst type of attacks on CSPs is the integrity attack on sensor measurements
transferred to controllers and control signals transferred to actuators. Indeed, the
secure functioning of CPS relies considerably on the correctness of the information
transferred between system agents through communication networks [4].
The second type of attacks is the availability attacks on sensor readings and
control signals. In scan cycle architecture, programmable logic controllers (PLC)
often use the sensor readings that were last saved in the input buffers. By conducting
a jamming attack, the attacker deceives the controller about the state of the system.
The next type of attacks on CPS is reprogramming the control logic. This attack is
also considered as one of the most dangerous attacks on CPS.
4 The Proposed Model
This section describes the proposed method for modeling and evaluating the
security of CPS, step by step. The proposed method captures both the intrusion
phase and the disruption process of security attacks against CPS. Through modeling
the intrusion process, we can predict how an attacker is likely to penetrate into the
system while the intrusion has remained undetected. By modeling the disruption
phase of attacks, we can estimate the probability of causing undesired effects on the
physical process under control by an attacker with a given knowledge-level.
The proposed stochastic model will be recognized to be a semi-Markov chain.
The state-based nature of the semi-Markov model combined with the game theory

distribution functions to its transitions. In fact, unlike some stochastic models (such

In short, the primary goal of this paper is to propose a comprehensive method for
modeling the intrusion and disruption process of security attacks against CPS. In the
next step, the proposed model is parameterized with suitable data obtained by
solving the game and studying the dynamic behavior of CPS under attacks. Then,
some suitable metrics are determined for quantifying the security of these systems.
Finally, an approach for solving the proposed stochastic model and evaluating the
deﬁned security metrics is presented.
4.1 Model Description
The proposed model is a stochastic game model comprising a set of states and
transitions between them. In fact, a state represents the current situation of dynamic
interactions between the system and adversary. A transition between two states
indicates a system reaction or an attacker’s action. Stochastic games progress as a
sequence of states [38].

4.1.1 The Intrusion (Penetration) Phase
In order to model and evaluate the security of CPS, two issues should be captured.
The ﬁrst important issue is to identify the source of attacks, and the attack surface of
the system deﬁned as the set of ways in which an attacker can obtain the necessary
privilege to potentially cause physical damages. This phase of the security modeling
process has been referred to in different literatures as intrusion process modeling.
This section describes intrusion process modeling. The second issue, which is the
modeling of the physical disruption process (after the success penetrating into the
system) will be discussed in the next section.
Now, we give the detailed description for constructing the model in the intrusion
phase. In the model, the intrusion process of attackers is considered as a dynamic
interaction between the system and attackers. Indeed, due to the growing frequency
of attacks conducted against CPS, it is reasonable to consider that these systems
alternate between an initial secure state (N) and a penetrated state (P).
It is assumed that initially the system is in the normal state N in which the attack
has not been started. Once the attack is not initialized, the system will continue its
operations in the normal state. If a conducted attack is detected and removed during
the penetration and exploration phases, the system remains in the initial secure and
normal state.
In fact, if the weak points and vulnerabilities of the system are identiﬁed and
removed during the attacker’s intrusion phase, the system can remain in the initial
normal and secure state. Otherwise, the attacker will be able to successfully
penetrate into the system.
4.1.2 The Disruption Phase
In this section, we describe and model the dynamic interactions between the system
and the attacker in the disruption phase. Depending on their characteristics, CPS can
be either fail-safe or fail-operational [36]. In the fail-safe case, the system can
identify a safe state in the case of a failure and reach it. In most situations, the
system is suspended until the problem is resolved. We can refer to a railway
signaling system as an example of this type of CPS. Nevertheless, in the fail-
operational case, the system must remain operational to avoid a hazardous event.
We can refer to the ﬂight control system of airplanes as an example of the fail-
operational case.
It is assumed that in the penetrated state, the attacker successfully penetrates into
the system. In this state, he/she will try to disrupt the physical process. Based upon
his/her knowledge-level, the attacker may conduct attacks with various results. One
possibility is that due to the insufﬁcient knowledge-level, the attacker may decide to
withdraw the attack. Hence, if the attacker conducts an attack aimed at causing
physical disruption, the system will enter into the warning state (W). Otherwise, the
system returns to the initial normal state. The state transition diagram of the model
is shown in Fig. 1.
In the warning state, intrusion prevention systems (IPS) are responsible for
detecting anomalies in the behavior of the physical process. In this case, based on

4.2 Model Parameterization
In order to evaluate the proposed model, we need to parameterize it with
suitable data. For this purpose, two sets of parameters are required
1.
The transition probabilities (pab) between different states (from the state a to the
state b).
2.
Mean sojourn time (Sa) in each state (a), which is deﬁned as the time duration
from the instant of entering until the instant of leaving the state. This parameter
is only considered for the transient states.
Clearly, for the model to be accurate, the accurate estimation of the model
parameters is very important. Here, we focus on computing the mean sojourn time
in each state and the transition probabilities. We propose game-theoretical models to
capture the interactions between the system and attacker in the intrusion and
disruption phases. In the next phase, we use the game results to estimate the model
parameters.
4.2.1 The Intrusion Phase
First of all, we consider the intrusion phase of the attacks and provide a game model
to study the attacker and the system behaviors. In order to analyze the security, we

4.3 Deﬁning Quantitative Metrics
In this section, we describe some security related metrics with respect to the
proposed stochastic model. Clearly, the quantitative metrics must be deﬁned based
on the underlying assumptions and the nature of the proposed model. The
quantitative metrics are as follows:
•
MTTSD This metric can be deﬁned as the expected amount of time that attackers
need to successfully disrupt the physical process. MTTSD is a metric
representing the time units that the proposed model spends in intermediate
states (describing the interactions between the attackers and the system) before
reaching the failure state. This metric can be an important measure of the
resilience level of CPS against security attacks.
•
Availability Availability denotes the proportion of time a system is in a
functioning condition. The availability of a CPS can be estimated by the ratio of
the total time the system is capable of being used during a given interval to the
length of the interval. In fact, the availability is a metric representing the time
units that the proposed model spends in intermediate states before reaching the
failure and stopped states. In order to analyze the availability measure, all the
states of the proposed model need to be considered as transient states.
•
Attack probability This metric reveals the probability that an attacker conducts
an attack against the system. Here, we consider two attack probabilities
including the probability of penetrating into the system, and the probability of
disrupting the physical process after obtaining the required privilege level in the
intrusion phase. The value of this metric can be estimated using the proposed
game model according to Eqs. (13) and (23).

5.2 Attack Scenarios
This section focuses on presenting attack scenarios and investigating the behavior of
the plant under integrity attacks against control signals issued by the controllers.
The main goal is to estimate the sensitivity of the control signals to integrity attacks
and calculate the determined security metrics. To do so, we study the impact of the
conducted attacks against the control signals.
By considering the deﬁned speed limitations of the valves, an attacker requires to
focus on attacks at the control level to disrupt the physical process.
The ﬁrst step is computing the transition probabilities for the model. We ﬁrst
consider the intrusion phase of the attack against this plant. It is assumed that in the
penetration phase, the attacker exploits the VPN vulnerabilities to carry out an
attack against this plant. VPNs extend secure communications across insecure
public networks. VPNs provide security services through the use of virtual tunnel-
ing protocols, dedicated connections, or trafﬁc encryption.
VPNs are often considered to be secure. The fact is, protocol ﬂaws and
implementation vulnerabilities expose VPN weaknesses in many industrial deploy-
ments [45]. VPNs are the primary means of securing communications in process
control and are widely used by industrial control systems [47]. In order to protect
industrial protocols, it is recommended to wrap them within VPN tunnels.
In an industrial control system, VPNs are the means of securing sensitive data.
Therefore, these networks are main targets of adversaries for breaking into a system.
By successful breaking into a VPN tunnel and having a sufﬁcient level of
knowledge about the target system, an attacker can remotely alter sensitive control
signals or sensor readings [48].
The process variables considered in this example are the control signals issued
for feed-water, fuel and steam valves. The behavior of the plant (the impact of the
attacks on the investigated parameters) before and after conducting an attack on the
considered process variables is examined.

6 Conclusions
This paper proposes a stochastic game model for quantitative security evaluation in
the domain of cyber-physical systems (CPS). By dividing the process of security
modeling in CPS into an intrusion process modeling and disruption process
modeling process, we can predict how an attacker is likely to penetrate into the
system and disrupt the physical process. In the next step, the proposed model is
parameterized according to suitable data obtained by solving the game models and
investigating the dynamic behavior of the system under attacks to quantify the
security metrics. Indeed, the game-theoretical approaches are used to predict the
attacker and the system behavior and compute the probabilities of the expected
attacker and system behaviors. The security of CPS is evaluated by solving the
proposed game in each state and studying the dynamic behavior of the system under

a conducted attack. The system-focused security analysis is performed in terms of
quantitative metrics, such as MTTSD and availability. The adversary-focused
security analysis is also performed in terms of attack probability. We showed how
changes in the characterization of an attacker affect the security strength of the
system.
The evaluation results revealed that the attack factors and defensive counter-
measures, such as the attacker’s knowledge-level about the failure conditions,
mechanics, physics, signal processing and control principles of the system, timing
aspects of attacks, e.g., intrusion duration and time to physical disruption, intrusion
and anomaly detection probabilities, anomaly detection interval, and the attacker’s
penalty from getting detected are very inﬂuential on the security and resiliency of
the system to adversarial actions.
There are other security concerns for CPS that are not addressed in our current
research. For instance, we have not considered the effects of human–machine
interfaces (HMIs). However, we know that in CPS the operators monitor the overall
operation of the system from the HMI and most of the catastrophic events in these
systems are the result of an incorrect operator’s decision making [49].
For future work, we plan to provide separate game models for IDS/IPS and the
HMI operators and measure the interaction of them with attackers. We also plan to
take the attacks targeting information transferred into the HMI station into account.
