Summary
This study presents a novel design that combines cooling tower (CT) and tradi-
tional solar chimney power plant (SCPP) technologies for electricity generation
and seawater desalination. The proposed hybrid solar chimney power plant
(HSCPP) shares the operation of the chimney part and the bi-directional tur-
bine between the SCPP and CT, allowing alternative operation of the CT dur-
ing the nighttime and the SCPP during the daytime, and achieving continuous
system utilization. The performance of the HSCPP design was validated
against baseline models using 1 year of weather data from the city of Aqaba in
Jordan. Results revealed that the HSCPP has the potential to produce 50%
electricity (528 MWh/year) higher than the traditional SCPP (365 MWh/year).
The annual seawater desalination capacity of the HSCPP was estimated at
138300 m3, which is 1.5 folds higher than the traditional SCPP. The HSCPP
reduced the annual CO2 emissions by 40% (500 tons) compared to traditional
SCPP with annual revenue of US$190 000. Furthermore, the results show that
the HSCPP is 1.4 times more efficient than the traditional SCPP. The HSCPP
achieved a system utilization factor of 0.73% compared to 0.52% for the tradi-
tional SCPP. The HSCPP showed promising sustainable and economical tech-
nology for the production of electricity and water while reducing the emission
of GHG.

viduals.3,9-12 Therefore, the attention of the scientific
community was diverted toward sustainable, renewable,
and clean energy resources that can replace existing
internal combustion engines13-17 and mitigate the emis-

The first pilot-scale testing of SCPP dated back to Spain

different modules: the base-collector, the mechanical tur-
bine, and the tower. The received solar radiation on the
base-collector heats the air inside the system, increasing
its pressure and density, and leading to a significant
increase in the enthalpy and momentum of air. The
changes in the air's physical properties drive it upward
the chimney with high thermal and kinetic energy. The
airflow collides with the turbine blades converting

the optimized highest and internal diameter. The waste

process economy.

HSCPP description
The SCPP consists of three major components: a SC, a
collector, and a turbine. In recent research,38 a pool of
seawater was added at the base of the chimney to pro-
duce distilled water. The structural 3D, top, and cross-
sectional views of the SCPP are shown in Figures 1A to
C. Similarly, the CT is also constructed from a SC and
turbine, but without the collector, as shown in Figure 1D.
By comparing the two structures, it can be seen that the
chimney and turbine are the two common components,
which are considered the major contributors to the con-
struction costs.80 In general, the SCPP and CT are usually
constructed and operated separately. The SCPP operates
during the day as it depends on the intercepted solar radi-
ation, while the CT can be operated both day and night.
Therefore, the new proposed HSCPP combines both
structures in one and allows for its use as CT during the
night and SCPP during the daytime. The principle opera-
tion of the SCPP and CT is based on employing the differ-
ence in the density between the air inside and outside the
chimney to create mechanical work. In the case of the
SCPP, the intercepted solar radiation heats the air under-
neath the collector during the daytime leading to a signif-
icant decrease in its density and expanding it upwards
toward the bottom of the chimney. The accumulation of
hot air from the collector inside the chimney creates a
pressure gradient between the top and the bottom forcing
the hot air to move upwards the chimney and escapes to
the surrounding areas. As the hot air moves in the chim-
ney its velocity increases and exchanges energy with the
turbine and generates energy (see Figure 1C). The same
principle applies to the CT, except that the air inside the
chimney is not heated, as there is no collector. Instead, a
mist of water is sprayed at the top of the chimney that
instantly evaporates to cool the dry air, which then
becomes denser than the outside air. The density differ-
ence creates pressure gradients that increase the velocity
of cold air and drive it downwards to exchange energy
with the turbine, thereby producing energy before exiting
the system from the bottom. One key design parameter
in the proposed HSCPP is the use of a bi-directional tur-
bine that can move in both directions (clockwise or coun-
terclockwise) depends on the operation of the system as
SCPP or CT. In the combined design, the SCPP operates
during the day and becomes idle at night allowing for the
system to operate as CT. The system can be operated in
different modes, depending on the favorable weather
conditions.

Mathematical model
To assess the performance of the proposed system, a
mathematical model was developed based on the Equa-
tions 1 through 16. Figure 2 shows a diagram for the
energy and mass balance equations for all three sectors
of the HSCPP. The equations have been implemented
and resolved numerically using MATLAB R2013a. A pro-
gram was developed, verified, and tested. The program
uses the genetic algorithm function built-in MATLAB to
find the numerical solution for the temperatures in the
system. The program starts with an initial guess and iter-
ates continuously until the successive results do not
change more than 1 × 10−6. The program reads hourly
weather data of air temperature, solar radiation, wind
speed, and relative humidity from an input file and pro-
cesses each data point individually. The results are saved
in an excel sheet. The program takes an average of
3 hours to process 1000 data entry. Thus, for 1 year worth
of data (8760 data points corresponds to 8760 hours), the
program takes about 26 hours to run. The physical
dimensions and properties of the different components
for the HSCPP used in the program are presented in
Tables 2 and 3, respectively.

3.1

HSCPP performance
Figure 4 shows the production of electricity and water
along with solar irradiation values during the 24-hour
operation of the proposed HSCPP. Results revealed a
continuous production of electricity and water through-
out the 24 hours, both day and night. During the day-
time, the electricity production from the SCPP mode
peaks around noon with a rate of 148 kWh and slows
down towards the afternoon. Furthermore, the graph
shows that the SCPP mode also produces distilled water
continuously, where it peaks around noon and slows
down toward the afternoon. The graph also shows that
the production of electricity continues through the
nighttime when the HSCPP operates in CT mode.
Although it is proposed that the CT operates at night-
time only, the extension of the operating hours of the
CT to the early hours of the day should be considered
and investigated in the future. That is because the solar
radiation amount in the morning is at the lowest, so, the
electrical power production from the CT might be much
higher than that from the solar radiation at that time.
The energy and water produced in the proposed setup
were found to be comparable with the value reported by
Zuo et al.81
3.2
|
CT performance
The effect of outside temperature and relative humidity
on the performance of the CT was studied as shown in
Figure 5. Since the CT operates at nighttime, it is selected
to present the data from 6:00 PM until 5:00 AM. The out-
side temperature and relative humidity directly affect the
amount of electrical power produced. There is an indirect
relationship between the outside temperature and the
energy produced. Decreasing the outside temperature
from 40C to 35C decreased the energy produced by
7.70% (174-161 kW). Figure 5 shows a slight increase in
the output energy during the decrease of temperature
before the midnight (time 22:00 to 23:00). This trend can
be related to the change in wind direction and speed and
other climate conditions that cause an increase in the
output energy at this part of the night. On the other
hand, increasing the relative humidity decreased system
output power. As the relative humidity increased from
32% to 45%, the output power decreased from 174 to
161 kW. Similar to what was observed for outside tem-
perature, there was a sudden decrease in the relative
humidity before midnight (time 22:00-23:00) which was
combined with a slight increase in the output power. The

CONCLUSION
A novel HSCPP design that combines the traditional
SCPP and CT has been proposed. The design relies on
using the chimney, the common component between the
SCPP and CT, and the bi-directional turbine for effective
energy production. In such combined design, the HSCPP
operates as CT during the nighttime and as an SCPP dur-
ing the daytime achieving continuous operation and
high energy outcome. The HSCPP design effectively
harnessed and converted the solar irradiation into
energy that can be used for the generation of electricity
and desalination of seawater. Compared to the tradi-
tional SCPP that can operate on average 8 hours/day, the
proposed HSCPP can be operated continuously (day and
night). Results revealed that compared to SCPP, the con-
tinuous operation of the HSCPP achieved a higher utili-
zation factor, generated an additional 50% of electricity
(528 MWh/year), produced around 138 300 tons/year
of distilled water, and reduced CO2 emissions to the
atmosphere by around 502 tons/year. The produced elec-
tricity and freshwater generated annual revenue of US $
190 000. Furthermore, the results show that the HSCPP
is 1.4 times more efficient than the traditional SCPP. The
HSCPP achieved a system utilization factor of 0.73%
compared to 0.52% for the traditional SCPP. It is rec-
ommended to connect the proposed design with different
industries to utilize waste heat for electricity and fresh-
water production. This work will be extended in the
future to include Artificial Intelligence algorithm to con-
trol switching ON/OFF the CT mode and the SC mode
to maximize output power. Switching between the
modes will be done during the daytime, depending on
predicted weather conditions and if they are favorable
for the CT mode or the SC mode.
