A biogas-steam combined cycle for 
sustainable development of industrial-
scale water-power hybrid microgrids: 
design and optimal scheduling
Farkhondeh Jabari , Hamidreza Arasteh, Alireza Sheikhi-Fini, Power Systems Operation and 
Planning Research Department, Niroo Research Institute (NRI), Tehran, Iran
Hadi Ghaebi, Department of Mechanical Engineering, University of Mohaghegh Ardabili, Ardabil, Iran
Mohammad-Bagher Bannae-Sharifian, Behnam Mohammadi-Ivatloo, Faculty of Electrical and 
Computer Engineering, University of Tabriz, Tabriz, Iran
Mousa Mohammadpourfard, Faculty of Chemical and Petroleum Engineering, University of Tabriz, 
Tabriz, Iran
Received April 23 2021; Revised August 09 2021; Accepted September 16 2021;
View online October 23, 2021 at Wiley Online Library (wileyonlinelibrary.com);
DOI: 10.1002/bbb.2300; Biofuels, Bioprod. Bioref. 16:172–192 (2022)
Abstract: Lack of sufficient pure water resources and energy crises are two important issues in summer, 
especially for remote communities located in tropical zones. Electricity is used for seawater desalination, 
and power generation units need freshwater for energy extraction, conversion, transportation, and 
distribution. The design and operation of renewable energy sources based on water-energy hybrid grids 
therefore reduce on-peak electricity demand, potable water scarcity, and pollutant emissions, significantly. 
This paper proposes a combined cycle for water and power production and investigates its performance 
under different ambient air temperatures and various load levels. First, biogas is employed to fuel a gas 
turbine electricity generation process. Then, its waste heat is utilized in a steam turbine heat-and-power 
extraction cycle. This heating flow is used in a water-heater air-cooler desalination process. A non-linear 
optimization problem is developed under engineering equation solver (EES) and generalized algebraic 
modeling system (GAMS) with the aim of finding the near-optimal operating points of the proposed 
industrial-scale co-generation system, considering its technical constraints and changes of fresh water 
and power demands during a 24 h operating period. This stand-alone microgrid is able to produce 
2.8 L of drinking water per second and 5.8 MW of electricity for large industrial sectors with a minimum 
efficiency of 37%. Replacing natural gas with biofuels in the water-power nexus contributes most to 
emissions mitigation, peak electrical-demand reduction, and sustainable water treatment. © 2021 Society 
of Chemical Industry and John Wiley & Sons, Ltd
Key words: biogas-steam combined cycle; cogeneration; waste heat recovery; water-power nexus

Introduction
I
n summer, tropical regions face fresh water and energy 
crises due to the large amount of electricity consumed 
by the air conditioners and the seawater desalination 
plants and inadequate natural water resources.1 Electrical 
power is required for seawater desalination and pure water is 
employed for cooling, heating, and energy production.2 To 
solve the issues raised by the global water and energy crises, 
the renewable energy resources based co-generation plants 
are developed.3
Cogeneration is an approach for improving energy 
efficiency, reducing energy consumption, and controlling 
environmental pollution.4 Some of the topics in this area that 
have attracted interest worldwide recently are downsizing 
the cogeneration systems, improving their efficiency, and 
incorporating them to meet different objectives. Poly-
generation systems are modified forms of cogeneration that 
meet more than two objectives, including the provision of 
heat, power, cooling, and the production of energy or fuels.5 
This paper discusses recent studies and practical experiences 
related to trigeneration and poly-generation systems.
Motivation
Due to the high penetration level of renewable energy sources 
(RESs), and considering the possible fluctuations in their 
energy production, sufficient operational flexibility should 
be provided to mitigate concerns regarding the balance 
of supply and demand.6–8 Technical problems related to 
increasing the penetration level of RESs, as well as associated 
market mechanism problems, could be solved by improving 
energy system flexibility.9 It is therefore important to provide 
a flexible system to overcome the challenges of production 
fluctuations. To achieve this goal, one of the most important 
solutions is to use combined heat and power (CHP) systems.6 
The use of RESs as the prime movers for the cogeneration of 
power and heat could be considered as progress towards a 
zero-carbon society. Such an approach would considerably 
enhance thermodynamic performance by improving the 
overall efficiency of the system. Moreover, compared with a 
stand-alone system, greater profitability could be achieved by 
supplying a secondary product, providing greater revenue.10
In brief, water deficit tension and energy crisis, especially 
in tropical regions as the consequence of increased 
utilization of heating, ventilation, and air conditioning 
(HVAC) devices, are the issues considered in this paper. 
Climate change and an increase in temperature cause an 
increase in HVAC utilization periods and therefore increase 
electricity consumption. Such phenomena negatively affect 
the performance of the desalination units that consume 
electricity (for electrolysis, for instance) and reduce their 
potential to produce pure water throughout the summer. The 
design and operation of active microgrids as ‘procumers’ that 
could act as both the producers and consumers of water and 
electric energy, by using the RESs for large industries, would 
enable these systems to function independently from the local 
electric and water distribution networks, and also reduce the 
consumption at peak periods.11
Literature survey
Nowadays, satisfying the increasing demand for electricity 
is a challenge in many countries.12,13 In this regard, 
cogeneration is a compelling way to enhance the efficiency 
of energy harvesting. By increasing the penetration level 
of RESs, the simultaneous utilization of CHPs and RESs 
could be considered as a promising strategy towards energy 
transition.10,14
Studies on RES-based poly-generation methods have 
become more attractive.5,15 For example, a biomass-solar 
driven building cooling and power (BCP) plant is studied 
by Wang and Yang16 from the energy-exergy-emission 
perspective, with uncertain loads and solar irradiation. They 
demonstrated that the use of photovoltaic (PV) panels and 
heat collectors produced 17.56% exergy and 62.23% energy 
efficiency, respectively. In a study by,17 the solar Rankine cycle 
was simulated and found to generate 62.1 MW electricity 
and 20.7 MW cooling capacity using parabolic collectors, 
heliostats, heat storage unit, and the water-cooled absorption 
chiller. The maximum energy efficiency of this cogeneration 
plant increases up to 64% and 70% using parabolic troughs 
with an axis orientation and heliostats with central receivers, 
respectively. Angrisani et al.18 found that the thermo‐economic 
performance of the solar heating and cooling units is assessed 
under 10–70° tilt angel, 30–60 m2 solar collection area, various 
storage capacities, and variable prices. Fong et al.19 designed 
and simulated a solar-geothermal hybrid air conditioning 
system to save electricity by up to 44% in large-scale office 
buildings. In this research, geothermal heat and solar irradiance 
were used by radiant cooling equipment and a water heater, 
respectively. Another study20 coupled solar PV cells with an 
anaerobic digestion process to provide electricity for wastewater 
treatment. The results showed that the bio-PV hybrid system 
in Phoenix needed a solar collection area of 4032 m2, whereas 
Lansing needed a wider solar collection area (5821 m2) because 
of its cold climate.

using Stirling engines and the thermochemical conversion of 
biomass.21 In one study,22 a solar Stirling cycle was combined

and piston coupling. Another study24 discussed important

The economic performance of the PV integrated vapor 
compression refrigeration process was compared in two 
cases without and with the application of pumped hydro 
storage (PHS).25 The PHS unit pumps water to the upper 
reservoir, stores potential energy at low-price hours and uses 
it within the high-price time intervals or on-peak cooling-
load periods.26 The stability of the hydropower station could 
also be studied using the non-linear mathematical model 
of the Francis hydro-turbine governing system considering 
both the fractional-order derivative and the time delay.28 
Xu et al.29 proved that the integration of PHS units with the 
wind-solar assisted power generation systems overcomes the 
randomness, volatility, and intermittency of wind speed and 
solar irradiance and improves reliability and stability.
Many researchers have studied the optimal scheduling of 
renewable and CHP–based systems, in which different types 
of distributed generation (DGs) have been considered.10,30 
A stochastic model and the Modified Firefly Algorithm 
(MFA) have been proposed30 to solve a mixed-integer 
non-linear programming problem. By taking the systems’ 
reliability level into account, the uncertain parameters, e.g., 
wind and irradiation data, as well as electricity prices were 
modeled.30 The solution of an optimal scheduling problem 
for CHP-based microgrid with reconfigurable capability 
has been addressed. The same paper analyzed the economic 
and environmental operation of CHP-based reconfigurable 
microgrids. Mirzaei et al.31 proposed a two-stage hybrid 
stochastic–information gap-decision theory based on the 
network-constrained unit commitment framework. Their 
paper investigated the influence of feasible operating regions 
of the CHP units on the energy and flexible ramping reserve 
market and operation cost of the system. The uncertainty of 
renewable energy sources have also been considered.32 Li et al.33 
argued that an iterative approach could be considered as an 
effective way to solve a complex non-linear problem. Several 
types of DGs have been modeled, such as wind turbines, 
CHPs (in terms of prime mover technologies, i.e., gas 
turbines, micro-turbines, diesel generators, and fuel cells), 
photovoltaic units, and conventional power- and heat-only 
units.34 The same paper provided a multi-objective function 
in which operation costs, purchasing costs from the grid, 
and emission rates and penalties were considered. Two 
methods were considered in this paper to solve the proposed 
problem. First, a single-objective optimization problem was 
developed using the financial and emission aspects. Second, 
two objective functions were optimized simultaneously. It 
was found that the solutions of single and multi-objective 
problems may be conflicting. On the basis of the decision 
maker’s preferences or priorities, an approach was developed 
to choose the best solution. By comparing these techniques, it 
was shown that not only is the single-objective approach easy 
to implement but that it is able to achieve better compromise 
solutions.
Misaghian et al.35 introduced an optimization model for 
an industrial microgrid with the aim of minimizing the cost 
terms through the optimization of a day-ahead scheduling 
problem. Photovoltaic and CHP units, wind turbines, and 
thermal and electrical energy storages are modeled in this 
paper. Another paper36 shows that the implementation of 
electrical boiler and heat storage could considerably decrease 
the wind power curtailments. The electrical boiler could 
mitigate wind curtailment, and thermal energy storage 
could provide more savings by decreasing every kWh wind 
curtailment. Other research37 has found that the exergy 
efficiency of the optimal unit would be better than a stand-
alone organic Rankine cycle (ORC) electrical unit.
Different approaches have been proposed to optimize 
the performance of cogeneration systems. Generally, the 
operation of energy systems should be enhanced considering 
thermodynamic performance, economic feasibility, and 
environmental issues.10 Search-based methods38 and calculus 
techniques39 are two types of optimization schemes that have 
been addressed in the literature.40,41
Martinez et al.42 studied the effectiveness of solar units for 
cogeneration systems and the evaluation of prime movers 
in CHPs. They investigated several micro-CHP models 
for generating electricity and heat by using biomass, solar 
energy, and fuel cells. Another work43 studied the current 
gaps in this technology through the development of models 
and economic analysis. Modi et al.44 provided an overview 
of solar-based CHPs, pointing out fully renewable plants in 
Denmark. This paper carried out comparative analyses among 
the existing models in which PV modules, solar flat plates, 
ground heat pumps, biomass burner, and ORC have been 
utilized. Another study45 investigated various approaches to 
waste heat recovery in solar and geothermal units.

for wind and PV power output fluctuations, may cause 
significant damage to the ecosystem. Despite some ecological 
advantages, such as climate change mitigation, peak demand

controlling erosion in upstream watersheds, and careful

The minimum flow should also be released to ensure the 
hydrologic continuity of the river and the consequent 
conservation of natural habitat and ecological life.50 
Moreover, the sedimentation problem should be mitigated 
by constructing the small-scale weirs and mechanically 
removing the sediments (dredging).51 It is important to 
investigate the water/food/energy utilization patterns of the 
local population living in such areas, the water requirements 
of flora and fauna, the potential losses that can take place in 
forests/pastures/lands under emergency situations, and the 
geological/topographic/climactic conditions along with the 
social and cultural effects of the hydropower projects.52
Monte Carlo simulation-based reliability analysis should 
be carried out on solar combined PHS systems using the loss 
of load expectation (LOLE) and expected energy not served 
(EENS) indices.53 In one study,54 a solar-fuel cell combined 
heat and power system was designed and analyzed from the 
energy and exergy points of view. In off-peak time intervals, the 
surplus electrical power produced by the PVs was employed 
for hydrogen production.55 During on-peak demand hours, the 
stored hydrogen was used by the fuel cell for power generation. 
An absorption chiller unit and a heat recovery steam generator 
are integrated with a PV-fuel cell hybrid system and fed from 
the flue gases of SOFC for cooling and heating of a residential 
building. In all of the solar-assisted systems mentioned, 
electrical energy storage, such as batteries,56 thermal systems 
such as molten salt technology,57 and phase-change materials,58 
could be used for the trigeneration of heat, distillate water, 
and power when sunlight is insufficient and at night. The 
uncertainties associated with solar irradiations under cloudy 
and clear-sky conditions59 should also be modeled using 
information-gap decision theory to maximize economic 
benefits in robustness or risk aversion and opportunistic or 
risk-seeker decision-making strategies, respectively.60
In another study,61 the natural gas-fired Brayton cycle is 
linked with cooling and heating modes of an air-to-air heat 
pump to procure its power consumption, which releases 
more ozone depletion substances. It recommends that 
the electricity requirements of the air source heat pump 
in summer cooling and winter heating modes should be 
supplied by a biogas-fueled gas turbine power generation 
cycle to reduce CO2 footprints and fossil fuel consumption. In 
a further study,62 the energetic, economic and environmental 
(3E) aspects of the biogas upgrading, biogas driven combined 
heat and power plant, and the biogas integrated SOFC are 
compared using the energy efficiency, payback period and 
green production degree indices. Studies proved that the 
biogas upgrading unit has the highest energy efficiency 
(46.5%) and the shortest payback period (8.9 years) with 
the lowest green production degree (9.29 gd.day−1). Despite 
the highest green production degree (21.77 gd.day−1) in 
the case of a biogas integrated SOFC system, the payback 
period and energy efficiency are longer (14.5 years) and lower 
(13.6%), respectively, than those reported for the biogas 
upgrading system. In another study,63 a bi-level programming 
approach is introduced to minimize the energy-generation 
cost and the water treatment cost. The mass balance and 
the availability of fossil fuels, electricity balance criterion, 
power and water production capacities, energy demand for 
water treatment, the water requirement of the electricity 
generation process, availability of groundwater-surface-gray 
water, and the permitted range of CO2 emission production 
have been considered as optimization constraints. No 
wastewater management is considered in this research, 
while the treatment, disposal, or recycling of the wastewater 
may affect the energy-water nexus optimization decisions 
significantly.64 Finally, one study65 has classified several 
existing cogeneration systems in terms of the combination of 
prime movers (hybrid cogeneration), and has also explained 
their main interests and findings.

aware, there have been no studies of the design and optimal 
scheduling of the biofuel-steam combined cycle coupled 
humidification-dehumidification (HDH) desalination 
microgrid. Moreover, in this paper, the optimum operation 
point of the cogeneration system has been found based 
on both the electricity and the freshwater demands of an 
industrial-scale end user – an aspect that is not usually 
considered in the design and evaluation of a multi-generation 
facility. Hence, not only is the thermodynamic design

Contributions
As mentioned, the design and performance assessment of 
the different multi-generation systems have recently been 
discussed. However, the tropical regions of southern Iran 
face annual on-peak electricity and freshwater demands and 
severe air pollution in summer. A biofuel-steam combined 
cycle power plant that produces both fresh water and power 
has not been designed and investigated for large industrial 
end-users. Moreover, the optimal operating points of the 
reviewed systems have not been found considering the 
pure water and electrical demands of the water-power hub 
network. Hence, this paper presents the thermodynamic 
design and evaluation of the biogas-steam combined 
cycle for supplying a benchmark industrial consumer. In 
addition, the sensitivity of the proposed system to potable 
water and power demands and environmental conditions 
such as the air temperature are analyzed over the 24 h time 
horizon. Thermo-economic optimization of the bio-fueled 
cogeneration plant is carried out under engineering equation 
solver (EES) and the generalized algebraic mathematical 
modeling system (GAMS). The daily electrical load-
generation mismatch is minimized as the main objective 
function. Moreover, the thermal restrictions of the bio-
fueled desalination and electricity procurement method are 
modeled as optimization constraints. The energy efficiency, 
optimal values of the decision variables, and the objective 
function (load-generation imbalance at each hour and 
over the sample day) have been obtained and are discussed 
comprehensively to reveal the best performance of the system 
under peak demands and severe climatic conditions.
The original aspects of the current paper are as follows:
•• Design, verification and optimization of a bio-fueled 
combined cycle power plant are presented under variable 
ambient air temperature and different electrical and 
potable water demands of industrial end users. In most 
reviewed works, the operating points of the systems that 
are presented are usually found without considering the 
electricity and freshwater demands of the customer side. 
In this paper, the impact of the demand fluctuations 
and the environmental parameter variations in the 
proposed microgrid are studied. Moreover, a non-linear 
programming approach is developed to minimize 
the total electrical load generation imbalance to find 
the near-optimal operating point of the combined 
desalinated water and power (CWP) production 
facility during a 24 h horizon. In addition, the technical 
concepts of the CWP plant are modeled as equality and 
inequality constraints.
•• There are two main reasons for insufficient drinking water 
resources: Physical issues and techno-economic issues. 
The physical water scarcity is a result of inadequate natural 
water resources to supply a region’s consumption, while 
the techno-economic issues refer to the poor management 
of sufficient available water resources. In summer 2021, 
water scarcity in southern Iran has attracted significant 
attention because of frequent power outages and techno-
economic issues. Hence, the operation of a renewable-
energy-source-based seawater desalination system is 
essential in such climates. A desalination unit is activated 
by the condenser heat exchanger. In other words, the high-
temperature waste heat is received from the working fluid 
of the steam turbine power generation cycle and used for 
seawater purification. This subsystem is used to mitigate 
freshwater shortages in very hot and humid coastal areas.
•• The fresh water and power utilized by an industrial 
sector or a number of residential customers are procured 
by the HDH process and the biogas-fueled combined 
cycle power plant, respectively. The water and energy 
produced by the proposed system can be used by large 
consumers and can reduce the peak demands of the 
regional water and power companies. In other words, 
the stand-alone water-energy hub network introduced 
reduces the number of people affected by the water-
energy crises in summer and improves social welfare.
•• Zero-emission production: The use of biogas and 
recovered waste heat for cogeneration of power and 
freshwater reduces daily greenhouse gas emissions 
significantly. A secure and grid-independent combined 
power and water generation system not only increases the 
customers’ satisfaction from water and energy services 
but also mitigates the pollutant greenhouse gases.
Materials and methods
The proposed combined fresh water and power production 
grid is shown in Fig. 1. The ambient air is first compressed 
and then combusted with biogas (60% CH4 + 40% CO2) 
to produce power after passing a gas turbine. An electrical 
generator is mechanically coupled to the gas turbine to 
convert its net power into electricity and to supply the total 
energy consumption of the industrial customer. A detailed 
problem formulation can be presented for each stage.

(47) and imported in the non-linear optimization problem.

the mass flow rate of the ambient air causes an increase in 
electricity production of the combined cycle microgrid, 
lower heat losses of the combustion chamber, and higher 
thermal efficiency. More heat is also transferred from 
the saturated vapor to the seawater and more saline 
water will be desalinated at each hour. In other words, 
more potable water and electricity will be produced if 
the air mass flow rate increases and vice versa. When the 
electrical demand of the industrial unit increases from 5.16 
(minimum) to 5.88 MW (maximum), the mass flow rates 
of air and combustion products will increase. Hence, the 
output electrical power of the gas turbine-generator set 
increases from 3.52 (off-peak value) to 3.91 MW (at t =19
). Meanwhile, the net electricity production of the steam 
turbine cycle changes from 1.64 to 1.97 MW as a result of 
increasing the steam mass flow rate. Increasing the air mass 
flow rate reduces the heat losses of the combustion chamber 
while increasing the gas turbine power. The thermal 
efficiency of the combined cycle therefore improves from 
37% to 44%. Increasing the steam mass flow rate also causes 
the value of heat extracted from the saturated vapor to the 
saline water to increase, which results in higher freshwater 
production at the peak load hour (2.81 kg/s potable water at 
t =19 ). If the air mass flow rate increases, the mass flow rate 
of the biofuel will increase, resulting in lower amounts of 
LHV and heat losses and greater values of power production 
and energy efficiency. When the ambient air temperature 
increases, more seawater enters the dehumidifier, condenser 
heat exchanger, and humidifier to humidify the dry air. 
Meanwhile, the mass flow rate of the dry air entering the 
humidifier is increased to generate higher hot humid air, 
dehumidify it, and extract more drinking water. As a result, 
this cogeneration facility is able to satisfy both the electricity 
and pure water demands of an industrial consumer in severe 
hot summer days. In summary, a zero-emission zero-energy 
water-power hybrid grid was designed and validated for 
industrial applications.
In future research, time of use and real-time demand-
side management programs could be applied to this 
non-linear optimization problem. These demand response 
approaches make it possible to find how a part of the 
peak loads shift to other off/mid-peak hours to maximize 
the total revenue achieved by selling the surplus power/
freshwater to other adjacent microgrids. In this study, 
the exchange of water and energy products with other 
hub networks has not been considered. The risk-aversion 
and risk-taker optimization strategies can be addressed 
by information gap decision theory to maximize the 
gained output ratio of the HDH desalination cycle under 
uncertain potable water demand. The robust optimization-
based uncertainty modeling approach can be considered to 
find the risk-aversion operating points of the cogeneration 
microgrid against the temperature and load changes over a 
1 year period. The sizing and siting of bio-fueled combined 
cycles in active distribution networks should be studied 
with the aim of minimizing the power losses of the power 
distribution feeders and improving the voltage profile. The 
integration of the biogas-steam combined cycle power
