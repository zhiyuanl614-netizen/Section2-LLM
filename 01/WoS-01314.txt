A B S T R A C T
As a new application form of sustainable energy generation, building-integrated photovoltaics provide great
flexibility for energy conservation and emission reduction. However, due to the randomness and volatility from
photovoltaic generation, the full exploitation of the advantages of building-integrated photovoltaics has become
an important question for the utilities. To address this issue, this paper proposes a novel and comprehensive
(combined) water-energy supply system applied to the smart community with building-integrated photovoltaics
and two-stage electric-hydraulic dispatching method. It was designed to minimize the electricity consumption
cost and power fluctuation while meeting the water demand loads, followed by the iterative algorithm as well as
linearized techniques to solve the optimisation model. The Shapley value method was introduced to ensure the
fair revenue between the property company and owners. Simulation studies on a building-integrated-photo-
voltaics community and a 15-node water distribution system indicates that the annual operation cost of the
water-electricity combined supply system was reduced by 29.6% while the external grid obtained a better
prediction regarding the real-time exchange power. The results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed model
on peak shaving and valley filling and fluctuation smoothing.
1. Introduction
The current energy crisis and environmental requirements have
stimulated the development of building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV)
[1]. However, BIPV applications must deal with the problem of the
owners bearing an economic loss due to either PV power curtailment or
selling to the external grid (EG) at a low price during the time periods
when the photovoltaic (PV) peak output exceeds the load demand [2].
Meanwhile, the unpredictability and volatility characteristics of PV give
rise to great challenges in achieving power balance and reliable mi-
crogrid (MG) operation [3]. Although the above problems can be solved
well by electric energy storage systems, their application has been
limited by their high operational and maintenance costs and other
drawbacks [4]. Thermal energy storage systems such as residential
buildings with air conditioning [5], wall bricks with a heater [6] or hot
water storage with a heat pump [7] are regarded as economic solutions,
but the weak heating/cooling demand in regions with perennial com-
fortable temperature leads to a limited annual comprehensive utiliza-
tion rate. Therefore, to fully exploit to the advantages of BIPV, the
development of an economical and universal energy storage system is
urgently needed.
On the other hand, as it is well known that the water treatment acts
as a common and considerable consumer, thus many studies have fo-
cused on its electricity consumption and sought breakthroughs in the
energy conservation. For instance, it occupied approximately 3% of the
total electricity in the United States [8] and 20% of that in California

According to the estimation of the World Bank, the above proportion
will increase significantly due to the growing water demand at an ex-
pected rate ranging from 43% in North America to 283% in Sub-Sa-
haran Africa from 2005 to 2030 [11]. However, unlike other rigid large
energy consumers, water distribution systems (WDS) incorporated
water pumps (WPs) with the flexible adjustment capability for im-
proving the efficiency and providing various services. For example, the
WP was optimized by shifting electricity consumption to low-price
periods to decrease its operational costs in [12]. Considering the rain-
fall and water demand uncertainties, [13] proposed a robust two-stage
strategy to optimize the WP power. On this basis, [14] introduced an
online robust control method for practical application and [15] carried
out the multiple experiments and analysed a corresponding systematic
error for improving reliability of the test results. In addition to the
above works, several mathematical and heuristic optimization algo-
rithms were introduced accordingly. Ref. [16] presented an algorithm
for WP modelling and scheduling. It uses the WP start/end run times as
continuous variables and the WP status at the beginning of the sche-
duling period as binary variables. On this basis, [17] introduced a two-
stage solution method for the optimal scheduling problem of WDS
where the relaxed continuous variables are optimized in the first stage
and the binary variables are processed in the second stage by using a
local branch and bound method. In [18], the operational optimization
of pumping station with multiple WPs was treated as a dynamic pro-
gramming problem and was solved using the reduced dynamic pro-
gramming algorithm. Ref. [19] adopted a neutral evolutionary search
method to solve the least cost WP operation optimization problem for
an industrial size WDS. Six multi-objective evolutionary algorithms
including strength Pareto evolutionary algorithm, multiple objective
genetic algorithm and so on were introduced to solve the optimal WP-
scheduling problem and compare the solution performance [20]. The
operation optimization problems and solutions of WDS were compre-
hensively reviewed in [21].
The above-mentioned literature mainly focuses on the primary WDS
operated by the water supply corporation in which the flexible opera-
tion of WPs can benefit the operators. However, for the secondary WDS
(such as in a community) owned by a property company, in addition to
WPs, water tanks are generally used to ensure safe and adequate water
supply to the owners and these WDS have rarely attracted the attention
of academic researchers. In practice, by optimizing WPs and tanks, the
secondary WDS purchases electricity at a low price from the EG to re-
duce its operational cost as much as possible regardless of the MG,
which means that the problems caused by the BIPV still exist. However,
the perfect regulation effects of secondary WDS indicate that the
community with BIPV can consume the excessive energy generated
during PV peak output by storing the water in a tank and mitigating the
power fluctuation by regulating the WPs; this approach has not yet been
applied or mentioned in the literature. To elucidate the problems and
verify the performance characteristics of these approaches, a novel
water and electricity combined supply system (WECSS) involving dif-
ferent stakeholders, together with a detailed hydrodynamic model in-
corporating water tanks is proposed for the first time in this paper.
The existing work focusing on the linkages between the water and
electricity industries to find opportunities to reduce energy consump-
tion and operating costs provide an excellent reference for the modeling
of WECSS [22]. For example, a multi-plant real-time simultaneous
economic dispatch of power and water was developed to address the
coupling relationship between WDS and the power grid in [23]. A
quantitative engineering system of the energy–water nexus was mod-
elled using bond graph representations of the system’s modelling lan-
guage in [24]. Ref. [25] developed a day-ahead power systems opera-
tion model that integrated the energy regulation flexibility of the WDS.
Ref. [26] introduced an energy efficiency evaluation strategy for WDS
design and a mathematical model for optimizing WP operation in high-
rise buildings. Ref. [27] presented the energy-water optimal scheduling
model using harvested rooftop rain water and the time-of-use electricity
price tariff. A framework consisting of two optimization models was
proposed in [28] where the first-step model optimizes the procurement
cost of the WDS operators and the second-step model optimizes the
demand response and frequency regulation. Furthermore, with different
peak price of the electricity tariff, the model that optimizes variable
speed WPs in WDS to provide the demand response for a power system
was established in [29]. Models that focus on passive energy efficiency
and price-dependent load management methods in WPs and water
treatment plants were presented in [30]. In [31], the energy use and the
state of demand response readiness in wastewater treatment facilities
were characterized. Although the long-time scale performance of WDS
has been well examined in the above literatures, the flexibility provided
by water tanks and WPs on a short-time scale such as by eliminating
power fluctuations has not been modelled and studied. Additionally,
concentrating on the WDS and large power grid, these models neglect
the electric limits of the power flow and WP ramp rate and the hy-
drodynamic constraints of WDS for which the dispatching strategies
may not be supported in WECSS involving WDS, MG and EG particu-
larly under risk conditions. Moreover, as the basic premise for pro-
moting cooperation, the distribution of the cooperation revenues
among the participants with different interests and price barriers has
not been discussed to date.
In addition to supplementing the shortcomings of existing work in
modelling, this paper also proposes a two-stage hydraulic and electric
combined dispatching method to ensure the performance of the WECSS
on multiple time-scales. In fact, the dispatching problem has been a hot
research topic [32]. For example, economic power generation strategies
are formulated in the day-ahead stage while prediction errors are mi-
tigated in the real-time stage in [33]. On this basis, [34] integrated
model predictive control (MPC) into the two-stage dispatching model,
significantly enhancing the ability of the dispatching system to cope
with risks. Regardless of the violent fluctuation of real-time power, [35]
directly adopted a one-stage MPC strategy to minimize the operational
cost. Likewise, [36] used a hierarchical iterative control algorithm for
optimal balance of the grid load (both the voltage drop and current
load) while satisfying users power demand. By contrast, a power
smoothing objective adopted in [37] resulted in the poor operation
economy of an energy storage system. However, unlike the existing
dispatching system, for WECSS, both electric balance and hydraulic
balance in multiple stages should be met to simultaneously ensure safe
water and electricity supply to residents. More importantly, the pre-
diction errors such as the PV output error will have a profound impact
on the electric and hydraulic balance on multiple time-scales, further
increasing the dispatching difficulty of WECSS. Consequently, a novel
dispatching method must be designed to coordinate the hydraulic and
electric systems and eliminate the impact on multiple time-scales
caused by prediction errors. Meanwhile, the method for solving the
novel dispatching model of WECSS efficiently to meet the actual de-
mand of the novel dispatching system must be carefully considered.
Summarising, to solve the storage problem caused by BIPV, a novel
combined water-energy supply system is proposed. A detailed com-
prehensive model with technical considerations, constraints and its
profit distribution is presented for the first time, to analyse its perfor-
mance on multiple time-scales. (This is the special consideration and
the main contribution in this paper.) In addition, the Shapley value
method is introduced firstly to distribute the cooperation revenues
between both sub-systems. Lastly, to reduce the impact of forecast er-
rors, a two-stage dispatching method is proposed and an iterative
procedure as well as linearized techniques is adopted to solve the
problem efficiently.

dispatching method, case study and parameters and the multiple in-

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Structure of the water-energy supply system
With BIPV and WDS in a smart community, the designed WECSS is
shown in Fig. 1. As observed from the figure, the electric-hydraulic
systems are coupled with WPs. The MG connected to the EG can not
only supply power to various electric loads, but also supports the power
demand of WPs directly. The water demand loads are met by WDS
service. There exist two operation modes of WECSS in the process of
exchanging power with EG:
Mode 1: Water storage mode. Electric energy is converted into po-
tential energy of water by WPs and is stored in the tanks;
Mode 2: Water release mode. Water with high potential energy in
the tanks is released to reduce the power demand of WPs.

2.3. Two-stage electric and hydraulic combined dispatching method
As observed from Fig. 4, a two-stage dispatching model including
the day-ahead stage and the real-time stage was proposed to optimize
WECSS operation in the smart community. In the day-ahead dis-
patching stage, the sub-problem of hydraulic dispatching to meet the
predicted water load demand is addressed first and is followed by the
optimization of WPs in the electric problem in order to minimize the
cost. Originated from day-ahead dispatching, the power exchange be-
tween the community and the EG is applied to guide real-time dis-
patching. To eliminate the risk of SOC violation of WECSS due to
forecast errors, a risk management modular is introduced to regulate
the exchange power. In the real-time stage, based on the difference
between the actual data and the predicted data, real-time MPC dis-
patching is devoted to mitigating the negative effects on both the
electric sub-problem and the hydraulic problem. The corresponding
detailed explanations are as follows:

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Numerical simulations results
To facilitate the understanding of the performance of WECSS, the
basic properties of WECSS were analyzed. On the one hand, the cor-
relation between the flow rate, elevation difference and WP power is
shown in Fig. 10 (a). As observed from the figure, with the pipe re-
sistance parameter G = 1297, both the WP power and elevation dif-
ference between the nodes could drive the water flow increase while
the rates of flow increase would decrease. For instance, the flow rate
rose from 427 m3/h to 2998 m3/h as the WP power increased from
0 kW to 100 kW under the conditions of 25 m elevation difference, but
it only increased to 3667 m3/h when the WP power reached 200 kW. As
a consequence, WP determines the efficiency of the water supply. On
the other hand, it is assumed that the WP is located in the front pipe, the
tank is in the intermediate node, and the water load is in the later node,
and the performance of the tank is shown in Fig. 10 (b). With 0 ele-
vation difference and 1000 m3 water stored in the tank initially, it is
observed that the input flow was controlled by the front WP, the output
flow was determined by water demand and the flow difference was
regulated by the tank. For instance, keeping 500 m3/h water demand
fixed, the stored water increased to 2937 m3/h with a WP power of
100 kW while it was 3628 m3/h with a WP power of 200 kW. Conse-
quently, the tank is essential for keeping the water supply and demand
balance at all times.
Comparing Fig. 10 (a) and 10 (b), it can be concluded that the WP
and tank are active components in WECSS. Fully considering their
properties, the optimization results based on the proposed two-stage
dispatching method are reported as follows:
First of all, the day-ahead electric consumption of WECSS and
WEISM is shown in Fig. 11. As observed from Fig. 11(a), for WEISM on
a sunny day, WDS consumed electricity regardless of the energy output

achieved.

lower and smoother power exchange and is more friendly to EG.

of WECSS during the peak hours was shifted to the valley hours in the
cloudy day with insufficient PV output. The operational costs of WECSS
and WEISM were 2115 Yuan and 3237 Yuan, respectively (1398 Yuan
for WISM, 1839 Yuan for EISM), so that WECSS could save 1122 Yuan
for the community. Additionally, the maximum exchange power of the
two cases was 496 kW and 416 kW, respectively, implying that the MG
with WECSS is even more friendly to EG.
Secondly, the robustness of WECSS under risks is shown in Fig. 13.
As observed from Fig. 13(a), compared with the predicted value, the
actual PV output would increase or decrease during the dispatching
period on sunny days. Faced with the power deviation, the WP regu-
lated their power consumption correspondingly, resulting in the
changes in the storage energy in WECSS as shown in Fig. 13(b). It is
observed from the figure that the SOC of WECSS under a 15% stochastic
prediction error remained within the [0 1] constraint for various risks.
Consequently, with balancing the water supply and demand, WECSS
has a perfect property to resist the influence of prediction errors. Note
that for WEISM, due to the price barriers between WISM and EISM, the
deviation of PV output caused by errors was transited to the EG while
the water supply load maintained the same as predicted power.

3.3. Comparison
To reflect the superiority of the two-stage dispatching model, a one-
stage MPC dispatching model used in [35] was introduced to optimize
the operational cost of WECSS. The dispatching results are shown as
Fig. 16. Comparing Fig. 16 (a) with Fig. 11 (a), it can be seen that the

with 1574 Yuan. Further, comparing Fig. 16 (b) with Fig. 14 (a), the

In addition, the original model with nonlinear terms optimized di-

operational costs and computation times for sunny and cloudy scenarios

1.2% respectively. However, the computation times of the original
model was more than 19 times longer. The computational results show
that the proposed solution algorithm provides fairly accurate results in
a relatively short time.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, a novel water-electricity combined supply system with
economy and universality involving different stakeholders was pro-
posed to solve the problems arising from the integration of building-
integrated photovoltaics. To evaluate the performance of the water-
electricity combined supply system on multiple time-scales, a detailed
electric-hydraulic combined analytic model incorporating the power
flow limits and WP ramp rate and a two-stage dispatching method were
proposed, followed by the iterative solution algorithm to efficiently
solve the optimization model. The simulation study indicated that the
water-electricity combined supply system could ensure the balance
between supply and demand of both water and electricity, and based on
the Shapley value method, the fair distribution of cooperative profits
could be realized.
Based on the proposed two-stage dispatching method, the water-
electricity combined supply system could achieve peak shaving and
valley filling and smooth power fluctuation by storing the water in
tanks and regulating the operating power of water pumps. Therefore,
the operational cost of the community could be reduced by 29.6%
(259,000 yuan) while the operational pressure of the external grid
could be decreased. In addition, the proposed iterative algorithm could
greatly improve the computational efficiency of the operation model for
a water-electricity combined supply system by more than 19 times.
Although the excellent performance of the water-electricity com-
bined supply system on multiple time-scales was verified by the simu-
lation, the work was only about theoretical discussion and ignored
some technical details. In real application, the water tanks should be
equipped with the self-regulating valves to flexibly control the water
inflow and outflow while the water pumps should have the function of
automatic power regulation and both of them should be able to respond
to remote control instructions. Future work also will include the ex-
perimental verification of the model results.
