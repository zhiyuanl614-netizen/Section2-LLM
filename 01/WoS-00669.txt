In this study, an innovative design of hydro-electricity system was applied to an unconventional site in an
attempt to generate electricity from the exhaust cooling water of a coal-ﬁred power plant. Inspired by the
idea of micro hydro, present study can be considered new in three aspects: design, resource and site. This
system was hung at a cooling water discharge weir, where all sorts of civil work were prohibited and sea
water was used as the cooling water. It was designed and fabricated in the university's mechanical
workshop and transported to the site for installation. The system was then put into proof run for a three-
month period and achieved some success. Due to safety reasons, on-site testing was prohibited by the
power plant authority. Hence, most data was acquired from the proof run. The driving system efﬁciency
was tested in the range of 25% and 45% experimentally while modeling results came close to experi-
mental results. Payback period for the system is estimated to be about 4.23 years. Result obtained val-
idates the feasibility of the overall design under the sensitive site application.

1. Introduction
It is true that there is a lack of grid-based electricity supply to
remote areas in developing countries and as a consequence,
renewable energy technologies such as hydro, solar and wind are to
be developed and implemented [1e5]. Broader application of
renewable energy technologies contributes substantially towards
sustainable economic growth and prominent environmental con-
servation, though it is common that the intermittent nature of
renewable energies has made them comparatively less efﬁcient and
economic when operate independently in providing reliable and
consistent electricity to consumers [6]. More often than not, a
combination of both renewable energy and conventional fuel are
found to be the most feasible and practical solution to off-grid
electricity, with the conventional fuel complementing the renew-
able energy [1e3,7]. Most often, there will be many issues associ-
ated with different energy schemes and requirements [2,3].
Evidently, there are rooms for improvements and innovations for
renewable energy technologies.
Among various types of renewable energy technologies, micro
hydro is deemed as one of the most reliable [4e6,8e10]. Typical
micro hydro directly utilizes moving water from stream or river to
generate electricity, and in many cases these natural water re-
sources are rather consistent and predictable [6,11]. Considering its
consistency and availability which are relatively better than other
renewable energies, micro hydro has been widely accepted as the
most established and reliable. Its other advantages include zero
emission, low maintenance, high durability as well as can be efﬁ-
ciently run by just a few of average local folks. In fact, it has been
proven as an effective yet economic solution for off-grid electriﬁ-
cation in rural areas [8,9,11,12].
So far it is known that perhaps only drought and winter will
affect the water supply, and these factors are relatively gradual and
discernible. In the case of these unsteady, torrential rivers having
different runoff regimes there is a need to optimize the plant and
ﬁnd the suitable system parameters thus optimized efﬁciency with
shorter investment payback time [13]. Using a mixed-integer
nonlinear programming approach, Catalao et al. [14] introduced

Inspired with its efﬁcacies, present study aimed to adapt this
concept but on a very different site e a cooling water discharge weir
in SPC (Sejingkat Power Corporation Sdn. Bhd.), which is a local
seaside power plant. SPC is a base load power plant located near
estuary connecting to South China Sea. It pumps water from the
estuary to cool its coal-ﬁred power generators due to two distinc-
tive reasons: (1) the water is abundantly available for the plant-
wide heat rejection applications, and (2) it is very consistent all
year round e an average drought will have no adverse effect on the
water supply. The exhaust cooling water is then discharged back to
the estuary. However, at the discharge weirs, the cooling water is
observed to have an abrupt drop, forming an artiﬁcial waterfall. The
waterfall has the potential for energy recovery. Thus, present study
attempts to recover the energy by design, develop and install a
cooling water power generating system, which is in some ways
similar yet very different from other existing micro hydro. The
surrounding variables and the marine environment are among the
challenges that have made this project a unique case.
It is worthy of note that utilizing artiﬁcial sites to generate
electricity has become one of the newer trends in the renewable
energy technologies. One of the recent efforts has been done by
Stevanovic et al., who carried out a feasibility study on the potential
of hydro-electricity at a thermal power plant which utilizes river
water as the cooling water [17]. Due to seasonal variations, the head
changes with time depending on the river water level, while the
ﬂow rate remains constant most of the time. The study and pro-
posed design of a conventional micro hydro done by Stevanovic
et al. seemed promising in harnessing the hydro potential of the site.
Present study, however, dealt with sea water of consistent head and
ﬂow rate at an artiﬁcial pond site which prohibits any civil work.
As to modeling works, Barelli et al. [13] presented the mean and
median AFDC (annual ﬂow duration curves) model which utilizes
recorded data, allows the optimization of the hydro plant under
unsteady, torrential rivers having different runoff regimes, with
optimized efﬁciency and shorter investment payback time. A
payback period range of 0.9e9.7 years has been estimated via the
model for the ﬁve rivers investigated.
Using a mixed-integer nonlinear programming approach, Cat-
alao et al. [14] considered optimal hydro scheduling and offering
strategies considering price uncertainty and risk management.
Market uncertainty was introduced in the model via price sce-
narios; while risk management was included using CVaR (Condi-
tional Value-at-Risk) to limit proﬁt volatility.
By combining a production cost simulation model (PLEXOS) to
represent the operations of the electric system with a hydropower
modeling tool (RiverWare), Ibanez et al. [18] presented a method-
ology which allows for the combined optimization of both systems.
Unlike the complex variable ﬂow schemes, the present study
only requires the development of an uncomplicated mathematical
model.

3. Methodology
In concise, present study deals with design, fabrication, instal-
lation and proof run of the cooling water power generating system.
The site parameters and conditions were ﬁrst investigated for
feasibility study and subsequent custom design. Design and fabri-
cation were done based on the pertinence and constraints of the
unique site. The system was then transported to the site for
installation, followed by a three-month period of proof run. In
addition, techno-economical study was conducted to forecast the
payback period and the potential of the project.
3.1. Resource particulars
The cooling water is essentially raw sea water [19]. It only un-
dergoes simple ﬁltration to separate out logs, debris and marine
lives before being sent to cool the turbines. There is no any desa-
lination or puriﬁcation treatment done to the cooling water.
Exhaust cooling water is then discharged into the estuary again by
gravity alone, without involving any mechanical work. It passed
through discharge channels buried beneath the soil and two
exposed discharge weirs. At the intake, the sea water was measured
around 30e31 C. It was slightly alkaline, with pH of 7.5e7.9. The
chloride contain was measured 26.64 mg/L, and the particle counts
were around 600e1000 mg/L.
Due to the corrosive nature of the site, the whole system should
ideally be constructed with marine-grade material. However, in
practical the budget and the material availability must be taken into
account. Consequently, the turbine and the rig were constructed
with 316L stainless steel, whereas the arm was built with 304
stainless steel, which costs about half of 316L stainless steel. Fig. 4
shows the schematic diagram of the system.
3.2. Design requirements
There are many types of micro hydro turbines available in the
market, though it is unwise to just purchase one and installed it to
the site. Those turbines are generic designs and require signiﬁcant
amount of civil work in order to install, whereas SPC authority had
prohibited any sort of civil work to be carried out to the site. The
reasons were that the cooling water cannot be stopped, and the
strong water is a serious threat to whoever carried out any civil
work there.
In order to install the system to the site without stopping the
water, the system must be an integrated and comprehensive sys-
tem that can be installed with crane alone and did not require any
civil work. Through the site survey, it was identiﬁed that the water
characteristics of the site are low head and high ﬂow. In addition,
SPC authority wanted the system to be adjustable to suit various
heads and ﬂow. With these considerations in mind, a comparative
study, as shown in Table 2, of various types of micro hydro turbines
and waterwheels available in the market was made to decide on the
type of turbine best suit the system [11,20,21]. Thus through this
comparative study, it was found that the most suitable micro hydro
turbine for the site is a hybrid design based on crossﬂow turbine
and breastshot waterwheel. The present hybrid design of hydro-
electricity system is innovative, as far as authors’ knowledge is
concerned.
4. Techno-economical study
The cooling water power generating system is an original design
that was fabricated with locally available material. Therefore, its
economy and efﬁciency were the focus for present study.
4.1. Energy recovery
The system recovered energy from the cascading cooling water,
which is supplied by the pumps to cool the coal-ﬁred gas turbines
inside the power plant. It should be noted that this system did not
recover any thermal energy from the exhaust cooling water; it only
salvaged mechanical energy (kinetic and potential energy). The ERE
(energy recovery efﬁciency) used for this study can be deﬁned as
the ratio of power recovered from the cooling water over the power
used to supply the cooling water. In reality, this amount can never
go beyond 100%. The power used to supply the cooling water can be
known from the pump speciﬁcation, which is 658 kW per pump.
Since the discharge weir accommodates cooling water from two
pumps with total capacity of 1,316, the maximum theoretical ERE of
the site is 69.6/1316 ¼ 5.3% while the hanging cooling water power
generating system's ERE is 10/1316 ¼ 0.76%. This means that if the
designed system is 100% efﬁcient, it can recover 69.6 kW or 5.3% of
the energy supplied to the pair of pumps. In actuality, the efﬁciency
of the system is 10/69.6 ¼ 14.4%. This is considered rather low based
on the percentage, and implies that current study still has room for
improvement.

4.3. Durability
The durability of the system was being tested during the proof
run. Since the system came into direct contact with warm sea
water and vapor (measuring around 37e39 C), the corrosion
rate is inevitably grim and rapid. After three months of proof run,
the system was observed to have different degrees of rust at
various parts that were not constructed with 316L stainless steel.
Bearings, chains, sprockets, bolts and nuts are among the parts
that were not locally available in 316L stainless steel or other
marine-grade material, and therefore suffered visibly severe rust.
Although grease, paint and coat were applied to these parts, the
elevated sea water and vapor that the system constantly came
into contact with had quickly eroded these protections. Never-
theless, the system was able to continue the proof run for
another nine months before it was decided to be lifted out from
the site.

namely the synchronous pulley-belt and sprocket-chain. An infra-

quently all tests were done outside of the site and there is no safety
risk to the testers. The overall schematic diagram of the operation is

built up line. In order to obtain ideal condition, rotation build up

achieve 50% efﬁciency. Lower gradient lines show lower efﬁciency
due to losses.

5.2. Discussion
There are three primary factors that distinguish the present
cooling water power generating system from other existing micro
hydro: resource, site and design. The resource for this study is
heated sea water, unlike other micro hydro that deals with cool
fresh water. Furthermore, vapor of the heated sea water is contin-
uously gushing up from the bottom of the site, ﬁlling and wetting
the atmosphere above and around the site. Therefore, both iron-
based and steel-based materials that are commonly used for ordi-
nary micro hydro are not suitable for this system. Any unprotected
and uncoated iron and steel-based product will rapidly corrode and
collapse when exposed to the site. Galvanization, coating and sur-
face treatment will slow down the corrosion rate considerably, but
these methods are not ideal for moving or rotating parts, which
happened to be the case in this study. Instead, marine-grade ma-
terial should be used for the whole system to minimize the
corrosion. Fortunately the turbine and the rig were constructed
with 316L stainless steel, a marine-grade material that is available
locally and not overly expensive. The arm was constructed with 304
stainless steel, which is not a marine-grade material though a lot
more resistant to corrosion compared to irons and steels. However,
not all parts were available in stainless steel or marine-grade ma-
terial. The bearings, generator and drive system (both sprocket-
chain and pulley-belt) are only available in irons and steels. These
parts had been heavily greased and painted, but the protection
lasted only for a limited period of three months.
Secondly, the site is a cooling water discharge weir and the
cooling water is supplied to the coal-ﬁred gas turbine continuously.
A halt of the cooling water is practically impossible. With the huge
amount of water running continuously and consistently in the
discharge weir, installing the system to the site is a risky task. This
hazardous condition is unique to this artiﬁcial site e even when
compared to similar project proposed by Stevanovic et al. [17] in
their corresponding site at the coal-ﬁred thermal power plant
“Nikola Tesla B” in Serbia. As a complement, the heads available at
both sites is presented in Fig. 10; the ﬂow rates for both sites are
consistent since both power plants operate as base load power
plant. For safety reasons, SPC prohibits any civil work to be carried
out to the discharge weir. This means that the cooling water power
generating system must be an integrated and comprehensive sys-
tem comprising all important parts of an ordinary micro hydro, and
that its installation as well as subsequent operation must not
interrupt the continuous running of cooling water.
Thirdly, the design of the system is unique to the site. It is
designed in such a way that adaptiveness was prioritized over
performance. Therefore, there is a limitation to the size and weight
of the generator located within the system. A generator of higher
output (such as 20 kW or 30 kW) attached to this system may
produce better performance and ERE, but it is too bulky and heavy
for the system. Similar cases apply to low rpm generators, where
they are comparatively larger and heavier than their 1500 rpm
counterparts. Conclusively, with the hanging design of this system,
it is not suitable for common micro hydro sites such as stream and
river; rather, it is more suitable for some industrial sites which have
similar conﬁguration to the site in this study.
From economy perspective, the present prototype payback
period is calculated to be around 4.2 years i.e. less than 4 years. A
payback period range of 0.9e9.7 years has been reported by Barelli
et al. [13] via their AFDC model for the ﬁve rivers investigated in
their studies.
6. Conclusions and recommendations
Present study contributes in the aspects of design, development
and implementation of a novel energy recovery system. It is a local
experimental effort in an attempt to recover the waste energy from
the cooling water. In this study, the system had operated accord-
ingly during the three-month period of proof run. Environment
wise, it is a waste-to-energy project that did not consume any fossil
fuel and produced zero emission. Economy wise, its payback period
is calculated to be less than 4 years. Its driving efﬁciency was tested
in the range of 25% and 45%. For this study, available sea water is
corrosive and detrimental to the iron and steel parts of the system.
On the other hand, stainless steel, especially marine-grade stainless
steel is a better option, though it is very expensive for the present
project and rather rare in the local market. The design and fabri-
cation of the system received certain degree of restriction from this
very factor.
Though this system can run, there are rooms for improvements
for this design. Some further works are suggested as follow:
 Local hardwood such as Belian can be used to substitute the I
beams that supported the hanging system for long term appli-
cation and sustainability consideration;
 A combined hydro-photovoltaic system would be nice for the
site, considering factors such as abundant and unsheltered
sunlight, improved performance and esthetic design. Such
endeavor design is still underway.
Further modeling and forecasting of the system performance in
more details are suggested for future works.
