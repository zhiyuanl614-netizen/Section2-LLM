A B S T R A C T  
The interdependence between the power grid and water supply system (WSS) provides opportunities for energy 
conservation. However, the existing water supply flexibility has not been fully developed, resulting in an un­
satisfactory effect. To address the problem, this paper proposes an optimal energy management strategy for a 
renewable microgrid (MG) integrating WSSs. First, a novel supply model that evaluates the interdependence 
between the large WSS and several islanded sub-systems, hydraulic stability, and desalination characteristic is 
established, in order to reduce the hydraulic coupling constraints and ensure water supply safety. To achieve an 
optimal performance of the MG integrating WSSs, a novel two-stage dispatching method is designed, where the 
day-head decisions fully consider the real-time power fluctuation, and the real-time stage corrects renewable 
generation deviation and supports external grid. Additionally, a two-stage algorithm is proposed to efficiently 
solve the scheduling problem. Simulation studies on a MG integrating WSSs indicate the proposed energy 
management strategy can reduce the water supply loss by 5.2%, and improve the MG economy by 19.5%, while 
supporting the external grid (EG).   
1. Introduction 
Water supply systems (WSSs) are responsible for seawater desali­
nation, and living water distribution in the offshore island commu­
nities. It is indicated that a lot of electricity is consumed by WSSs in 
many offshore microgrids (MGs) [1,2]. Due to the presence of water 
tanks (WTs) or reservoirs, water supply loads (such as desalination and 
pumping loads) can be flexibly adjusted [3]. Therefore, if properly 
managed, it can not only significantly reduce the loss of water supply, 
but also greatly improve the operation economy of MG. 
1.1. Literature review 
There have been many research efforts on optimizing water supply 
for economic or security purposes. For example, [4,5] develop a La­
grangian relaxation-based algorithm for a collaborative water supply 
chain network design problem that coordinates the coordinate the 
water supply and wastewater collection. Ref. [6] presents a water re­
source and distribution system design optimization model with many 
objectives including minimizing operating costs while maximizing en­
gineering performance. Ref. [7] discusses an energy efficiency evalua­
tion measure for WSS designs and a mathematical model for optimizing 
water pump (WP) operation through the arrangement of WTs in a 
building. Mitigation and control strategies are studied in [8] to mini­
mize the energy consumption of the desalination device (DD) con­
sidering environmental factors. A robust optimization framework for 
urban bulk WSSs is proposed in [9] to explore the trade-off between 
efficiency and sensitivity to different climate change scenarios. A two- 
stage stochastic model is presented in [10,11] to reduce the energy 
consumption of WPs considering water demand and rainfall un­
certainties. A price-dependent load management method is used in [12] 
to optimize the municipal water supply and wastewater treatment. 
Likely, an optimal water distribution model is developed in [13] to 
optimize the WPs according to the dynamic electricity price. 
Although a lot of work has been done in the development of water 
supply flexibility, due to pipe hydraulic coupling constraints, the WPs 
and WTs still cannot be fully regulated, resulting in serious water dis­
tribution loss. Additionally, due to the lack of a feasible power-output 
model reference, DDs generally operate at rated power to ensure the 
freshwater supply, which causes a waste of power regulation capacity. 
As a further study, the energy-water management in cost saving and 
consumption reduction has attracted researchers' interest [14]. A 
system-based framework is proposed in [15] to synthesize the inter­
woven connections between energy consumption and water use in a 
city. A bond graph of system modeling language is used in [16] to 
analyze the interdependence between the power grid and WSS. A fuzzy

However, the frequent use of WPs to smooth electric power will 
cause water pressure fluctuations on the minute-time scale, resulting in 
a bad user experience, while without a feasible model reference, direct 
regulation of desalination load will cause desalination loss. Even worse, 
the classic day-head dispatching model cannot consider the real-time 
electric and hydraulic power fluctuation on the minute-time scale. 
Therefore, following the day-head decisions, both the DDs and WPs 
may not smooth the instantaneous renewable generation variation due 
to power ramp constraints, and the real-time water pressure fluctuation 
may exceed the allowance. Additionally, regarding the MG connected 
to the external grid (EG), the WSSs can not only respond to the local 
MG, but also support EG (reduce the demand for peak and frequency 
modulation), which has not been discussed. 
1.2. Contribution 
It can be seen from the literature review that the water supply loads 
are not well studied on flexibility development and energy saving. 
Therefore, a novel water-energy management strategy needs to be de­
signed. 
Note that there are two main obstacles to solve the above problem. 
Firstly, a novel water supply model needs to be proposed, in order to 
release the regulation ability. Secondly, a novel dispatching method 
needs to be presented to ensure the performance of the MG integrating 
WSSs. 
To tackle the first challenge, we propose that by controlling pipe 
valves, the large WSS can be split into several islanded sub-systems to 
reduce nodal coupling constraints. The nodal hydraulic relationship 
before and after the system splitting is modelled. Also, the hydraulic 
stability and desalination characteristic are analyzed to ensure water 
supply safety. To tackle the second challenge, we propose a novel two- 
stage dispatching method. The day-head stage fully considers the hy­
draulic fluctuation, and instantaneous load regulation difficulty due to 
the real-time power fluctuation, and the real-time stage corrects re­
newable generation deviation and supports EG. Hence, the main con­
tributions of this paper are summarized below: 
(1) A novel water supply model is proposed, where the inter­
dependence between the large WSS and several islanded sub-sys­
tems, and the hydraulic stability and desalination characteristic are 
analyzed.  
(2) A novel two-stage MG dispatching strategy is designed, where the 
day-head decisions fully consider the electric and hydraulic fluc­
tuation using Latin hypercube sampling method, while the real-time 
stage corrects renewable generation deviation and supports EG.  
(3) A feasible two-stage algorithm is designed to solve the optimization 
problem efficiently. 
(4) Using the realistic demand and renewable generation, the effec­
tiveness and benefits of the proposed energy management strategy 
are illustrated. 
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Materials and Methods 
are presented in Section 2. Experimental results and discussion are in­
troduced in Section 3. The conclusion is drawn in Section 4. 
2. Materials and methods 
Fig. 1 shows the typical structure of a renewable MG integrating 
WSSs. Due to the existence of tanks and reservoirs, the water supply 
load can respond to the MG and EG demand while satisfying the hy­
draulic usage demand. To achieve an optimal performance of the MG 
integrating WSSs, a novel water-energy management strategy is pro­
posed in this paper.

the hydraulic factors (such as pressure heads) of adjacent nodes are 
connected to each other. To reduce the hydraulic coupling constraints, 
the large WSS can be split into several islanded sub-systems by con­
trolling pipe valves. The detailed model is presented as follows:

2.2. Two-stage dispatching strategy 
To ensure the performance of the MG integrating WSSs, a novel two- 
stage dispatching strategy is proposed, as observed in Fig. 4. Fully 
considering the real-time electric and hydraulic fluctuation, the day- 
head stage optimizes the WSS to minimize the MG operational cost, and 
the real-time stage mitigates the MG renewable power fluctuation and 
respond to the EG demand. We can see the WSS can not only satisfy the 
MG demand, but also support the EG.  
(1) Day-ahead dispatching stage

model cannot consider the real-time power fluctuation. However, the 
violent variation of renewable generation on the minute-time scale may

the hydraulic stability constraint of (8) may not be satisfied. To solve 
the problem, a novel day-head model considering the real-time power 
fluctuation is proposed, as follows:

3. Experimental results and discussion 
3.1. Test system 
A grid-connected MG integrating a WSS is used to verify the effec­
tiveness of the proposed strategy. The renewable generation, electric 
loads, hydraulic loads and electricity price are shown in Fig. 5 [30].  
Fig. 6 shows the typical WSS [10]. Without loss of generality, the large 
WSS can be split three sub-systems marked as ①②③. The rated desali­
nation power is set to 600 kW, and the water supply parameters are 
shown in Table 1 in appendix [23]. Considering the natural precipita­
tion, the daily freshwater production is set to 2100 m3 in summer and 
3150 m3 in winter, respectively. 
To show the superiority of the proposed strategy, three cases are 
compared, as follows: 
Case 1: The novel WSS model is used to carry out demand side re­
sponse using the proposed dispatching strategy. 
Case 2: The WSS is dispatched to respond to the MG demand ac­
cording to [23], where the WSS cannot be split and the DD always 
works at rated power.   
Case 3: The WSS carries out demand side response according to 
electricity price [22], where the WSS cannot be split and the DD 
works at rated power. 
The simulations are coded in MATLAB and solved by Gurobi on a 
Windows-based PC with 8 GB of RAM. 
3.2. Analysis of the energy management method 
According to the above simulation parameters, the MG integrating 
WSSs is tested. The results are as follows: 
3.2.1. Energy consumption analysis   
1) Typical scenario in summer 
Fig. 7 shows that the electricity consumption of the controllable 
loads in three cases in summer. As observed from the figure, the con­
trollable loads in case 3 are shifted to carry out energy arbitrage by 
consuming more low-price electricity and less high-price electricity. 
Unlike this, the controllable loads in case 1 and 2 closely follow the 
abundant renewable generation. However, compared with those in case 
2, the energy consumption of the controllable loads in case 1 is more 
elastic. Therefore, the controllable loads in case 1 can better carry out 
peak shaving and valley filling. 
Fig. 8 shows the desalination power and production efficiency in 
three cases. For case 2 and case 3, the DDs are only allowed to work at 
rated power. The desalination time is optimized to reduce the system 
operational cost. However, in case 1, both the working time and power 
of the DD are dispatched according to the desalination characteristic. 
To satisfy the freshwater demand of 2100 m3, 5551-kWh, 6000-kWh, 
and 6000-kWh electricity are consumed by DDs in three cases, that is, 
the freshwater productions per kilowatt hour are 0.3783 m3 in case 1, 
and 0.3500 m3 in case 2 and case 3, respectively. Compared with that in 
case 2 and 3, desalination energy consumption in case 1 can be reduced 
by 7.5%. 
Fig. 9 depicts the WP power in three cases. ①②③ express the sub- 
systems, and the connection between them indicates the large system 
split state. As observed from the figure, the WSS in case 1 can be dy­
namically split by controlling valves, and the WP can be operated in the 
range of 38.10–398 kW. However, limited by the nodal coupling con­
straints, the WPs can only work within 86.40–422.2 kW in case 2 and 
71.01–345.14 kW in case 3. In the dispatching period, the WPs in case 
1–3 consume 3713.1-kWh, 3918.2-kWh, and 4022.0 kWh electricity, 
respectively. Compared with that in case 2 and 3, the water distribution 
loss in case 1 can be reduced by 5.2%, and 7.7%, respectively. 
Fig. 10 shows the exchange power between the MG and EG in three 
cases. We can see that compared with that in case 2 and 3, the exchange 
power in case 1 is smother. Additionally, unlike that the exchange 
energy is 3704.0 kWh in case 2, and 8959.3 kWh in case 3, only 2946.1- 
kWh electricity is traded in case 1. The results show that the MG in­
tegrating the novel WSS is more elastic and friendly to the EG.  
2) Typical scenario in winter

three cases in winter. The controllable load in case 3 still only responds 
to the low-price electricity. Although both of them can follow the re­
newable generation variation, the controllable load in case 1 is more 
elastic than that in case 2.

4. Conclusion 
This paper proposes an optimal energy management strategy for a 
renewable MG integrating WSSs. A novel water supply model is pre­
sented that considers the large system split feasibility, hydraulic sta­
bility, and desalination characteristic. To ensure the performance of the 
MG integrating WSSs, a two-stage scheduling method is designed for 
minimizing the MG operational costs and power fluctuations. This 
scheduling problem is efficiently solved by a two-stage algorithm. The 
simulation study indicates that the energy-water management strategy 
can carry out energy arbitrage, power smoothing while supporting the 
external grid simultaneously. 
The novel water supply model can significantly improve the op­
erational flexibility of the DD and WP. The desalination loss and water 
distribution loss can be reduced by more than 7.1% and 5.2%, re­
spectively. Additionally, using the proposed scheduling strategy, the 
operational cost of the MG integrating WSSs can be reduced by 19.5%. 
The hydraulic and electric stability can also be ensured. 
However, some technical details neglected in the theoretical dis­
cussion of this paper may be of great importance in practical applica­
tion. Therefore, we will conduct a real case study in the future. 
Additionally, the extreme weather significantly affects the reliability of 
the power supply. The resilience of power gird integrating water supply 
systems will also our future work.
