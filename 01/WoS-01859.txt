Abstract: Problems in energy supply security threaten smart grids and cities, 
critical infrastructures and industrial control systems, and directly affect the 
economy. This study aims to examine the increase in energy supply, energy 
supply security risks, global warming, the importance of renewable energy 
sources, and environmental factors in energy infrastructure security. Also, a 
security framework has been proposed for critical energy infrastructures such 
as electricity, water, natural gas, coal, oil, renewable energy, and nuclear 
energy sources, taking into account the key risk factors of energy supply 
security and environmental impact sources. In the proposed framework,  
17 different types of risks have been defined due to the reasons for natural gas, 
electricity, coal, oil, nuclear, and renewable energy sources. Risks concerned 
energy supply security and environmental impacts are calculated according to 
energy sources and it is determined that renewable energy sources have less 
risk (9%) than other energy types, electricity (13%) and natural gas (15%). 
Keywords: energy supply security; emissions; smart grid; renewable energy 
grids; natural gas.

Introduction 
Energy supply security is one of the most important issues to be addressed in recent years 
to ensure the safety of people and life on earth. The importance of renewable and 
environmentally friendly smart grids, energy supply security in critical energy 
infrastructures, and industrial control systems have been increasing in recent years. 
Energy supply security becomes an important issue with the increasing energy 
consumption of countries. As an example, it is estimated that the consumption of 
renewable energy sources will be 100 quadrillions by 2020, the consumption will be  
250 quadrillions by 2050 and natural gas consumption will be 200 quadrillions by 2050 
(EIA, 2020). 
The main sources of energy supply are natural gas, coal, oil, renewable energy, and 
nuclear energy that are used for electricity generation. Adequate measures should be 
taken due to the increasing cybersecurity attacks on energy resources in recent years. The 
energy supply security framework consists of national and international security, 
technology security, economic security, environmental security, transportation security, 
physical security, and cybersecurity steps. Security gaps are among the issues that should 
be taken into consideration by policymakers due to the nature of the energy sector. Also,

the dependence of all sectors on electricity and fuel brings the obligation and 
responsibility to increase the resiliency of energy infrastructures against possible attacks. 
Numerous studies have been published on establishing the conceptual framework of 
energy security and analysing supply security. Energy security is a multidimensional 
concept in academic studies and the use of energy security indicators has been an 
increasing trend in evaluating energy supply security performance (Ang et al., 2015). 
Looking at recent research, most of the energy security studies can be divided into two 
categories. These categories are indicated by the insecurity of specific energy supply 
(Gnansounou, 2008; Gupta, 2008; Le Coq and Paltseva, 2009; Cabalu, 2010; Lefevre, 
2010), and energy security in general (Cohen et al., 2011; Sharifuddin, 2014; Sovacool 
and Brown, 2010). 
When the practices in developed countries aiming at the protection of critical 
infrastructures are examined, it has been determined that in addition to taking measures 
against physical and cyber-attacks, sharing the experience among sector employees is one 
of the effective methods. Special regulations for the energy sector should be developed 
by integrating the state and private sectors concerning the security of energy 
infrastructures. The specific threats and political position of each country should be 
assessed and implemented accordingly. Based on the primary energy consumption in the 
world, renewable energy consumption has witnessed a substantial increase over the years. 
While discussions on nuclear energy resources have been going on since the middle 
of the 20th century, the importance and use of nuclear energy have drawn attention. The 
high-security risk factor and the global warming phenomenon, which is the focus of this 
study, has shined the star of renewable energy sources, which are sustainable and clean. 
Renewable energy sources have rapidly become widespread for both residential and 
industrial uses. 
Global warming and environmental pollution are among the biggest threats to human 
health and other living organisms as well as to energy supply security. The insecurity of 
energy supply and the insufficiency of natural resources are the biggest threats to a 
country’s economic and political stability. Policymakers should provide significant 
support in energy supply security, otherwise, academic studies will be insufficient to 
address these problems. Environmental pollution, especially caused by energy 
production, causes human health losses and a decrease in human activities as well as 
global warming. These events also cause global economic crises. Global warming causes 
the degradation of nature, diseases, natural hazards and disasters, melting glaciers, and 
more severe weather events. The use of renewable and sustainable energy sources with a 
secure energy supply can minimise the occurrence of these events. The main goal is to 
create a safer energy infrastructure to minimise environmental impacts on the energy 
supply while contributing to the country’s economy (Jacobson, 2009). 
Regarding the security of the supply of energy infrastructures, a special program 
should be developed for the energy sector by a holistic approach with the coordination of 
the state and private sectors. The specific threats and political position of each country 
should be evaluated and practices should be done accordingly. It is seen as an imperative 
to take steps to protect the energy infrastructures of all countries in various national and 
international projects, to develop public-private sector cooperation, and to bring a 
different perspective to energy security in this context. 
Energy supply security definitions also change according to the strengths and policies 
of the countries in the international arena. Due to the global climate change experienced 
in recent years, the concept of clean energy has been added to these definitions (Sevim,

2009). Discussions on energy supply security require a good understanding of the 
concept. Different interpretations and approaches complicate the definition of energy 
supply security. Some studies interpret energy supply security economically and 
strategically/politically. In addition, local production of energy systems, especially 
promising renewable energy technologies, plays an important role in energy security in 
terms of economic sustainability (Bicer et al., 2015a). There are additional incentives 
while using locally produced equipment as well as in Turkey as in some countries (Bicer 
et al., 2015b). 
Government officials, who are experienced in the economy generally argue that it is 
related to a problem and ignoring it completely markets, such as energy supply security. 
They also argue that the state should only intervene when the market failure occurs. 
According to this approach, which is parallel to the classical economic approach, it is 
claimed that there is no such problem of energy supply security. According to another 
approach, the nationalisation of energy resources makes the issue of energy supply 
security a national security problem (Checci et al., 2009). 
When the literature in the last ten years is examined, it is seen that there is no 
emphasis on the effects of energy supply security and cybersecurity on environmental 
security. Therefore, in this study, the risks that may occur on energy supply security are 
analysed and their effects on the environment are discussed. While these risks were 
analysed and calculated, a certain number of experts were consulted. A specific scoring 
system is used while obtaining an expert opinion. The critical energy infrastructure for 
smart cities and grids was determined by considering several factors. The primary energy 
supply within critical energy infrastructures for reliable energy networks is emphasised 
through cybersecurity. Cyber-attacks are increasing day by day for critical energy 
infrastructures and threatening a secure energy supply. Hence, a detailed framework for 
the security of energy supply and environment subcomponents is suggested based on 
expert opinions focusing on key factors and risk types. Therefore, the proposed 
framework considers economic, environmental, physical, technological, and national 
security aspects as a holistic approach. Besides, it is aimed to draw attention to the effects 
of environmental security problems on global warming.

factors concerning energy and environmental security. Finally, Section 7 concludes the 
study by mentioning the main findings and future studies.

Critical energy infrastructures 
The critical energy infrastructure includes all energy-related generation and storage 
facilities, transmission lines and routes, as well as consumption facilities and systems. 
Platforms and natural gas wells used in the production of energy resources such as 
petroleum and natural gas, all pipelines from the main transmission lines that play a role 
in transporting these resources to secondary transmission lines, and all kinds of electricity 
generation systems, including liquefied natural gas (LNG) terminals, are evaluated within

the scope of critical infrastructure. In addition to these, houses, tankers, refineries, pump 
stations, renewable energy facilities, dams, and power control and protection systems, 
nuclear power plants, and power plants electricity generation systems are evaluated 
within the scope of critical infrastructure (Özertem, 2012). Critical infrastructures are the 
vital elements of the world community and the needs for critical infrastructure for the 
future should be taken into consideration. It is essential to implement a sustainable, 
environmentally compatible safety rule and policy based on uninterrupted flow and 
supply security and non-leakage in oil and gas pipeline facilities (The White House, 
2003). Furthermore, to make sure about the quality of the utilised energy systems and 
services, there are accreditation bodies who assure that the energy system manufacturer 
or smart grid operators comply with the international standards. Accredited testing and 
certification processes in smart grids, especially for renewable integration, are quite 
significant to sustain high qualities and uninterrupted energy supply (Bicer et al., 2015c). 
The economic welfare and development of the modern state, the use of advanced 
technology, and the development of the level of production and development to ensure 
the effective use of energy resources and infrastructure security is an indispensable 
element of national security policies (Caşın, 2014). In addition to the widespread use of 
coal, oil, and natural gas, the widespread use of nuclear energy has been increasing in 
recent years. Information technologies, communication, and data communication 
technologies that provide the control and distribution of these systems gain increased 
importance. In particular, these systems play an important role in the control and 
distribution of fuel, gas, and electricity flow in daily life, in terms of sustainable energy 
supply security and environmental awareness. The International Energy Agency (IEA) 
redefines the three vital elements of balanced energy policies as energy security, 
economic development, and environmental protection (CRS Report for Congress, 2005). 
The rising importance of the concept of globalisation in the international system has 
accelerated intergovernmental interdependence and hence cooperation efforts in the 
energy sector. In addition to the free movement of capital, services, and goods, it has 
brought the implementation of strategies and policies to ensure the continuous flow of 
energy to the world markets. In this context, the developed countries need to develop new 
and creative policies by taking into consideration the environmental aspects of 
uninterrupted, cheap, and safe energy, not only by accelerating the global supply of 
energy and demand due to the increasing population and industrial production (Caşın, 
2014).

Smart cities from environmental point of view 
The concept of a smart city is a holistic approach that uses information and 
communication technology (ICT) to improve the quality of life and accessibility of 
human beings and ensures continuous improvement of sustainable social, economic, and 
environmental development. It provides real-time, quality, and cost-effective solutions 
and services to human needs and the intersecting interaction between people and cities. 
Smart cities are based on the idea of reconstructing cities to maximise human and 
natural efficiency by reducing their complexity (Nam and Pardo, 2011). Also, smart cities 
have people-oriented, strategic management, open to improvement in terms of 
infrastructure, environmentally sensitive, and supportive management approach. Due to 
these reasons, these cities are city structures with improved service areas and living

standards. These structures are based on the ability to create comfortable, healthy, 
people-oriented, self-sufficient new living spaces where resources are used effectively 
and wisely, respecting nature and environmental problems are minimised by using 
innovative and sustainable methods. 
It includes components such as smart cities, smart grid, smart agriculture, smart 
environment, smart home, smart education, smart state, IoT, open data, and smart health 
systems. Smart agriculture is a technique that enables soil and product management to 
increase agricultural productivity and minimises the damage to the environment by using 
resources more economically. A smart environment includes renewable energy, smart 
grids, micro-grids, smart metres, air pollution monitoring technologies, environmentally 
friendly buildings, energy-efficient lighting systems, and smart water management. 
With technological advancements, the most distinctive feature of our age is the rapid 
change in human life in terms of adaptation to technology. The formation of societies that 
can keep up with the needs and rapid changes of the age is directly related to sustainable 
and high-level living environments. In recent years, many countries have attempted to 
structure their city infrastructures and services to increase the living welfare of 
communities and to manage growth and development sustainably. Similar to many other 
countries, Turkey has also experienced rapid population growth, urbanisation, global 
climate change, increased carbon footprint, which has caused environmental and habitat 
deformation. This is one of the reasons for introducing several regulations to promote 
renewable energy systems and their implementation in the country with encouraging 
incentives (Bicer et al., 2015a). The utilisation of smart energy and smart management of 
the energy grid will be one of the main criteria of smart cities. 
At this point, smart cities have come to the forefront in determining the policies of 
countries and international organisations as an approach that has an important potential in 
rational solutions to urban problems. A smart city is an approach based on the logic that 
urban infrastructure and networks, especially transportation and energy, can be  
self-managed without the need for human intervention. It also aims to achieve a 
significant improvement in people’s living standards and to create a livable city. The 
most important point to note in this matter is that the basic philosophy of this approach 
should be based on the ‘transformation’ logic. However, it is essential to make this 
transformation gradually following the texture of the city and without disturbing the 
existing systems, environment, and infrastructure of the city. In this respect, it is very 
important to initiate this transformation move within cities and to build according to the 
needs of the cities. 
A smart city needs to be efficient and technology-oriented. ICT helps to increase 
system efficiency and ensure the sustainability of energy supply and provides a way for 
the end consumer to communicate with the utility and network service provider. A smart 
network has a two-way communication model. These enable reliable, stable, and efficient 
communication between the service and the consumer using ICT (Ahuja and Khosla, 
2019). 
The British Standard, Public Available Specification (PAS 181), which is related to 
the smart city, shows that a smart city that has a functional integration of digital, 
concrete, and humanitarian systems offering a sustainable and comfortable living 
standard (British Standard Institution, 2014).

Smart grids for global warming mitigation 
The smart grid is made possible by using renewable and safe energy sources, by 
employing some sensors to save energy in public services and sectors where high energy 
is used, and by reducing the environmental footprint (Abbasi and Seifi, 2010). 
Infrastructure services in city life have an important position for a peaceful and 
sustainable human life. Control of these infrastructure services has gained vital 
importance due to the increase in the urban population. The growing population has made 
it impossible to use and control infrastructure without Information Technology (IT) 
applications. International Organization for Standardization (ISO), which is concerned 
with optimal management of physical assets, indicates that infrastructure management 
should be holistic, systematic, risk-based, optimum, and sustainable as illustrated in 
Figure 3. Organisational life plans are required through a foundation that manages 
systematised and interconnected movements and assets and institutions systems and 
control the execution, risk, and expenditures for success (Minnaar et al., 2013). This 
requires smart grids for systematic control. 
The smart grid field communication is based on the International Electrotechnical 
Commission (IEC) 61850 standard. The IEC 61850 protocol standard for the sub-station 
allows all protection, calculation, testing, and monitoring to be combined with a single 
standard protocol (Baigent et al., 2004). Otherwise, devices of different manufacturers 
will not be able to use a large number of protocols and interfaces that do not mutually fit 
or are not parallel to each other. In the technical field, equipment standardisation and 
interoperability have been implemented for a long time (Dönmez, 2013). 
One of the most important contributions of smart grids is to protect the environment 
in terms of global warming and to prevent greenhouse gases to be released into the 
environment. With this approach, the existence of smart grids is vital for the 
environment. All countries around the world determine their international strategies in 
terms of smart grids (ELDER, 2020). Smart grids are at the core of energy recovery,

Discussion 
Energy security is important in terms of maintaining social life and continuity of  
well-being. In addition to the environmental impact of energy security, cybersecurity 
implications have come to the fore in recent years. Cyber-attacks pose a serious threat, 
especially to critical infrastructures. It is noteworthy that in recent years this 
cybersecurity and environmental safety has not received sufficient attention. It is 
important to minimise these effects. The aim here is to treat this security of power supply 
in terms of different security as well as the types of risks that can arise in critical 
infrastructure sectors. These risks are posed to various experts and opinions are accepted. 
In light of these opinions, several key factors are identified and discussed in this section. 
In addition, the effects of environmental risks on other energy sources in energy supply 
security are discussed. When looking at risk levels, renewable energy sources, electricity, 
and gas resources are among the least risky energy sources. A risk calculation is made in 
Table 1 according to the opinions of experts on this subject. 
6.1 Security of energy supply and environment 
Energy supply security is defined as the realisation of energy supply, transportation, and 
demand within the scope of production, transmission, and consumption of energy in 
sufficient quantity and quality, at reasonable cost/prices, in an uninterrupted and 
environmentally sensitive manner (Stiller et al., 2008). The IEA defines energy supply 
security as “uninterrupted accessibility of energy resources at an affordable price.” 
Energy supply security includes security of gas, electricity, oil, coal, nuclear and 
renewable energy, and other vital sources (IEA, 2020). Another definition “Provision of 
sufficient quality and clean energy at affordable prices and without interruption” states 
that energy should be clean as well as sufficient (Ediger, 2007). Koyama and Kutani 
(2012) explained energy supply security as “securing people’s life, economic, social and 
defense activities at a reasonable price level, among other purposes, the energy required 
for the project” in their studies. 
Energy supply security consists of four main important elements, namely; 
availability, accessibility, reasonable price, and acceptability. The existence of these four 
basic elements ensures energy supply security (Kruyt et al., 2009; Elkind, 2010). The 
energy supply security framework consists of national and international security, 
technology security, economic security, environmental security, transportation security, 
physical security, and cybersecurity steps. The energy supply security framework is 
proposed to make the energy supply security safer, as shown in Figure 1.

Conclusions 
In this study, it is emphasised that energy supply security is a very important issue in 
terms of sustainability, especially with the increase in the world population and the 
increasing energy demand. Energy is an indispensable building block of all countries and 
has an impact on the whole economy and the environment. Interruptions in the energy 
supply chain threaten the environment, health, and welfare and directly affect the 
economy and prevent continuous and regular energy distribution to consumers. Smart 
grids and cities in critical energy infrastructures have become one of the most important 
topics studied in the world in recent years. 
Considering the values obtained from the opinions of experts regarding the risks other 
than the energy source in energy supply security, it is seen that renewable energy sources 
are the most reliable sources with 9.22%. Looking at other resources, the second position 
is electricity with 12.76% and gas resources with 14.44%. 
Apart from energy supply and environmental security, 17 risk types have been 
identified. These risks are given in the table depending on the research of all academic 
studies and expert opinions. Looking at risk types, it is observed that there is about 
8.411% security risk, 8.005% cybersecurity risk, and 7.019% personal risk weight. Thus, 
it can be seen that the security of the systems affects the environment, nature, and global 
warming. Disruption of systems with interventions in systems varies depending on 
environmental and resources. The size of the risks is given that they affect greatly 
depending on the risks. 
In addition to presenting energy supply security as a framework in many areas, 
attention is drawn to the importance of environmental impacts, environmental safety, and 
their impact on the environment. The economic and health aspects of environmental 
damages are discussed in more detail. For environmental safety, priority should be given 
to balancing environmental plans, environmental responsibilities, and energy fields with 
low greenhouse gas emissions such as renewable energy and nuclear energy. Also, in this 
study, a new framework is proposed to evaluate energy supply security. This proposed 
framework useful for future studies for researchers and institutions engaged in energy 
supply security accounting for environmental impacts and some other risks.
