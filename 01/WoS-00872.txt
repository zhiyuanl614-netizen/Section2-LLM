Estimating the possible impacts of security attacks on physical processes can help to rank the critical assets
based on their sensitivity to performed attacks and predict their attractiveness from the attacker’s point
of view. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a new method for assessing the direct and indirect
impacts of attacks on cyber–physical systems (CPSs). The proposed method studies the dynamic behavior
of systems in normal situation and under security attacks and evaluates the consequence propagation of
attacks. The inputs to the model are control parameters including sensor readings and controller signals.
The output of the model is evaluating the consequence propagation of attacks, ranking the important
assets of systems based on their sensitivity to conducted attacks, and prioritizing the attacks based on
their impacts on the behavior of system. The validation phase of the proposed method is performed by
modeling and evaluating the consequence propagation of attacks against a boiling water power plant
(BWPP).

1. Introduction
The risk of disruptive interventions against critical infrastruc-
tures from various groups of adversaries has become increasingly
high in recent years. Because attacks against critical infrastructure
(CI) may have catastrophic impacts on the physical world and hu-
man lives [1], the security of critical infrastructure has become
an active research area in recent years. Smart grids, water plants,
chemical plants, oil and natural gas distribution systems and trans-
portation systems are examples of critical infrastructures, which
many people depend on [2].
Integrating computing and communication systems with
physical world has led to the emergence of cyber–physical systems

(CPSs). CPSs are computer-controlled systems monitoring and
controlling physical processes [2]. These systems use a number
of controllers such as programmable logic controllers (PLCs),
communication equipment, sensors and actuators to manage and
monitor some entities in the physical world [2].
As mentioned in [3], the modernization of critical infrastruc-
tures and emergence of CPSs are crucial to improve efficiency and
flexibility, but, on the other hand, this integration and progress
have subjected them to cyber threats. The security failures in CPSs
can impact human lives and safety in general, and cause damage
to industrial products and facilities [1]. According to the report
released by the industrial control systems computer emergency
readiness team (ICS-CERT) [4], the number of incidents related to
the security breaches involving CPSs in 2012 was more than five
times their 2010 level [5]. Thus, addressing the security of these
systems has become an important topic in recent years.

of CPSs is to study how the physical process is being controlled

To achieve this goal, in this paper we propose a new method

The DEMATEL method has been used for solving several groups
of interdependent problems [8,9]. This method has been used
in various contexts in recent years. For instance, it is applied to
identify key success factors of hospital service quality [10], and to
evaluate the causal relations among the criteria in auto spare parts
industry [11].
DEMATEL is a comprehensive method used for building and an-
alyzing a structural model involving causal relationships between
complex factors [6,7]. This feature of the DEMATEL method is em-
ployed in this paper to evaluate and rank the impacts of intentional
disturbances against the behavior of physical processes.
It is noteworthy that although we use the DEMATEL method for
sensitivity analysis of control loops, the input data of the model are
the control parameter values representing the dynamic behavior
of the underlying physical process. In fact, in contrast to the
traditional DEMATEL method, where the values of the parameters
are assigned by system experts, in the proposed method, the
impact evaluation of control parameters is performed according to
the system dynamics.
In this paper, by considering sensor measurements and control
signals as the primary target of disturbances, we study how an
attack against system parameters can affect the values of other
parameters. The normal behavior of the system without any
disruptions and the abnormal behavior of the system under attacks
are compared. The system parameters are divided into two classes
of cause and effect parameters, which can be same as or different
from each other. Finally, new metrics are proposed, which can be
used for quantifying the importance level of each parameter in the
underlying physical process. By using the resulting quantitative
values, we can prioritize the sensor readings and control signals
based on their sensitivity to conducted attacks. Besides, we can
rank the performed attacks based on their direct and indirect
consequences on physical dynamics of the system.

2. An overview of cyber–physical systems
In this section, we discuss about the overall architecture of CPSs
and the security issues of them.
2.1. The architecture of cyber–physical systems
The current state of CPSs can be described by capturing the
values of its important process variables. Two kinds of important
process or state variables in CPSs include (1) measured variables
representing the sensor measurements and (2) control variables
representing the control signals [12]. The normal value of a certain
process parameter is referred to as the set point. For example, the
desired value of the pressure inside a tank in a chemical plant is
called the set point of that process parameter [12].
In CPSs, the distance between the values of process variables
and the corresponding set points is calculated by controllers.
After calculating this offset, the controllers using a complex set
of equations, elaborate a local actuation strategy, and calculate a
new actuating or control variable. The resulting manipulated value
is sent to the suitable actuator to keep the process closer to the
determined set point [13]. The controllers also send the received
measurements to main control servers and execute the issued
commands from them.
In CPSs, the operators of the system must be aware of the
current state of the controlled objects. Thus, a graphical user
interface (GUI) called human–machine interface (HMI) represents
the current state of the controlled object to the human operators.
Fig. 1 depicts an overall representation of CPSs architecture.
The architecture of a CPS is often composed of two primary
layers [3,14]:
(1) The cyber layer, that consists of corporate network, control
network and a demilitarized zone (DMZ), and
(2) The physical layer, which consists of sensors, actuators and
physical devices.
The corporate network containing workstations, employees
and application server is in charge of business management and
customer interaction.
The control network is composed of controllers like PLCs,
control server and HMI. A DMZ is defined as a separate
network segment that connects directly to firewalls [15,16].
Servers such as data server, data historian and web servers (for
corporate–customer interactions) containing the data from the CPS
that needs to be accessed from the corporate network are put on
this separate segment for improved security [16].
The cyber layer often uses industrial protocols such as
DNP3 [17], 61 850 [18] and Modbus [19] to communicate with the
physical layer devices. A hugely popular approach for protecting
the industrial control protocols are virtual private networks
(VPNs) [16]. In order to protect these protocols from unauthorized

The communications between sensors and controllers in a CPS

2.2. The security of cyber–physical systems
The main difference between the security plans in cyber do-
mains and CPSs is that the target of security improvement tech-
niques in cyber domain is protecting the stored and transmitted
data and availability of services. But, in CPSs, protecting the op-
erations is the primary target of the security improvement tech-
niques [12].
In cyber–physical domain penetrating to the system is not
the same as breaking the system [21]. The required knowledge
for conducting a successful attack against CPSs is considerably
different from that of the cyber domains. In fact, one of the most
important differences between attacks in cyber domains with
cyber–physical domains is that in order to disrupt the physical
process, attackers need to acquire some sufficient knowledge
about the process behavior and its failure conditions, control
principles and signal processing, and so on [21]. Without this
knowledge, an adversary cannot bring the system to the unsafe
state.
There are two main data flows in CPSs [3]. The first flow is the
measured information by sensors that are sent to the controller
devices and the second data flow is the control commands, which
are sent to the actuators. These two data flows are the significant
targets of attacks in cyber–physical domain.
By sending false data into the controller, it will decide based on
these false measurement and issues incorrect commands that may
bring the system into unsafe state. Also, by changing the issued
command to the actuators, the system may enter into unsafe state.
In fact, the correctness of these transited data plays an important
role in secure functioning of CPSs [22]. Denial of service (DoS) is
another possible attack on sensor readings and control signals. By
performing this attack, the controller will decide according to the
latest received measurement. It has been shown that this attack
may not cause the system to move to a critical state when the
system is in steady state [23]. Indeed, the consequences of DoS
attacks against individual devices are usually restricted.
Despite the similar meanings of attacks in the cyber domain
to the cyber–physical domain, the primary goal of attacks in
the cyber–physical domain is disrupting physical processes.
In summary, attacks in cyber–physical domain have different
consequences and targets [1]:
– Some of attacks may only violate some security attributes and
cannot disrupt physical processes.
– Some of attacks may cause catastrophic damage to system
equipment and assets (as a result of overstress of equipment
and violation of safety limits).
– Some of attacks may target products and lead to production
damages (e.g., the attacks that target product quality, produc-
tion rate and operating costs).
– Some of attacks may endanger human life and safety.
– Some of attacks may cause environmental damage, for instance,
environmental pollution.
It is desirable to evaluate the possible impacts of integrity
attacks against CPSs to rank the system assets based on their
sensitivity to specific attacks. For the purpose of evaluating
and ranking the consequences of attacks against CPSs, a system
dynamic approach will be proposed in Section 4.
3. Related work
In this section, we review some related works on concepts and
approaches for security assessment of CPSs. The authors in [23]
have proposed a method for developing threat models for denial
of service (DoS) and integrity attacks against control systems.
The primary focus of the authors is evaluating the economic and
physical impacts of attacks against these systems. They apply their
method on a chemical reactor system and examine the behavior of
the physical process under min, max, scaling and additive attacks.
Exceeding the shut-down limits is considered as the basis of the
impact evaluation of conducted attacks.
The impact of identifying the optimal time for conducting
attacks against CPSs is investigated in [21]. They primarily focus
on the impacts of DoS attacks on physical processes, when they
are performed at proper time. Their experiments show that to
disrupt a physical process, attackers need to conduct attacks at
proper moment of time. Otherwise, attacks will have limited
consequences on the process. The impacts evaluation of attacks is
performed based on reaching the considered shut-down limits.
Genge et al. [24] address the challenge that how network pa-
rameters such as packet losses, communication delay, control logic
scheduling time and the network background traffic can affect the
consequences of attacks. The main contribution of this article is to
determine the most important network parameters that can affect
the resilience of physical processes. The experimental results have
indicated that the communications parameters (e.g., communica-
tion delay) have a limited impact on the result of the conducted
attacks and the task scheduling parameter can affect the resilience
of physical processes.
The authors in [25] have proposed a method for estimating
the effects of cyber attacks on physical processes. The proposed
method in this paper is based on the behavioral assessment of
physical processes and the sensitivity analysis. The evaluation
method calculates the covariance of observed variables before and
after performing individual attacks against control variables. One
main feature of this method is its applicability in situations where
the physical process is not available. This method has considered
only individual attacks against control signals.
The effect of network segmentation process on the resilience of
CPSs is addressed in [26]. Separating the controllers that control
free-valves from the controllers that control feed-valves is the
primary outcome of this paper. An approach for assessing the
results of attacks against CPSs is proposed by Gollmann et al. [27].
The primary goal of this paper is to demonstrate the situations
that the success of attacks relies on expert knowledge about the
physical process under control. The proposed approach is applied
to a vinyl acetate monomer plant as a case study.
In [28], the authors have proposed a language for describing the
possible attacks against CPSs and their consequences. The main
goal of this language is determining and describing the features
that should be described in expressing attacks and countermea-
sures against CPSs. Although, they have not dealt with the security
evaluation process, they believed that the proposed attack descrip-
tion language can be employed for security assessment targets.
Hu et al. [29] have discussed about the concept and strategies
for building a robust and resilient CPS. They have defined the
resiliency as a 3S-oriented design, that is, stability, security, and
systematicness. They have also explained the CPS modeling issues.
Yoo et al. [30] have also discussed about the vulnerabilities,
security requirements and architecture of CPSs.
Cardenas et al. [2] have investigated the attacks against
control systems, risk assessment problem, detection and response
mechanisms for these systems. The surge attacks, bias attacks and
geometric attacks against control systems are modeled and the
sequential and change detection strategies are used to propose a

nets (GSPNs) to propose a methodology to model the operating
sequences of protections in power systems. They have primarily
focused on modeling the uncertainty in the operation of protection
devices using GSPNs and measuring its impacts on the security
evaluation.
Wei et al. [35] have discussed about potential cyber attacks,
their consequences and defensive requirements for power grids.
Furthermore, they have provided a survey for security challenges
and strategies in power grids.
Ashok et al. [36] have proposed a game-theoretic approach to
evaluate the security of smart grid. They have primarily concen-
trated on the cyber–physical security of wide-area monitoring,
protection and control from a coordinated cyber attack perspec-
tive.
Srivastava et al. [37] have addressed the attack modeling using
the vulnerability of information, communication and electric grid
network. They have analyzed the vulnerability of electric grid
with incomplete information using a graph-theoretic approach.
Furthermore, they have introduced an integrated cyber–physical
vulnerability index and utilized it for real-time modeling to
demonstrate the impact of aurora attack.
Finally, Varshney et al. [38] have proposed the use of parallel
computing for fast and accurate analyzing of integrated security of
power systems. They have applied the proposed method on IEEE
30-bus system and a practical NREB 246-bus Indian power system
as two case studies.
Comparing to the above mentioned researches, in this paper
we propose an approach to estimate direct and indirect impacts
of attacks against CPSs in a more comprehensive manner. In
fact, the primary focus of the reviewed studies is assessing the
impacts of attacks that may bring systems into an unsafe state
(by investigating the effects of attacks on determined parameters).
In contrast, the proposed approach provides the possibility of
investigating the impacts of considered control parameters to each
other and determines the most significant parameters and assets in
the system at a time. By using this information, we can gain a good
insight about the most vulnerable control loops in CPSs.
4. The proposed method
This section describes the proposed method in detail. The
primary goal of this method is providing a modeling approach
for evaluating the impact propagation of attacks in cyber–physical
domain. As described in [27], an attack against CPSs has several
stages. The first stage is access. This stage is very similar to the
traditional IT hacking. In this stage, an adversary by exploiting
the system vulnerabilities and intrusion paths tries to obtain the
required privilege level to disrupt the physical process.
The second stage is discovery. The primary goal of this stage
is discovering the required information about the system. In fact,
in order to disrupt the physical process, attackers need to acquire
some sufficient knowledge about the process behavior and its
failure conditions, control principles and signal processing [21].
Without detailed knowledge, an adversary cannot cause serious
damage to the system, production, or environment.
The third stage is control. Note that in dynamic systems, such
as CPSs, the continuous changes are associated with the values of
process variables according to the laws of physics. In this stage, the
adversary tries to discover the physical dynamics of the system.
By using this process model, an adversary can determine the most
vulnerable areas of the system. It is noteworthy that in CPSs unlike
IT domains, successful intrusion to the system is not same as
disrupting the physical process under control. As stated earlier, in
order to carry out an attack aimed at disrupting a physical process,
attackers need to know the failure conditions and the control
principles of the target system.
The next stage is causing damage to the system. To disrupt
the physical process, an adversary may be faced with several
scenarios. Thus, in order to choose the attractive one, he/she needs
to develop some suitable metrics. Our aim is to propose a metric to
discover the most vulnerable process variables of the system. In the
following, we first describe the formal description of the dynamic
behavior of CPSs and attack process against them. Then, we present
the detailed description of the proposed method.

attacks and evaluating the consequences of cyber attacks against
them. Our primary focus is the second one and our goal is
proposing a metric suitable for studying the direct and indirect
impacts of attacks against CPSs.

4.3. Formal description of the proposed method
In this section, we provide the formal description of the
proposed method. The control parameters in the proposed method
are classified into two categories of cause and effect groups.
The cause group has an impact on the effect group where such
impact is used to estimate the rank of the parameters. The
control parameters such as sensor readings and control signals
are considered as the cause parameters group. For the effect
parameters group, except the control parameters, other important
parameters such as operating costs (based on the characteristics of
the underlying physical process) can be taken into consideration.
By using the proposed approach, we can assess the impacts of
attacks against each parameter on other parameters, and as well,
the impacts received from attacking other parameters. Based on
the considered cause and effect parameters, the proposed method
involves two different cases. The first case is when the cause and
effect parameters are the same. In this case, the direct and indirect
impacts of attacks will be estimated.

5.3. Defensive countermeasures
1. Using intrusion detection and prevention systems and
anomaly detection systems
In order to identify the attacker’s intrusions, CPSs employ
host-based and network-based IDSs. The former is often used on
HMIs, and engineering workstations while the latter is often used
between the control and corporate networks along with a firewall.
It is needed that an intrusion detection system investigates
continuously the presence of any security penetration in sensors,
actuators and control devices [5]. The proposed approach can
identify the most critical components for employing IDSs.
In order to prevent physical disruptions caused by malicious
attacks, anomaly detection systems should be used to identify the
abnormal behavior of the physical process under control [40,41].
ADSs concentrate on detecting the existing deviations from the
normal behavior of the system. Recently, several researches have
been conducted in this context (for instance, [42–44]).
2. Applying network segmentation and implementing demilita-
rized zone
Network segmentation is a suitable technique for achieving a
significant security improvement. The primary goal of applying
network segmentation techniques is to provide a better system
protection. To this end, servers such as data server, data historian
and web servers containing the data from the CPS that needs to
be accessed from the corporate network are put on a separate
segment called demilitarized zone [14]. A DMZ is defined as a
separate network segment that connects directly to the firewalls
that offer three or more interfaces [15].
Two firewalls are utilized in this configuration to establish
a DMZ between the control and corporate networks. The first
firewall is placed between the corporate network and the DMZ
and the second one is located between the DMZ and the control
network. The arbitrary packets originated from the corporate
network to enter into the control network can be blocked by
these firewalls. As a result of this configuration, no direct traffic
is allowed between the control and corporate networks and the
probability of successful external attacks is considerably reduced.
3. Using virtual private networks
Another important issue in CPSs is the remote access of
network services. VPNs extend secure communications across
insecure public networks. VPNs provide security services through
the use of virtual tunneling protocols, dedicated connections,

4. Applying defense-in-depth strategies
In order to provide a more secure CPS, a multiple layer
security strategy called defense-in-depth must be employed [16].
Implementing an effective defense-in-depth strategy in one way
requires a proper understanding of the source of attacks, the
possible attack scenarios and their outcomes, and in the other way
includes the use of DMZs and firewalls, security policies, intrusion
and anomaly detection systems, attack response strategies, and
people training.
Another important strategy in the implementation of defense-
in-depth strategies is deploying the redundant components
(backup-in-depth [16]). By efficient employment of redundant lay-
ers, the attacked system can return to the normal behavior by
masking the attack’s consequence. Depending on the characteris-
tics of their applications, CPSs can be categorized as fail-safe and
fail-operational [13]. In the fail-safe case, the system must be made
to stop functioning to limit the damage that may be caused by

method in comparison with the preceding approaches:
– The proposed approach estimates the direct and indirect
impacts of the attacks against CPSs in a more comprehensive
manner. In fact, the proposed method provides the possibility
of comparing the behavior of systems at the different time
instants, and under different disturbances on different control
parameters.
– The proposed method not only can be used to rank individual
control parameters, but can also be applied to some possible
combinations of them, in contrast to the existing methods
(e.g., [25]) that can only investigate the behavior of systems
