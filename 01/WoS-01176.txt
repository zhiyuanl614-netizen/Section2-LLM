Most sectors of modern society are currently undergoing rapid digitalization. With the 
progress of this development IT networks are becoming an ever more crucial asset whose 
failure are prone to cause economic losses and risks to health and safety of the population. 
Due to this development, it is increasingly important that IT networks are designed to be 
resilient, meaning that they either maintain or quickly recover their functionality when ex- 
posed to strains of various kinds. Simulation-based methods that consider supply network 
topology as well as system for repairing the supply network have previously been applied 
and found to be useful for assessment of resilience of electricity and water networks. A 
method of this kind is here applied for IT networks. The method, however, considers only 
hardware and operator fault modes, not software and dependency faults. The IT networks 
are studied as socio-technical systems, here broadly understood as systems whose func- 
tionality depend on technical as well as organizational sub-systems. The aim of the present 
paper is to investigate if the method is applicable for assessment of resilience of large-scale 
IT networks that are critical to society, meaning that (1) it is possible to apply for such sys- 
tems based on available data, in this case gathered mainly through interviews with focus 
groups, (2) the results are relevant for users/maintainers/owners of these systems and (3) 
it captures all relevant factors. The method is tested in case studies on the IT network of 
a municipality and the SCADA system of a wastewater network. Follow-up interviews were 
conducted with system managers to assess the perceived relevance of the method and re- 
sults. It is concluded that the method can be used to obtain the resilience metrics sought for 
and that the method enables system owners to see if and for what levels of strain they are 
presently reaching their desired targets concerning system resilience. Follow-up interviews 
also indicate that the method is perceived by the system managers as being able to support 
decision making concerning improvement in system resilience.

1. 
Introduction 
Most sectors of modern society are developing an increasing 
dependence on IT networks. As this development progresses 
it becomes increasingly important that IT networks are de- 
signed to be resilient, meaning that they can either maintain 
or quickly recover functionality when exposed to strains of 
various kinds. This calls for new and better means of assess- 
ing IT network resilience. Simulation based methods in which 
network topology and system responsible for network repair 
are both explicitly considered have previously been used and 
found to be advantageous for assessing resilience of elec- 
tricity [1] and water distribution systems [2] . In these cases, 
the infrastructure system is studied as a socio-technical sys- 
tem, here broadly understood as a system whose functionality 
depends on technical as well as organizational sub-systems. 
The potential gain of such an approach is that both technical 
and non-technical factors can be evaluated concerning their 
impact on overall system resilience. In a preliminary study, 
the approach has been applied for parts of the IT network 
of a university [3] and it was found that it could be used to 
assess resilience of such a small-scale IT network. The aim 
of the present paper is to test if the method is applicable 
for assessment of resilience of large-scale IT networks which 
are critical to society, meaning that (1) it is possible to use 
for such systems based on available data, in this case gath- 
ered mainly through interviews with focus groups, (2) the re- 
sults are relevant for users/maintainers/owners and (3) it cap- 
tures all relevant factors. The method, however, considers only 
hardware and operator fault modes, not software and depen- 
dency faults. 
Resilience is here broadly understood as the ability of a sys- 
tem to withstand sudden chocks [4] . Many metrics have been 
suggested for quantitative resilience assessment of engineer- 
ing systems; see e.g. ( [5,6] ). McDaniels et al. [7] suggest that 
resilient systems are robust and/or recover their functionality 
quickly, which is also referred to as rapidity. Based on [8] , ro- 
bustness ( X ) and rapidity ( T ) are formally defined as follows:

initial and end states of the restoration process, while inter- 
mediate states do not affect the result. This shows that other 
metrics besides robustness and rapidity are needed for en- 
abling a more precise assessment of actual system resilience.

quantifies the overall resilience behaviour over a longer time 
period which may include multiple disturbances. The main 
advantage of the AR metric, compared to RL is that it does not 
only consider the consequences once a disturbance occurs but 
also considers the probability of disturbances. 
In all decision making concerning what level of resilience 
to strive for monetary considerations are likely to be decisive. 
Vugrin et al. [12] suggest three metrics which can be of use 
in this context. System impact (SI) is the cumulative conse- 
quences resulting from an outage. The second metric is to- 
tal recovery effort (TRE) which is the cumulative costs of re- 
sources expended during the recovery process. Finally, the re- 
covery dependent resilience (RDR) index is proposed for as- 
sessing overall system resilience. It sums up SI an TRE and 
normalizes through division with overall revenue. There are 
also resilience metrics that are based on network theory met- 
rics. Wang et al. [13] propose a metric based on the change 
in ratio of the closeness centrality of the network before and 
after disturbance. System resilience can also be categorized 
into several sub-tasks that must be carried out. Wang et al. 
[14] suggest a resilience metric which is based on the relative 
completion times, demands for and weights of all such tasks. 
Some metrics also assess which system components that are

more, no analysis is performed concerning what network sub- 
components that contribute most to lack in resilience along 
the lines suggested by Barker et al. [15] . 
2. 
Related work 
When it comes to IT systems and risk analysis it was found 
that a majority of the methods are qualitative and evaluated 
for IT systems in general, without considering any specific 
field. Risk analysis is a general approach of evaluating an IT 
system’s ability to function and provide and process infor- 
mation in different situations. The application of risk anal- 
ysis for analysis of resilience of communication systems is 
investigated by Schöller et al. [16] who proposes a method 
for collecting qualitative data e.g. from focus groups. The ap- 
proach results in a list of challenges that engineers can use 
when designing a network. However, quantitative approaches 
have also been used for assessing IT system risks as is demon- 
strated in a review by Stergiopoulos et al. [17] . There are also 
a number of research attempts to investigate resilience on a 
more technical and general level. In this paper graph models 
are used for representing the IT network and this approach 
is frequently used for resilience analysis of IT systems (e.g. 
[18–23] ). Some research has been conducted on identifying 
and solving resilience problems as they happen during us- 
age of IT and computer networks (e.g. [24–28] ). [24] present 
a cyber physical testbed for making assessments of the ef- 
fects of cyber-attacks. The platform can be used to study a 
wide range of disruptions such as host and link failures, dis- 
tributed denial of service attacks, and integrity attacks. Sou- 
pionis and Benoist [25] present a logic-based framework to 
evaluate the resilience of computer networks in the face of 
incidents, i.e., attacks from malicious intruders as well as ran- 
dom faults. The model uses a two-layered presentation of de- 
pendencies between files and services, and of timed games to 
represent not just incidents, but also the dynamic responses 
from administrators and their respective delays. Cetinkaya et 
al. [26] present a framework to evaluate network dependabil- 
ity and performability in the face of challenges. Simulation 
is used for analysis of the effects of perturbations to normal 
operation of the networks. The framework can simulate both 
static and dynamic challenges, based on the failure of indi- 
vidual components, as well as modelling geographically cor- 
related failures. The framework is demonstrated for a real as 
well as for synthetically generated topologies. It is shown how 
packet delivery ratio drops with number of component fail- 
ures, number of failures ranging from 1-10. Malicious and non- 
malicious node perturbations are considered. [27] propose an 
approach to proactively work with “resilience management”
in computer networks in order, for example, to consider mali- 
cious threats. Smith et al. [28] focus on network management 
with respect to resilience to improve the network when nec- 
essary. That is, there is available approaches and presented 
research focusing on planning and monitoring of networks 
with respect to resilience. To our knowledge there is not as 
much research presented on analysing IT networks, which is 
an example of a socio-technical system, with respect to re- 
silience and time behaviour. In particular, we have found no 
example of repair system resources being explicitly consid- 
ered in IT network restoration simulations. Explicitly consid- 
ering repair system resources is desirable since the method 
can then be used to assess resilience performance given dif- 
ferent hypothetical levels of resources. This type of analy- 
sis may provide a basis for decision making concerning di- 
mensioning of repair system resources. Also, there is little re- 
search regarding high levels of network strain, Cetinkaya et 
al. [26] being a noteworthy exception. In this research the net- 
works, including their recovery times, are modelled in Monte 
Carlo simulations. In distinction to many of the above men- 
tioned approaches the here suggested framework does not 
consider probabilities of failure events. The reason for this 
is that the model is intended to be useful for assessment 
of large disturbance events. For these events there is little 
failure data on which component failure probabilities can be 
based. 
3. 
Research methodology 
The research is conducted through proposing a simulation 
approach based on already an already available method pro- 
posed by Landegren et al. [1] and using it in two cases. I.e. the 
research can be classified as a multiple case study with two 
cases in different contexts [29] . 
The overall objectives of the research are to investigate if 
the approach is possible to apply to the area of IT networks, 
and to investigate to what extent the results are perceived as 
relevant by experts in the area. These two objectives were in- 
vestigated in the following steps. 
First the two cases were decided based on previous con- 
tacts with the areas. It was seen as positive with two differ- 
ent cases since it provides opportunities for comparison, and 
also gives more than one view on the applicability. During the 
initial contacts with the cases it was described that a first at- 
tempt was already conducted for a university IT network with 
positive results [3] . 
Representatives from each of the two cases were inter- 
viewed in focus group settings in order to identify important 
questions for the cases and to obtain information about the 
IT infrastructures. In both cases a subset of the authors met 
with 2,3 experts, where the objectives of the research were 
presented and the simulation approach was described in more 
detail. After that a set of questions about the infrastructure, 
the organization, and their objectives were discussed. Based

on the data from the interviews, the infrastructures were

When the modelling was carried out the results were pre-

The objectives of these meetings were to present the results to

There are a number of measures that can be taken in or- 
der to improve the validity of a case study, e.g. [27] , which 
are reflected upon here. First of all, triangulation can be ap- 
plied in order to obtain more than one source of informa- 
tion. In this study interview data was combined with docu- 
ments provided by the participants, and the basic design as 
a multiple case study instead of a single study with only one 
case. Another approach is prolonged involvement which means 
that the researcher has a long-going relation with the organi- 
zation. In this study we identified the organizations through 
mutual contacts, so there has been no long-term collabora- 
tion before. However, the research lasted for several months 
and we believe that we have been able to understand the 
organizations’ IT infrastructures as well as their organiza- 
tions goals and objectives. Peer debriefing means that the re- 
sults are reviewed by fellow researchers. We believe that we 
have achieved this through involving researchers from moth 
the fields of IT management and infrastructure simulation, 
as well as involving IT experts who are working in the area 
in their day-to-day business. Member checking means that ex- 
perts from the investigated cases are consulted in the re- 
view of the data and the result. This was one of the rea- 
sons that the second interview round was conducted. In ad- 
dition to this an audit trail was achieved through electronic 
recording of the interviews with text transcripts, and by study- 
ing and discussing alternative cases, i.e. negative cases in the 
analysis.

Nodes are categorized into source, transfer and load nodes, 
where the first are supplying a service to the network, the sec- 
ond are transmitting the service and the third are consuming 
the service. In the IT network context source nodes are of two 
main types, servers and internet access points, transfer nodes 
may be routers, firewalls or distribution switches and load 
nodes are access switches. Using the network graph, the frac- 
tion of load nodes that are connected to one or more source 
nodes can be evaluated. The graph model is used for simulat- 
ing component faults. Two kinds of node faults are simulated, 
hardware and operator faults. Some components have redun- 
dancy. Therefore, in case of a single hardware failure there will 
be a failover so that component functionality is either main- 
tained or only reduced to some extent. Remaining faults, i.e. 
operator failure or two simultaneous hardware failures, mean 
that the network component is effectively removed from the 
network. Node faults are represented by means of two vectors, 
V 1 and V 2. V 1 is a Boolean vector in which the i th element 
is 1 if the i th node, specified in Eq. (4) , has had a failure. V 2 
specifies the number of intact sub-components in the network 
nodes. Some nodes have several sub-components, and may 
then have one hardware fault without complete loss of func- 
tion. A node i will be completely without function if the i th el- 
ement in V 1 is 1 and the i th element in V 2 is either 0 (in which 
case all sub-components have failed) or its original value (in 
which case the component has had an operator failure). For 
other cases, the component function will be either un-affected 
or decreased only by some degree which varies depending on 
the studied system. Concerning edges, a failure of edge e i con- 
necting n j and n k is simulated by setting a jk and a kj to 0. Capac- 
ity is not considered in the network model. A user is therefore 
considered to be supplied with a given service (e.g. internet 
access) if there is at least one unbroken path leading from the 
access switch at which the customer is located to at least one 
source node supplying the service. A breadth first search strat- 
egy is used to find all supplied nodes. The prioritization of re- 
pair jobs is done to maximize the number of demand nodes 
that can be reached through one or more paths from a source 
node. This can be illustrated by a simple example. If two re- 
pair jobs, A and B, require the same amount of work time but 
completion of job A will lead to 10 more nodes being reach- 
able from source nodes and completion of job B will lead to 
only 5 more nodes being reachable from source nodes, then 
job A will have more utility and will be placed before job B in 
the queue of repair jobs. To find out the utility of each repair 
job all possible single repairs are tested, and the number of 
connected nodes is calculated for each such repair scenario, 
using a breadth first search strategy. The calculation of utility 
of repair jobs is formally described in Eqs. (8) and ( 9 ).

p i denotes the probability that the next component failure will

5. 
Case studies 
The method for studying resilience is tested in case studies on 
a municipal IT network and the SCADA system of a wastewa- 
ter network shown, respectively above and below, in Fig. 3 . In 
the graphs I = internet access point, S = server, FW = firewall, 
SW = switch, M = modem. In the upper graph, all nodes in the 
access network are switches. Data was collected through in- 
terviews with focus groups and through document analysis, 
and the results were assessed for relevance through inter- 
views. Below the two case study systems are described in more 
detail under separate headings. The collected data and model 
parameterization is then described. 
5.1. 
Municipal IT network 
The municipal IT network (see Fig. 3 , upper) serves approxi- 
mately 27 700 IP-addresses. In agreement with the system per- 
sonnel, it was decided that only a minority of the access net- 
work would be explicitly included in the network model. Five 
networks in the access edge were selected for inclusion in the 
network model: three schools, the city library and a nursing 
home for disabled. In Fig. 3 , above, in the access, from left to 
right, networks 1, 2 and 4 are schools, network 3 is a nursing 
home for the disabled and network 5 is the city library. In total, 
the network model then encompasses 58 nodes (servers, inter- 
net access point and switches) and 64 edges (cables above and 
below ground). Each of the 49 access switches in the model is 
assumed to connect to 54 IP-addresses. In other words, 2646, 
or 9.8%, of the IP-addresses are connected to access switches 
included in the network model. Remaining IP-addresses are, 
in the model, associated only with a core switch or connect

5.2. 
SCADA system of wastewater network 
The SCADA system (see Fig. 3 , lower) is connected to 200 
sub-stations (pumping stations and treatment plants). Among 
the 200 stations 8 are manned stations and connect to the 
server through underground fiber while remaining stations

5.3. 
Data collection and model parametrization 
Interviews were carried out with system experts to collect in- 
formation about fault modes, their relative probabilities, re- 
pair times and resources needed for repair. 
Fault modes 
Four main fault categories were identified: hardware, operator, 
software and dependency fault. The last category includes pri- 
marily faults due to loss of power supply or cooling. It was de- 
cided that the latter two fault categories would not be consid- 
ered in the analysis. Software faults are not considered due to 
the irregular consequences and repair times associated with 
this fault category. Dependency faults are not considered since 
this would require model development which is believed to 
be too much outside the scope of the present work, e.g. sim- 
ulation of power outage durations and geographical scale as 
well as collection of data concerning heat resistance of var- 
ious components and the speed of temperature increase in 
absence of cooling. The software and dependency fault cat- 
egories are not believed to be less relevant to consider than 
hardware and operator faults, but they are believed to require 
other methods for their analysis than the present approach. 
Information about the identified fault modes are shown in 
Table 1 . Failure in wifi connections in the municipal IT network 
is not considered, also concerning the SCADA system failures 
in the server are not considered, since the server is virtual. All 
nodes in the core networks have fail-over, meaning that single 
hardware failure will not cause complete loss of function. In 
the municipal IT network such faults are, based on assump- 
tions by the respondents, assumed to cause outage for 1/2 of 
the users connected to the component, while in the SCADA- 
network a single hardware fault will not bring about any 
consequence whatsoever. Two simultaneous hardware faults 
or operator faults will cause complete loss of component 
function.

5.4. 
Scenario selection and convergence 
The analysis is performed for six levels of network strain: N-1, 
N-2, N-3, N-6, N-9 and N-12, where N-k denotes the failure of 
k out of the total N network components. For the municipal 
IT network, the analysis was performed with respect to two 
different network services, internet access and server access 
while for the SCADA-network it was only performed with re- 
spect to server access since the system is not connected to the 
internet. In this paper, for reasons of brevity, only results con- 
cerning server connectivity are presented. The system man- 
agers of the municipal IT network assessed that probability of 
failure in the core network is 1.5% and probability of failure in 
the access network is 98.5%. This large difference mainly re- 
flects the fact that the access network consists of many more 
components than the core network. Consequently, in the sam- 
pling of failure scenarios two component sets were distin- 
guished, one containing all components in the core network, 
and one containing all the access network components that 
are explicitly included in the model. The probabilities spec- 
ified in Eq. (11) of failure in core and access networks were 
set in agreement with the expert assessment. Concerning the 
SCADA system all component failures were sampled with the 
same probability. This is in line with the vulnerability ap- 
proach used here. Since fault scenarios as well as fault modes 
and repair times are stochastic variables many samples must 
be simulated to obtain a convergent result. 15 000 samples are 
used for analysis of all levels of strain. To generate the results 
in this paper then requires a total simulation time of about 
10 hours (simulations were run on a computer with AMD Phe- 
nom(tm) II X4 945 Processor with 4 cores, clock speed 3.00 GHz 
and 4 GB memory). The so-called coefficient of variation, β, is

6. 
Result 
In this section results from the case studies are first reported. 
This is followed by a description of the response from the sys- 
tem experts that was obtained in follow up interview sessions. 
6.1. 
Application of model 
In the following three different systems are investigated, the 
municipal IT network, the SCADA system given that repairers 
are working according to the present contract and the SCADA 
system given that all repairers are working based on a 24/7 
agreement. These are respectively referred to as a), b) and c) 
for brevity. In Fig. 4 robustness and rapidity of all simulated 
scenarios are shown (in all there are 270 000 scenarios since 
15 000 samples is used for each of the six levels of strain and 
the three systems). Robustness, which is measured on the ver- 
tical axis, is normalized by division with F norm to simplify com- 
parisons between systems. We see that most of the scenarios 
are associated with high robustness. This is so to a particularly 
high extent for the SCADA system, cases b) and c), for which 
most of the scenarios are associated with robustness values 
above 0.9, meaning that less than 10% of the sub-stations are 
affected by the outage. For the municipal IT network, case a), 
most of the scenarios are associated with robustness values 
above 0.7, meaning that less than 30% of the users are af- 
fected by the outage. We can see that for the SCADA system 
there is also a significant number of scenarios for which ro- 
bustness is very low or zero, meaning that all, or almost all 
sub-stations are affected by the outage. For the municipal IT 
network such scenarios are on the other hand almost non- 
existent. The main reason for this difference is that 96% of 
the sub-stations in the SCADA system are connected to the 
server through internet, and are dependent on the same fire- 
wall. A single failure is therefore sufficient to cause an outage 
for almost all sub-stations. Concerning the rapidity, measured 
on the horizontal axis we see that for the municipal IT net- 
work recovery occurs after about 30 h in the worst cases, while 
for the SCADA system recovery occurs after about 50 h in the 
worst cases. 
In Fig. 5 we see the development of the median, mean, 
5%- and 95%-percentiles of the system functionality over time 
given three different levels of strain. The median and mean 
values give an indication of the expected outcome in case of 
a disruption while 5%- and 95%-percentiles show the variabil- 
ity in the outcome. 90% of the scenarios are in the area be- 
tween the two percentiles. We see that for cases a) and c) the 
percentiles are very close together, showing that there is not 
much variability in the result, while for case b) there is a high 
degree of variability. Furthermore, we see that with increase in 
strain level the initial level of functionality, indicating system 
robustness, deteriorates, while the speed of recovery, or rapid- 
ity, is not that much affected. We see that case b) is perform- 
ing significantly worse than case a) and c) concerning median 
and mean outcome. The median recovery time in case b) is 
about twice of that in cases a) and c). Also, the 5%-percentile 
is dramatically worse in case b) compared to cases a) and c). 
For instance, at the N-6 level of strain the recovery time for the 
5% percentile is about 5 hours in cases a) and c) while for case 
b) it is instead 40 hours.

supplied service, as a function of level of strain. Resilience

6.2. 
Feedback from IT system personnel 
Follow up interview sessions were held with system experts to 
get feedback on the results. During these sessions results in- 
cluded in this paper were presented along with additional re- 
sults which, for reasons of brevity, have been omitted from the 
paper. The latter results concerned the change in robustness 
and rapidity with level of strain, the distribution of resilience 
loss over simulated scenarios and the relative contribution to 
overall resilience loss of components in the network. Follow- 
ing the presentation questions were posed to the system ex- 
perts concerning the following main topics: 
1. are results experienced as being valid/trustworthy; 
2. are results experienced as being useful; 
3. to what extent can results contribute to improvement of 
system resilience; 
4. what are the possibilities for improving the method; 
5. what are the possibilities for applying the method for other 
types of systems. 
Below the responses given by the system experts are re- 
ported individually for each of these five topics. 
6.2.1. 
Trustworthiness 
Municipality The respondents think that it is hard to tell to 
what extent the results are trustworthy. They have experi- 
enced some of the simple cases and they know that the model 
agrees with their experience on this point. They can only sup- 
pose that the results are correct also when many simultane- 
ous failures are simulated. Both respondents agree that based 
on their experience the results are reasonable. 
SCADA system Results are experienced as trustworthy. The 
respondents suggest that trustworthiness would be further 
improved if a probability aspect was considered in the model. 
In the results single component failures as well as large num- 
ber of simultaneous component failures are investigated but 
there are no results concerning the probabilities of scenarios. 
6.2.2. 
Usefulness 
Municipality The respondents think that the model provides 
information that is of interest but they think that the inter- 
face by which this information is accessed could be improved. 
One respondent suggests that it should be possible to click on 
scenario points and thereby see which component faults that 
are involved and other such scenario information. The respon- 
dents think that results concerning single component failures 
are most useful. In some cases when a risk assessment is to be 
made, two or three simultaneous component failures should 
be considered. Assessment of higher levels of strain is not ex- 
perienced to be useful by the respondents. 
The respondents think that it would be useful if the simu- 
lation tool could be developed so that it could be used by the IT 
managers or personnel themselves. They would then use it to 
experiment with system parameters and see how the param- 
eters influence system resilience. The respondents agree that 
it would be of interest to use the simulation tool for assess- 
ing how service level agreements as well as network topology 
influence system resilience. However, to make the tool use- 
ful it should be an add-in to an existing software used for IT 
network design so that network schematics will not have to 
be remade. The respondents see a use for the tool in demon- 
strating the benefit of system investments. Such results can 
also make it possible to get necessary increases in budget. 
SCADA system The respondents experience that the results 
are useful. They point to two uses of the presented results. 
The results make the system owner aware that having a 24/7 
contract concerning repair and maintenance with their inter- 
net supplier would increase system resilience dramatically. At 
present the organization is considering renegotiating the con- 
tract with the internet supplier so that 24/7 service will be in- 
cluded. This will bring additional cost on the organization and 
the respondents say that they will use the presented results in 
this context. Conversely, the respondents also pointed out that 
the results will be useful for service suppliers. When a 24/7 
service is set up it creates a large increase in cost. To make 
such an investment profitable many 24/7 contracts should be 
signed. The presented results could be useful from such a per- 
spective since the results show the benefit that a 24/7 contract 
can bring in terms of increases in system resilience. The re- 
spondents also experience that the simulation model makes 
it possible to find out what system improvement options that 
are preferable. In particular, they become aware that a major 
weakness, in terms of resilience is long response times. They 
conclude that their weakness lies on the organizational rather 
than on the technical side. 
6.2.3. 
Ability to increase system resilience 
Municipality Both respondents think that the approach can 
contribute to increased system resilience. 
SCADA system The respondents experience that the results 
have opened their eyes to an issue whose importance they 
have not fully realized previously. They express that they are 
now more aware of where to focus their resilience improve- 
ment efforts. The most attractive possibility according to the 
respondents is that the simulation tool can enable experimen- 
tation with system parameters. 
6.2.4. 
Improvement possibilities 
Municipality The respondents suggest that to get the full ben- 
efit of the simulation tool it should tell them if they invest 
too little in system resilience or too much and where money

7. 
Discussion 
Simulation based methods that explicitly consider technical 
network as well as repair system have previously been used 
for assessing resilience of electricity and water distribution 
systems. Such an approach has also been applied for a small- 
scale IT network as a test case for the present study. The 
objective of the present paper is to test if such a method 
(1) is applicable for large-scale IT networks that are criti- 
cal to society, (2) give results that are relevant to system 
users/maintainers/owners, 3) captures all relevant factors. Be- 
low these three questions are discussed. 
7.1. 
Applicability 
Concerning objective (1) results indicate that the method 
is applicable for the studied IT networks and that we 
can obtain the resilience metrics sought for. Results show 
that disturbance scenarios for which resilience is low can 
be identified based on three crucial resilience indicators: 
rapidity, robustness and resilience loss. Results of this 
type may be useful in the process of increasing system 
resilience. 
Results showing system functionality over time enables 
system operators to predict what the likely consequences will 
be in case of disruptions of various magnitudes. Results show- 
ing how resilience loss changes with level of strain make it 
possible for system owners/operators to see if and for what 
levels of strain that they are presently reaching their desired 
targets concerning system resilience. 
The fact that three systems were compared illustrates an 
attractive possibility, namely that IT network operators can 
benchmark IT networks against each other. The reasons for 
possible differences in resilience performance can then be fur- 
ther analysed which can create opportunity for learning and 
development on all sides. In the studied case, we find that the 
longest outages of the SCADA system are significantly longer 
than those of the municipality, about 50 h compared to 30 h. 
Also, for the SCADA system several scenarios are associated 
with a robustness close to 0 while this is not the case for the 
municipality. This indicates that there could be things to be 
learnt from the municipal IT network on the side of the SCADA 
system operators regarding how resilience performance can 
be improved. The comparison that was made between the 
SCADA system with and without 24/7 agreement for the au- 
tomation technicians and support team internet illustrates 
a further attractive possibility, namely that system operators 
can experiment with system parameters to explore their im- 
pact on overall system resilience. In this case a 24/7 agreement 
was found to have a dramatic impact on system resilience per- 
formance. 
7.2. 
Relevance 
Concerning objective 2) feed-back from system experts 
showed that the method is experienced as being able to help in 
the improvement of resilience of the IT networks. The method 
therefore is relevant. However, respondents of the municipal 
IT network believe that only analysis of lower levels of strain, 
N-1, N-2 and N-3 is practically useful. System experts of both 
case study systems agree in desiring a probability aspect in 
the result, i.e. not only what the consequences will be once 
a scenario occurs but also how frequently it will occur. They 
also agree in desiring to be able to use the tool on their own 
to explore the impact on resilience of various system parame- 
ters. It appears that the developed method can be potentially 
useful in the wider context of risk management. Pinheiro et 
al. [33] make a distinction between seven main ways in which 
risks can be dealt with: 
1. Avoiding the risk by deciding not to start or continue with 
the activity that gives rise to the risk. 
2. Accepting or increasing the risk in order to pursue an op- 
portunity. 
3. Removing the risk source. 
4. Changing the likelihood. 
5. Changing the consequences. 
6. Sharing the risk with another party or parties (including 
contracts and risk financing). 
7. Retaining the risk by informed decision. 
The method appears to be potentially useful for several of 
these ways of dealing with risks. Concerning 3. and 4. The 
method can show that certain types of faults correspond to 
a large part of the overall degradation in resilience which may 
lead to a decision to remove risk sources that can result in 
these types of faults or at least change their likelihood of oc- 
curring. Concerning 5. Change in consequences is likely to 
result from the application of the method since the system 
can be reworked so that resilience is improved meaning that 

===== PAGE 13 =====
124 
international journal of critical infrastructure protection 23 (2018) 112–125 
consequences of disruptions are overall smaller. The method 
is likely to give contributions also concerning aspect 7. Since 
many of the identified scenarios with large consequences may 
be found to be too unlikely to be worth counteracting in any 
way. The advantage of the method is in this case that a con- 
scious decision to accept certain scenarios can be made. 
7.3. 
Model completeness 
Concerning objective 3) software and dependency faults are 
not considered in the model. In the follow up interview ses- 
sion it became clear that an interest of the system experts is 
to find out where the system requires further investments and 
where, perhaps, too much investments have been made al- 
ready. For the method to fully support such an assessment it 
would have to include dependency faults in some form, and 
possibly software faults as well. An alternative means of get- 
ting the overview necessary for supporting decision making 
concerning investments is to use the here presented method 
in combination with methods which are suitable for consid- 
ering software and dependency faults. A further interest of 
the system experts is to explore if system investments are ap- 
propriate, or if over/under investments have been made. To 
fully support such an analysis, the model should be developed 
so that cost of various resources is included as well. This in- 
cludes cost of network components as well as repair system 
resources. Once this is achieved it will be possible to design 
systems in the computer environment and to get information 
about their resilience performance as well as price. The two 
topics of expanding the model to include other fault modes 
and to consider price of network and repair resources should 
be dealt with in future research. 
8. 
Conclusions 
In this paper a previously developed simulation based method 
is applied for assessing resilience of two large-scale IT net- 
works. Based on the results from the study as well as feed 
backs from the system operators it is concluded that the 
method is applicable for resilience assessment of large-scale 
IT networks. Resilience assessment is however only done with 
respect to hardware and operator failures, not with respect to 
software and dependency failures which are concluded to re- 
quire other methods for their assessment. The method can 
enable system owners to see if and for what levels of strain 
that they are presently reaching their desired target concern- 
ing three crucial resilience metrics: robustness, rapidity and 
resilience loss. The method can support benchmarking of re- 
silience between IT networks as well as experimentation with 
system parameters to explore the impact of parameters on 
system resilience. Interviews with system experts show that 
the method is experienced as providing results that can en- 
able improvement of system resilience. In future research, it 
will be of interest to find ways of taking software and depen- 
dency faults into account. Alternatively, the here presented 
approach can be used in combination with methods which are 
suitable for considering software and dependency faults. Also, 
it can be of interest to consider cost of resource in the model, 
which could enable optimization of the repair system given a 
limited budget.
