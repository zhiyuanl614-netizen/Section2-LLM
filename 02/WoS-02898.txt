Abstract—Compared
to
other
energy-intensive
industries,
urban water supply systems are unique due to the strong
temporal coupling with the power grid, which intensiﬁes grid
stress during peak hours. This paper addresses the water-
energy nexus by developing a water-energy resource scheduling
model for whole-process water supply that incorporates elec-
tricity cost prediction uncertainties. Meanwhile, to minimize
the scheduling cost in an end-to-end approach, we propose
the
Stochastic
Predict-then-Optimize
(SPTO),
which
aligns
prediction distribution with the optimality gap by integrating
probabilistic forecasting with stochastic optimization. Unlike
traditional two-stage approaches, SPTO establishes a closed-
loop feedback between forecasting and optimization, reducing
cumulative errors and improving real-time adaptability. To
enable end-to-end training, we derive the SPTO+, a differentiable
convex upper-bound surrogate function based on Lagrangian
duality, which provides surrogate gradient computation for end-
to-end training. Experiments on real-world systems demonstrate
that SPTO signiﬁcantly reduces operational cost and decision
regret, effectively coordinating water operations with dynamic
grid conditions to support cost-efﬁcient and grid-responsive
scheduling.

I. INTRODUCTION
M
ANAGING peak demand is a critical concern in mod-
ern smart grid operations [1], [2]. As the electriﬁcation
of infrastructure grows, power grids must increasingly coordi-
nate with large, ﬂexible, and often uncertain energy loads [3].
Among these, the urban water supply system emerges as
a signiﬁcant and controllable electricity consumer [4], [5].
According to national estimates, water-related infrastructure
consumes approximately 7–8% of total electricity in the
United States [6]. In speciﬁc urban regions, water pumping
alone can account for up to 30% of local utility energy
consumption [7], providing potential for demand-side ﬂexibil-
ity. More importantly, this load is highly time-concentrated.
Studies report that during peak electricity periods—typically
aligned with high-price hours—the contribution of water
systems to total grid load can reach 15–20% [8].
This temporal coupling intensiﬁes core operational chal-
lenges in smart grids, such as maintaining voltage stability,
managing frequency deviations, and ensuring sufﬁcient ramp-
ing capacity, especially under high renewable penetration [9].
Without coordination, this leads to greater reliance on
expensive peaking generators and higher system risk dur-
ing renewable ﬂuctuations [10], [11]. Therefore, coordinated
scheduling of water systems is essential to provide ﬂexible
load shifting that supports grid stability, with the extra beneﬁt
of reducing electricity costs. However, such coordination must
respect hydraulic constraints and process delays within the
water system, making joint optimization both valuable and
challenging [12].
While co-optimization models aim to shift pumping away
from expensive periods, most of them assume full knowledge
of future prices, which is a strong assumption that contradicts
the volatility and price unpredictability of real-world power
markets [13], [14], [15]. This gap undermines their practical
effectiveness in real-world smart grids, where electricity prices
are inﬂuenced by stochastic factors such as renewable variabil-
ity, demand spikes, and market bidding behavior [16], [17].
One standard approach to handle such uncertainty is the
two-stage predict and optimize framework [16], [18], [19].
It uses a forecasting model to predict electricity prices and
then solves an optimization problem with those predicted
values. This modular design is easy to implement, but suf-
fers from a fundamental misalignment. Traditional prediction
losses, such as mean absolute error (MAE) or negative log-
likelihood (NLL), measure forecast accuracy in isolation and
do not reﬂect the impact of forecast errors on scheduling
quality.
However, in grid-interactive infrastructure systems, the
effectiveness of forecasts is not only judged by statisti-
cal metrics, but also by their contribution to operational
decisions [20]. This has led to the emergence of Predic-
then-Optimize (PTO) methods [21], which combine prediction
with optimization through regret-based training losses. The
PTO ensures that the forecasting model improves outcomes
that matter directly to the optimization problem. However,
existing PTO methods face two key limitations when applied
to realistic energy systems.
First, the regret function is often non-differentiable with
respect to the predicted inputs [22], [23], especially in problems
with discrete decisions or piecewise objectives. This prevents
direct gradient computation and requires the use of surrogate
loss functions or differentiable optimization layers [24], [25],
[26], [27]. Second, and more critically, most PTO approaches
rely on point predictions of uncertain parameters. That is, they
train the forecasting model to output a single best-estimate price
and then optimize accordingly. This ignores the distributional
uncertainty inherent in electricity price forecasts, which can be
large and asymmetric, especially during price spikes or load
changes. Inability to model distributional uncertainty, leading
to high rates of regret, especially during rare but critical events
such as periods of grid stress or market volatility [28], [29].
This paper presents a decision-aware optimization frame-
work for water-energy scheduling under cost uncertainty in
smart grids. Urban water systems represent a major con-
trollable load, and their pumping operations often coincide
with periods of high electricity demand. To reduce peak grid
stress, this work extends the Predict-Then-Optimize (PTO)
framework to a stochastic setting, where uncertain electricity
prices are modeled as probability distributions. The main
contributions are summarized as follows:
1) A whole-process water-energy scheduling model is
developed that satisﬁes hydraulic constraints and lever-
ages electricity price responsiveness for demand-side
ﬂexibility in smart grids. This formulation enables
pump scheduling to adapt to electricity price variations,
contributing to peak load reduction and demand-side
ﬂexibility in the smart grid.
2) A learning-based optimization framework is intro-
duced,
where
electricity
prices
are
predicted
as
probability distributions rather than point estimates.
These
forecasts
are
directly
embedded
into
the
optimization problem through a decision-focused loss.
The
proposed
framework
improves
the
alignment
between prediction and scheduling under uncertain mar-
ket conditions, enhancing the robustness of demand-side
operations.
3) To address the non-differentiability of regret-based
objectives, a smooth surrogate loss is derived. This
formulation enables
gradient-based
training of the
forecasting model using decision-aware signals. The sur-
rogate maintains ﬁdelity to the original regret objective
while supporting efﬁcient model optimization.
4) The proposed framework is evaluated under a real-world
water-energy scheduling scenario. Results show that the
proposed method achieves lower decision regret and
improved cost performance compared to deterministic
and two-stage baselines. These ﬁndings support the
integration of data-driven decision models into grid-
interactive infrastructure systems.

II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
This study aims to address the coupled peak demand
problem in water systems and electric power grids. Urban
water systems consume signiﬁcant energy for pumping and
distribution, which can intensify power grid loads during
peak electricity demand periods. The study area is a speciﬁc
urban water supply district H in a mega Asian city, covering
approximately 20 km2 and serving around 500,000 residents.
Its water distribution network (WDN) includes thousands of
nodes and pipes, along with a set of essential production and
delivery facilities, such as pumping stations, reservoirs, and
treatment tanks. Electricity costs for operating these water
pumps account for a signiﬁcant part of the system’s total
operational expenses.

we formulate a cost-aware water-energy scheduling problem

III. INTEGRATED WATER-ENERGY SCHEDULING MODEL
This section presents the whole-process model of the urban
water-energy co-optimization system, emphasizing the inte-
gration of energy-aware pumping decisions and hydraulically
feasible water production and distribution processes. The
model consists of two coupled subsystems: the energy side,
which focuses on electricity cost-aware scheduling of pumps;
and the water side, which captures the ﬂow balance, pressure
dynamics, and process delays across the entire treatment and
supply pipeline.
A. Energy-Side: Energy and Price Costs Minimization
In water supply systems, even a single suburban district
typically operates dozens of pumps in the whole process
supply loop, including raw-water lifts, treatment boosters, and
distribution units. At peak, these drives draw MWs of electrical
power, making the district a non-trivial load in local demand-
side management. Moreover, their operations often align with
peak electricity demand periods, which further ampliﬁes grid
stress.
To address the impact of uncertain and time-varying elec-
tricity prices, we design the objective function to capture both
the operational energy usage and its temporal coupling price
cost. The optimization seeks to minimize the expected total
cost across the planning horizon:

IV. END-TO-END STOCHASTIC PREDICT-THEN-OPTIMIZE

Section III established a detailed integrated model including
both energy-side scheduling and water-side hydraulic oper-

uncertainty, we propose the end-to-end Stochastic Predict-
then-Optimize (SPTO) framework. As shown in Fig. 2,
the framework integrates a probabilistic forecasting neural
network for estimating uncertain parameters and the stage for
cost-efﬁcient scheduling optimization.
In the following sections, we ﬁrst describe the optimization
and prediction stages separately. After, we explain speciﬁcally
how to connect the two stages by constructing a surrogate
function with surrogate gradients in Section IV-C.

generality. For mixed-integer optimization problems, the origin
non-convex feasible region ˜U can be replaced by its closed
convex hull U. Since linear optimization over ˜U is equivalent
to linear optimization over U, the convex surrogate loss
functions can be directly applied to these problems. This
equivalence enables the Predict-then-optimize framework to
handle a broad range of decision-making tasks involving
binary variables.
In practice, directly solving (24) requires accurate proba-
bilistic forecasting of electricity prices, therefore motivating
the following forecasting mechanism.

Baselines: To evaluate the performance of the proposed
SPTO framework, we compare it with three baselines.
Deterministic two-stage approach (DTS) [26] predicts uncertain
parameters as ﬁxed point values and solves the optimization
problem based on these predictions. Stochastic two-stage
approach (STS) [24] addresses the uncertainty using probabilis-
tic forecasting models and optimizes the expected objective
value. However, the distribution is not directly incorporated into
the optimization stages. Smart predict-then-optimize (SPO) [21]
integrates the optimization objective into the training process
of the prediction model. Unlike DTS and STS, SPO aligns
predictions with the optimization stages. However, it utilizes
deterministic predictions and does not explicitly handle the
uncertainty of parameter prediction errors.

C. Effectiveness of SPTO Examination
Moreover, we would like to verify the misconception men-
tioned in the introduction that higher forecasting accuracy,
as measured by the two-stage approach, does not necessarily
suggest smaller decision regret. Fig. 7 presents a comparative
analysis of training forecasting models using SPTO loss and
Gaussian loss separately. In sub-ﬁgure (a), training with the
SPTO loss (red line) leads to an increase in the Gaussian
loss (blue line) of the prediction model, which increases from
approximately −1.2 to −0.6 within 10 epochs. Meanwhile,
sub-ﬁgure (b) shows that training with the Gaussian loss (blue
line) ultimately leads to a higher SPTO loss (red line). The
SPTO loss increases from 1740 to 1820.
These ﬁndings conﬁrm that the SPTO loss effectively tunes
the prediction and optimization stages. While Gaussian loss
improves the prediction accuracy of traditional measurements,
it fails to reduce decision regret. Instead, SPTO loss is able to

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper investigates the challenge of cost-efﬁcient
water-energy scheduling under electricity price uncertainty
within the smart grid. To address the inherent misalign-
ment between prediction and decision-making in traditional
two-stage methods, we develop an end-to-end Stochastic
Predict-Then-Optimize (SPTO) framework. By integrating
a probabilistic forecasting model with a stochastic regret-
minimization objective, the SPTO approach enables direct
alignment between predictive accuracy and downstream oper-
ational decisions.
Unlike prior PTO studies limited to synthetic benchmarks,
this work applies the SPTO framework to a real-world
urban water-energy system, demonstrating its practical scala-
bility and impact. The experimental results show that SPTO
effectively mitigates cumulative decision errors induced by
prediction uncertainty, leading to improved energy cost savings
and load ﬂexibility. Beyond optimizing pump operations, our
ﬁndings also underscore the coupled dynamics between water
infrastructure and power systems, advocating for integrated
approaches to urban resource management in smart grid
environments.
