Modeling of Water Transfer Delay for Optimal
Operation of Integrated Electrical-Water System
Yiming Yao
, Chunyan Li
, Member, IEEE, Heng-Ming Tai
, Senior Member, IEEE,
and Changzheng Shao
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper investigates the transfer delays of a water
distribution system (WDS). The delay implies that water balance
between the supply and demand does not meet instantly, which may
affect the regulation capability of WDS on the electricity consump-
tion proﬁle. Current research did not consider the role the transfer
delay plays in the cooperative operation of power system and WDS.
Not considering the delay hinders the water ﬂow dynamics from
being used to provide energy ﬂexibility for the power system oper-
ation. An electrical-water analogy method is proposed to model the
water transfer delay of WDS. The cooperative operation model of
the WDS and power system is created to facilitate the incorporation
of wind power to the power system and lower the operation cost.
Moreover, a graph-based searching scheme is developed to enable
efﬁcient and accurate calculation of the pressures and water ﬂows
in the WDS network. Case studies are presented to demonstrate the
merits of the proposed electrical-water analogy method in power
system operation.
Index Terms—Power system, water distribution system, transfer
delay, electrical-water analogy, wind power.

I. INTRODUCTION
W
ater distribution system (WDS) accounts for 5% of the
annual total electricity consumption in China, and up to
7.1% in California, USA [1], [2]. Electrical demand associated
with WDS is projected to increase. WDS can be regarded as an
adjustable electrical load from the perspective of power system
[3]. The electricity consumption proﬁle of WDS can be regulated
ﬂexibly by coordinating the operation of water pumps and tanks.
The cooperative operation of the power system and WDS attracts
great attention recently due to its merits of high-efﬁciency, cost-
effectiveness and sustainability [4], [5].
The optimal water-power ﬂow problem has been studied to
optimize the operation strategies of the power system and WDS
considering the network constraints [6]–[8]. Many efforts have
been made to deal with the non-convex hydraulic equations
of WDS, such as the piecewise linear approximation [9], the
second-order cone relaxation [10], and the quasi-convex hull
relaxation method [11]. The energy ﬂexibility of WDS has been
analyzed and integrated into the operational model of power
system [12], [13]. The controllable energy assets such as the
water pump equip the WDS to provide the demand response
service and the frequency regulation service in the electricity
market. The models for the WDS to bid in the electricity market
were also developed to maximize the proﬁt [14]–[16]. Most

works on the cooperative operation of power system and WDS
assume that the water is transported from the source to the user
instantaneously without considering the transfer delays of the
water [3]–[16].
To the best of our knowledge, not many researches on the
modeling of the transfer delays in the WDS have been reported.
Besides, the transfer delays have not been incorporated into the
cooperative operation model of the power system and WDS.
The transmission time of the water from the source to the
user depends on the velocity of ﬂow and the distance. It can take
several hours to transport the water in a large WDS network.
Neglectingthetransferdelaysmayhavesigniﬁcantimpactonthe
optimal cooperative operation strategies of the power system and
the WDS. On the other hand, the transfer delays in WDS offer
substantial potential for power system to leverage the ﬂexibility
provided by WDS. Electricity travels at a speed close to the
light, and it can be assumed that electricity is transported from
thegenerator totheloadinstantaneously. Thus, thepower system
must ensure the real-time balance of supply and demand. WDS
does not need to instantly balance the supply and demand. The
balance can be maintained by the excess water transported from
the source to the user. This enables the power shift capability
of WDS to be fully leveraged and allows the power system to
be operated more ﬂexibly. The shift here refers to the situation
that more electricity is consumed during the period of high
wind power output and less electricity is consumed when the
wind power output is low. More renewable energy can thus be
incorporated into the power system and the operation cost of the
integrated electrical-water system can be reduced.
This paper proposes a cooperative operation model of the
power system and WDS considering the delays of water trans-
mission. The movement of water ﬂow in the pipe can be de-
scribed by a series of partial differential equations (PDEs) [17].
The ﬁnite difference (FD) method, which discretizes the time
and space into many points, is used to deal with these PDEs
[18]. As the number of discretized points increases, accuracy of
the FD method improves, whereas the computational expense
increases dramatically. The increasing penetration of renewable
energy, such as wind power, has brought great challenges to
the power system operation due to its uncertainty. Since the
output of wind power varies rapidly, the time resolution of the
cooperative operation model should be in minutes. This makes
the time-consuming FD method not applicable.
The idea of electrical-water ﬂow analogy has been applied to
the modeling of the WDS [19]–[21]. The analogy approach uses
an equivalent electric circuit to provide an exact representation
of the model. These WDS network models were described
by a distributed parameter circuit, which was in the form of
PDEs and has to be solved by the FD method. Recently, the
method of electric circuit analogy was also exploited in the
modeling of integrated energy systems [22]–[26]. The methods
in [22], [23] did not consider the transfer delays. The equivalent
electric circuit analogy model proposed for a single pipe did not
model the transmission process of the ﬂow in the network [24].
Although the methods proposed in [25], [26] were discrete in
the time domain, severe calculation errors may occur when the
transfer delay is not an integer multiple of the time resolution.
Since the water ﬂow and pressure in WDS vary continuously
over the time and space domains, the discrete method may not
be applicable.
In this paper, a graph-based electrical-water analogy method
is proposed to solve the PDEs of WDS. The method aims to sig-
niﬁcantly reduce the computation time in comparison to the FD
method. The equivalent electric circuit for the WDS is developed
and transformed into algebraic equations in the Laplace domain.
The water transmission process in WDS is modeled by the
graph-based searching method. The inverse Laplace transform
can be applied to obtain the ﬂows and pressures along the
network in the time domain. The constraints of the water ﬂow
and pressure in the pipe expressed in the continuous time and
space domains are replaced by a series of simpliﬁed constraints.
The main contributions of this paper are
a) Propose an energy-water model considering water transfer
delay in WDS. The proposed model provides a realistic
representation of the water ﬂow dynamics which fully
exploits the energy ﬂexibility of WDS.
b) Establish an equivalent electric circuit model to describe
the water transfer delay of WDS and provide a series of
simpliﬁed constraints for the pipe in the continuous time
and the space domains.
c) Develop a graph-based electrical-water analogy method to
model the water transmission process in the whole WDS
network and an efﬁcient solution methodology to ﬁnd the
solutions efﬁciently.
In the remainder of this paper, the proposed electrical-water
analogy model of the power-WDS system is described in Sec-
tion II. Rigorous mathematical derivation is presented for veri-
ﬁcation. Section III presents the detailed modeling of the water
transmission process in WDS and the solution procedure for
the graph-based method. Section IV describes the cooperative
operation model of the power system and WDS. Case studies are
given in Section V to demonstrate the beneﬁts of the proposed
model on wind power accommodation and computation time
reduction. The paper is concluded in Section VI.
II. ELECTRICAL-WATER ANALOGY MODEL OF WATER
TRANSFER DELAYS
This section presents the proposed electric circuit analogy
model for the water transfer delay in the WDS. The PDEs of the
water ﬂow in the pipe are transformed into algebraic equations in
the Laplace domain. The non-convex models of the water pump
and valve are also turned into linear models using the electrical-
water analogy method. Prior to the detailed description of the
proposed model, the structures of the WDS and the integrated
electrical-water system are outlined.
A. Overview of Electrical-Water System
Fig. 1 depicts the structure of an integrated electrical-water
system. The reservoir serves as the source of water for WDS.
The water ﬂow is transported from one node to another in the
pipe due to the head pressure difference between two nodes.
The water pump in the WDS consumes electricity to increase the
pressure difference and transport the water ﬂow. More electricity

This study considers the WDS connected to the power trans-

Then the impact of WDS optimization on the entire power
transmission system can be evaluated.
2) The large-scale renewable energy output is generally
transported and processed via the power transmission sys-

on promoting the integration of renewable energy into
the power system, the power transmission system would
provide better assessment information.
3) By replacing the power ﬂow model with a power distribu-
tion network, the proposed transfer delay model for WDS
also can be applied to the cooperative operation of the
power distribution system and WDS. Future research will
conduct on a hierarchical control strategy of WDS and the
power transmission and distribution systems.

2) Water Transfer Delay on Wind Power Accommodation:
The electrical-water analogy model of the pipe in Fig. 2 contains
inductors and capacitors, which are energy-storage elements

of wind power. If power system faces a sudden increase of
wind power, the pump in WDS will increase the electricity
consumption and allow excess water be stored in the pipe to
ensure the balance between supply and demand. If the wind
power decreases suddenly, the electricity consumption of the
pump in WDS will be lowered and the pipe will release the stored
water to satisfy the water demand. Thus, power system can make
full use of the energy ﬂexibility of WDS to accommodate the
abundant wind power.

the water pipe [17], [32]. The water quality can be maintained
by optimizing the chlorine dosage and the location of chlorine
injection [33], [34].
Future research will be conducted to investigate the impact of
water transfer delay on the turbulent ﬂow and water quality for
safe and economical operation of the WDS.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper has presented a WDS optimization model to im-
prove the operation of integrated electrical-water system consid-
ering the water transfer delay. The water transmission process in
the WDS is modeled by a graph-based searching scheme. The
proposed method improves the performance of WDS in power
system by further reducing the wind power curtailment and the
operation cost. Moreover, it efﬁciently solves the PDEs of WDS
to lower the computation cost dramatically.
Results from case studies have demonstrated that the coopera-
tive operation of power system and WDS considering the water
transfer delays further improves the reduction of wind power
curtailment. Operation costs of the two energy systems have
been reduced considerably after coordinating with each other.
Moreover, the proposed method signiﬁcantly lowers the compu-
tational cost compared with the FD method and the scalability
of the proposed method is linear.
In addition to promoting the wind power accommodation and
reducing the operation cost, the proposed model can be extended
to other electrical-water systems, such as the energy ﬂexibility
analysis [12], the bidding strategy in the electricity market [14],
and the tank size optimization [18]. Future work will investigate
the impact of transfer delay on the above applications.
