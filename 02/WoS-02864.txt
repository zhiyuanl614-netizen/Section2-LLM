A B S T R A C T
With the increasing power demand of water distribution systems, utilizing pumps and water tanks as flexibility 
resources for the power system can effectively enhance the operational flexibility and efficiency of integrated 
energy systems. This paper proposes a data-assisted two-stage robust optimization method, incorporating a 
dynamic risk tolerance adjustment mechanism, to coordinate the operation of power and water supply systems, 
improve renewable energy penetration, and reduce overall operational costs. The proposed method first employs 
data-assisted techniques to generate uncertainty predictions and applies two-stage robust optimization to 
determine safe operational boundaries. Then, a convex hull method is used to construct probabilistic operation 
regions under different scheduling risk tolerances. Simultaneously, a dynamic risk tolerance adjustment mech­
anism is introduced, where the risk tolerance parameter is iteratively updated based on real-time evaluation 
indices to enhance the system’s adaptability to uncertainties. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed 
method effectively coordinates power-water system operations, enhances the flexibility of the water distribution 
system, improves renewable energy utilization, and reduces peak loads and reserve capacity requirements in the 
power system. Furthermore, the dynamic risk tolerance adjustment mechanism and probabilistic operation re­
gions enrich scheduling strategies, enabling the system to dynamically optimize scheduling schemes based on 
load fluctuations and renewable energy forecasting accuracy, achieving an optimal balance between security and 
economy.

1. Introduction
With the global energy system transitioning towards low-carbon 
development, the share of renewable energy in the electricity mix is 
increasing rapidly. The World Energy Outlook 2023 predicts that by 
2030, renewable energy will account for nearly 50 % of total electricity 
production [1]. However, due to the intermittency and uncertainty of 
wind and solar power, power systems face greater scheduling chal­
lenges, making the role of flexibility resources increasingly critical [2]. 
Insufficient flexibility may lead to wind and solar curtailment, resulting 
in economic losses and limiting the integration of renewable energy [3]. 
Against this backdrop, water systems have emerged as important re­
sources for enhancing grid flexibility due to their dispatchability and 
energy storage potential [4]–[5]. Water tanks and pumps in urban water 
distribution systems not only consume electricity but can also be opti­
mally scheduled to balance grid load, reduce peak power demand, and 
improve adaptability to renewable energy [6]. Therefore, developing an 
optimization scheduling model that coordinates the operation of water 
distribution and power systems is crucial for improving grid stability, 
reducing operational costs, and increasing the utilization of renewable 
energy.
In recent years, as the role of water systems in power system flexi­
bility management has gained widespread attention, an increasing 
number of studies have focused on exploring water-power coordinated 
optimization methods to enhance renewable energy integration and 
optimize operational costs. Reference [7] proposed an optimization 
approach based on chance constraints and stochastic programming, 
integrating machine learning-based forecasting models to optimize 
water-power-renewable energy hybrid scheduling and improve system 
flexibility. Reference [8] developed an optimization model that co­
ordinates second-level frequency response (FRF) and 15-min power 
regulation flexibility (PRF) to enhance stability in a wind-hydro hybrid 
power system. Reference [9] further incorporated probabilistic distri­
bution methods to assess the impact of renewable energy output and 
load forecasting errors, thereby improving grid security. Reference [10] 
introduced a two-stage nonlinear mixed-integer optimization model, 
coordinating wind power uncertainty using nonlinear programming and 
second-order cone programming (SOCP). Reference [11] adopted a joint 
probabilistic constraint optimization method, leveraging piecewise 
linearization techniques to transform the non-convex optimization 
problem in water-power systems into a solvable mixed-integer linear 
programming (MILP) problem for optimizing power and water supply 
costs. Reference [12] employed a distributionally robust optimization 
strategy combined with the Benders decomposition method to address 
false data injection attacks (FDIA) and renewable energy uncertainties. 
Reference [13] proposed a two-stage stochastic co-optimization 
approach that couples the power system with a reverse osmosis 
seawater desalination plant (RO-WDP) to reduce costs and operational 
risks. Reference [14] utilized second-order cone programming (SOCP) to 
analyze the cascading effects of power outages on water distribution 
systems, identifying critical vulnerable nodes and proposing optimiza­
tion strategies. Reference [15] introduced a water-energy co-optimiza­
tion method based on big data analytics to mitigate operational risks in a 
100 % renewable energy scenario. Reference [16] employed a quasi- 
convex hull approach to transform a mixed-integer nonlinear pro­
gramming (MINLP) problem into a mixed-integer convex programming 
(MICP) problem, thereby improving optimization computational 
efficiency.
However, existing studies primarily focus on source-side optimiza­
tion in integrated water-power systems, while research on optimizing 
the scheduling of urban water distribution systems as demand-side re­
sources within the demand-side management (DSM) framework remains 
relatively limited [17]. Additionally, current optimization approaches 
often concentrate on individual subsystems without fully considering 
the interactions between water and power networks, making it difficult 
to achieve globally optimal scheduling [18]. Although previous studies 
have explored the co-optimization of water and power systems [19], 
several limitations remain, including single-objective optimization, a 
lack of uncertainty modeling, and limited applications of data-driven 
techniques. Additionally, due to the complex structure of urban water 
networks and the uncertainty of water demand, directly utilizing water 
tanks as energy storage may lead to water supply challenges or addi­
tional costs.
To address these issues, this paper proposes a data-assisted two-stage 
robust optimization method with dynamic risk tolerance adjustment to 
effectively coordinate the optimized operation of urban water networks 
and power systems. By integrating data-driven uncertainty forecasting, 
robust optimization, and dynamic risk tolerance adjustment, the pro­
posed method optimizes water pump scheduling while enhancing 
renewable energy penetration, ensuring water supply reliability, and 
adaptively balancing system security and economy under different risk 
tolerance levels. The main contributions of this paper are as follows: 
1. Propose a data-assisted water-power system coordination optimiza­
tion method, which integrates data-driven uncertainty forecasting 
techniques to incorporate the stochastic characteristics of water and 
power loads into the scheduling optimization process, enabling more 
precise joint optimization modeling.
2. Develop a probabilistic operation region construction method and 
evaluation framework, which utilizes the convex hull method to 
establish operational boundaries under different scheduling schemes 
and introduces quantitative indicators to assess system flexibility and 
the adaptability of scheduling strategies under various uncertainty 
levels.
3. Introduce a dynamic risk tolerance adjustment mechanism within 
the robust optimization framework, which dynamically adjusts the 
risk tolerance parameter based on real-time system conditions, 
allowing the optimization scheme to flexibly control conservative­
ness and adaptively balance security and economy during the 
scheduling process.
The remainder is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces the 
operational constraints of the integrated water-power system and the 
construction and evaluation of probabilistic operation regions. Section 3
presents the solution methodology and simplification techniques. Sec­
tion 4 validates the feasibility and superior performance of the proposed 
method through case studies. Section 5 provides conclusions and future 
research directions.
2. Operational and optimization methods for the integrated 
electricity-water energy system
Introducing water tanks and variable-speed pumps as flexibility re­
sources in urban water networks can help the power system integrate 
more renewable energy and balance power loads. The power sector can 
utilize these facilities to adjust loads and meet the electricity demand of 
the water network, while the water sector can optimize scheduling to 
reduce operational costs or lease controllable assets to provide stable 
services. Independently optimizing the power and water systems may 
fail to achieve a globally optimal solution. Therefore, it is necessary to 
develop a coordinated scheduling strategy to ensure both power and 
water supply security, enhance renewable energy utilization, improve 
grid flexibility, and reduce overall operational costs.

2.2. Two-stage robust optimization method
Robust optimization aims to ensure system stability under the worst- 
case conditions. Two-stage robust optimization refines output con­
straints while achieving optimization objectives, reducing the impact of 
uncertainties and enhancing scheduling accuracy and reliability.

and penalty terms associated with renewable energy curtailment. The 
primary objective of the function is to minimize both costs. For the water 
distribution system (WDS), the objective function incorporates the cost 
of power provision along with penalties for unmet demand. Conse­
quently, the optimization of the WDS seeks to minimize these costs as 
well.

In the WDS, pumps are employed to sustain water flow when pres­
sure is insufficient, while valves regulate pressure or control water flow 
within the pipelines. Tanks function as reservoirs to store or supply 
water to the system. Pumps elevate pressure and transport water 
through the consumption of electricity, with greater electricity usage 
enabling higher water flow rates. Tanks store water to maintain the 
balance between supply and demand.
From the perspective of the power system, the WDS can be regarded 
as a controllable load. By optimizing the operation of pumps and tanks, 
the WDS can fulfill the flexibility requirements of the power system and 
integrate more renewable energy.
2.2.2. Framework tructure
The objective of the optimization is to minimize operating costs. To 
achieve this, the integrated power-water system optimization is carried 
out in three phases, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The first phase entails 
probabilistic forecasting of renewable energy and load uncertainties, 
where the accuracy of these forecasts directly impacts the subsequent 
optimization outcomes. The second phase optimizes power system de­
mand, determining any surplus or deficit, which reflects the system’s 
flexibility. The third phase focuses on optimizing the water distribution 
system’s response to the power system’s flexibility requirements while 
ensuring the WDS operates normally.
This paper adopts the Two-Stage Robust Optimization (TSRO) 
method, which considers uncertainty factors to optimize the coordi­
nated operation of a high-renewable-energy power system and the WDS. 
System flexibility is quantified by the proportion of renewable energy 
and curtailment rates, with the goal of identifying the optimal opera­
tional strategy for the integrated system. Furthermore, the uncertainty 
set constructed more closely reflects real-world conditions, avoiding the 
need to divide into multiple typical scenarios. In contrast, fluctuations in 
water demand are relatively minor and, as such, are excluded from the 
calculations.

uncertainty. This method can guide system scheduling, dynamically 
adjusting risk tolerance based on different operational states and risk

2.3.3. Dynamic adjustment and adaptive optimization
In uncertainty optimization scheduling, the setting of risk tolerance α 
directly affects both the security and economy of the system. A lower 
value of α corresponds to a larger uncertainty set, providing a higher 
safety margin for the system but increasing the operational cost. A 
higher value of α allows a more compact operating region, improving 
economy, but it may reduce the system’s robustness. To address this, this 
paper proposes a dynamic adjustment method for risk tolerance based 
on evaluation indices. The value of α is adjusted in real-time by assessing 
Beq, Ccen, and Vvio, thereby achieving an adaptive balance between se­
curity and economy in the scheduling strategy.
The risk tolerance α is dynamically updated at each scheduling time 
step t by the following recursive formula: 
αt = αt−1 + Δαt
(55) 
where the adjustment Δαt is determined by the evaluation indices: 
Δαt = k1⋅
(
Beq −BA
)
−k2⋅(Ccen −CA) −k3⋅Vvio
(56) 
where BA is the safety index threshold; CA is the concentration index 
threshold; and k1, k2, k3 are weight coefficients.
When Beq falls below the threshold or Vvio is too high, the system’s 
operating point is close to or exceeds the boundary of the POR, indi­
cating insufficient safety margin. In this case, α should be reduced to 
expand the operating region and enhance conservatism. When Ccen is too 
high, the unit outputs are dispersed, leading to greater uncertainty, and 
α should be reduced to tighten the operating region, improving sched­
uling determinism. Conversely, if Beq is too high, Ccen is too low, and Vvio 
is close to zero, the system is stable, and α can be moderately increased 
to shrink the operating region, optimizing economy. This dynamic 
adjustment mechanism ensures that risk tolerance α changes with the 
system’s operational state, achieving a balance between security and 
economy in the scheduling strategy.
Based on the dynamic adjustment of risk tolerance α, an adaptive 
POR can be constructed to ensure the system’s optimal operating state 
under different risk levels. This method includes the following steps: 
1. Uncertainty adjustment: Based on the risk tolerance α, calculate and 
generate the corresponding probabilistic confidence intervals. A 
lower α corresponds to a larger uncertainty set, covering a wider 
range and ensuring a larger safety margin for the system, but with 
higher operational costs. A higher α forms a more compact operating 
region, improving economy, but may reduce the system’s scheduling 
flexibility.
2. Boundary construction: At each αi level, calculate the system’s POR 
ΩR. By using the convex hull method, the boundaries of the operating 
region are computed and approximated as the safe operating region 
Ω, ensuring system stability at different risk tolerance levels.
3. Evaluation and optimization: Calculate the current operating do­
main’s Beq, Ccen, and Vvio, and combine them with the dynamic 
adjustment strategy to optimize α, ensuring that the final selected 
operating region achieves a dynamic balance between security and 
economy.

4. Case studies
4.1. Data description
The dataset comes from a city in eastern China, covering the period 
from May 14, 2021, to November 27, 2023, with a 15-min time reso­
lution, resulting in 96-time steps per day. All models were developed 
using Python and PyTorch, and run on a personal computer equipped 
with an Intel i7–13,700 5.20 GHz CPU, 16 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA 
RTX 4070 GPU. The optimization solver used was GUROBI 11.0, while 
the power system model was built using PANDAPOWER [23]. The 
dataset was split into 80 % for training and 20 % for testing to handle 
uncertainty factors.
This study uses the modified IEEE 37-node system to validate the 
effectiveness of the proposed method. The equipment parameters of the 
test system are shown in Table 1. The topology of the coupled power and 
WDS is illustrated in Fig. 7. The system consists of 18 load nodes, 4 PV 
power nodes (with capacities of 35 MW, 25 MW, 30 MW, and 30 MW), 4 
wind power nodes (with capacities of 50 MW, 40 MW, 55 MW, and 35 
MW), 2 battery energy storage nodes, 4 generation unit nodes, and 16 
water distribution system nodes. Additionally, nodes 10, 21, and 35 are 
connected to water tanks, each with a capacity of 2000 m3. The outputs 
of renewable energy, power demand, and water demand are detailed in 
Appendix B.
The industrial electricity prices for the power system are presented in 
Table 2. In the experiment, the confidence levels for wind power gen­
eration, photovoltaic generation, and load are set to 85 %, 90 %, and 95 
%. Wind power exhibits high fluctuations, so a lower confidence level is 
adopted to enhance system flexibility. PV generation is relatively stable, 
thus set at 90 %. Load forecasting is critical for power supply security 
and cost control, requiring a higher confidence level to ensure system 
stability.

4.3.2. Optimization results
Based on the uncertainty results generated by each model in the 
previous section, this paper compares the operating costs of different 
optimization methods under different risk tolerance levels, as shown in 
Table 5. Scenario A involves only the power system, while Scenario B 
integrates the water distribution system, introducing variable-speed 
pumps and water tanks as flexibility resources. To verify the effective­
ness of the two-stage robust optimization (TRO) method proposed in this 
paper, MILP (Mixed-Integer Linear Programming) is used as the 
benchmark method (assuming the predicted values are the expected 
values and ignoring uncertainty), and the stochastic optimization (SO) 
method (assuming the prediction errors follow a normal distribution and 
generating the uncertainty set using Method 3).
Table 5 shows that, regardless of the optimization method used, the 
operating cost in Scenario B is lower than in Scenario A, with the overall 
cost reduced by approximately 8.68 % to 13.16 %. This indicates that 
the water distribution system, by providing flexibility resources such as 
variable-speed pumps and water tanks, effectively reduced the power 
system’s output demand and lowered the operating costs. Among the 
different methods, MILP has the lowest cost but does not consider the 
uncertainty of renewable energy output and load demand, which may 
lead to scheduling failure or increased system safety risks during large 
fluctuations. The SO method is theoretically superior to TRO, but due to 
the assumption that prediction errors follow a normal distribution, it 
struggles to handle the skewness, heavy tails, or asymmetry of renew­
able energy output and load demand, leading to suboptimal results. In 
contrast, the data-assisted two-stage robust optimization method pro­
posed in this paper is more accurate in capturing the characteristics of 
renewable energy and load, improving the precision of the uncertainty

4.4. Day-ahead dispatching plan
In practical operation, schedulers need to dynamically adjust the risk 
tolerance α based on the fluctuations in renewable energy output and 
load changes to balance the system’s security and economy. This case 
study designs four scheduling scenarios to analyze the impact of power- 
water coupling optimization and dynamic risk tolerance: Scenario A 
considers only the power system scheduling, with no optimization of the 
water distribution system and a fixed α = 5%; Scenarios B and C 
introduce optimization of the water distribution system, with α = 5% 
and α = 20%; Scenario D, based on the previous scenarios, dynamically 
adjusts the risk tolerance αt according to the safety index Beq, concen­
tration index Ccen, and violation index Vvio.
The day-ahead scheduling plans for the four scenarios are shown in 
Fig. 9. In Scenario A, due to the lack of coordinated scheduling with the 
water distribution system, the system relies on traditional units to cope 
with renewable energy fluctuations and load changes, leading to 
frequent power output fluctuations, high reserve capacity demand, and 
increased operational costs. Scenario B, through the coordinated 
scheduling of the water distribution and power systems, effectively 
smooths the load by reducing pump power consumption and alleviating 
pressure on the power system during high load periods (6:00–12:00, 
18:00–22:00), while utilizing surplus renewable energy to store water 
during low load periods (1:00–5:00, 12:00–18:00). The operating cost is 
reduced by 10.54 % compared to Scenario A, but due to the lower risk 
tolerance, reserve capacity demand remains relatively high. Scenario C 
adopts a high-risk tolerance strategy with more aggressive scheduling, 
prioritizing the use of renewable energy and storage to reduce unit 
output, further lowering operating costs by 16.87 % compared to Sce­
nario B. However, this strategy leads to more frequent adjustments to 
the schedule during large fluctuations in renewable energy, increasing 
the risk of scheduling failure and insufficient reserve capacity, especially 
during high load periods. Scenario D, through dynamic adjustment of 
the risk tolerance αt, adapts to optimize scheduling based on the sys­
tem’s state: reducing αt during high uncertainty periods (6:00–12:00, 
18:00–22:00) to enhance system robustness, and increasing αt during 
stable periods (1:00–5:00, 12:00–18:00) to reduce reserve costs. 
Compared to Scenario C, Scenario D results in a 3.56 % higher operating 
cost, but by flexibly adjusting the risk tolerance, it balances economy 
and security while further improving the adaptability of reserve capacity 
allocation and the flexibility of unit scheduling.

effectively smooths the water demand of the water distribution system,

4.5. Analysis of scheduling results
4.5.1. Unit commitment analysis
In power grid scheduling that considers renewable energy uncer­
tainty, the POR of unit commitment describes the boundary of unit 
output and its coordination relationship. This paper selects three time 
periods—3:00, 14:00, and 20:00—to visualize unit output under 
different risk tolerance levels using a two-dimensional representation, as 
shown in Fig. 11. The red boundary in the figure represents the Safe 
Operation Region, which indicates the feasible output range of the units 
under extreme conditions to ensure that safety constraints are not 
violated.
Compared to Scenario A, the overall unit output in Scenarios B/C 
decreases, and the POR shrinks, demonstrating that coordinated 
scheduling with the water distribution system effectively reduces the 
burden on the power system and improves unit operational stability. In 
Scenarios B and C, increasing risk tolerance makes the scheduling more 
concentrated in the economic operation range: Low risk tolerance (light 
blue region) provides greater scheduling flexibility but increases reserve 
capacity demand and operating costs; High risk tolerance (dark blue 
region) reduces reserve requirements and lowers costs, but renewable 
energy fluctuations may increase operational risks. This change reflects 
the trade-off between economy and security.
Scenario D, by adaptively adjusting the risk tolerance, enhances the 
adaptability of unit commitment. Compared to Scenarios B/C, its POR 
dynamically changes over time: During high-uncertainty periods (e.g., 
20:00), risk tolerance is lowered, expanding the operating range and 
enhancing system robustness; During stable periods (e.g., 3:00), risk 
tolerance is increased, making scheduling more concentrated on eco­
nomic operation, reducing reserve capacity demand, and improving 
output stability. Compared to fixed risk tolerance strategies, Scenario D 
balances economy and security across different time periods, lowering 
reserve costs while mitigating operational risks.
4.5.2. Evaluation index analysis
To quantitatively assess the operational performance of different 
scheduling scenarios, this paper calculates the safety index Beq, the 
concentration index Ccen, and the violation index Vvio at 3:00, 14:00, and 
20:00, with the specific values for each scenario shown in Fig. 12.
During the nighttime stable period (3:00), Beq in Scenario A does not 
significantly differ from the other scenarios, indicating that when load 
fluctuations are small, different scheduling strategies have a relatively 
minor impact on system security. However, during high-uncertainty 
periods (14:00 and 20:00), Beq in Scenarios B/C is significantly higher 
than in Scenario A, demonstrating that coordinated scheduling of the 
water distribution system effectively mitigates renewable energy fluc­
tuations. At the same time, Scenario A exhibits a relatively high Ccen, as 
the lack of coordination with the water distribution system forces units 
to handle larger output fluctuations, leading to a more dispersed overall 
scheduling distribution. Additionally, as risk tolerance increases, Beq

5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a data-assisted two-stage robust optimization 
scheduling method for the coordinated operation of urban water dis­
tribution and power grid integrated energy systems and constructs a 
probabilistic operation region to improve system security and economy. 
The main conclusions are as follows: 
1. Quantification of the impact of flexibility resources on the power 
system: The demand for downward flexibility from the water dis­
tribution system is higher than the upward demand. Moreover, when 
the water tank is located near the renewable energy generation node, 
the system’s flexibility improvement is more significant. This in­
dicates that proper water resource scheduling helps reduce the 
impact of renewable energy on the power system.
2. The proposed data-assisted method effectively improves optimiza­
tion results: This method can effectively narrow the forecasting 
interval while ensuring the coverage of operational points, yielding 
higher comprehensive performance metrics. By incorporating the 
probability distribution information from historical data, the opti­
mization approach can better cope with uncertainty factors and 
enhance scheduling robustness.
3. Probabilistic operation region and evaluation indices enrich sched­
uling diversity: Operators can adjust scheduling strategies in 
different scenarios to dynamically balance security and economy. 
The introduction of the dynamic risk tolerance mechanism allows the 
system to adjust risk tolerance in real-time based on factors such as 
renewable energy forecast accuracy and load fluctuations, prevent­
ing the security or economic losses caused by a fixed tolerance level.
Future research will focus on developing systematic mathematical 
models to quantify the risk-economy trade-off boundaries and optimize 
the optimal decision points under different scheduling strategies. 
Additionally, robust game theory can be used to analyze the risk- 
economy evolution under different market environments and policy 
incentives. Furthermore, research can explore emergency response 
mechanisms for situations where renewable energy exceeds the proba­
bilistic operating region, optimizing reserve power deployment and 
demand-side management strategies to enhance the practical feasibility 
of scheduling solutions.
