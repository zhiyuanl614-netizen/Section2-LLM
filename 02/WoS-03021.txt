Abstract—Multi-voltage-levelurbanpowernetwork(UPN)isthe
basis for other infrastructure networks, especially water distribu-
tion network (WDN). Compared with other networks, UPN is more
vulnerabletoextremeevents,includingextremeweathereventsand
deliberate attacks. The affection of UPN fault on WDN displays
complex multi-stage characteristics. This paper ﬁrst proposes a
comprehensive tri-stage model of electrical-water fault propaga-
tion, including the electrical-water network connection model, the
reduced-order hydraulic network model, and the water supply
model of customers. Second, a scenario selection method and two
resilience assessment indices are proposed. The indices measure the
resistive capability of UPN considering the fault propagation effect.
Finally, the proposed model and assessment method are veriﬁed
by a simpliﬁed real-world urban power and water network based
on the selected typical fault scenarios. The resilience assessment
method helps electrical utilities to identify critical components
of UPN and to make optimal decisions for long-term component
hardening and short-term post-disaster restoration.
Index Terms—Resilience assessment, urban power network,
water distribution network, fault propagation, extreme event.

I. INTRODUCTION
City is the political, economic, science and technology, and
cultural center of a region and even a country. The infrastruc-
tural networks, such as electrical power, water, natural gas, and
communication, are the basis for the normal operation of the
city. In particular, power system failure not only affects different
typesofcustomersbutalsocausesmalfunctionofothernetworks
[1], [2]. The concept of "power system resilience” is introduced
to assess its ability to withstand and recover from signiﬁcant
damages caused by natural disasters (e.g., hurricanes, ﬂoods) or
deliberate attacks [3], [4], [5].
With global climate change, extreme weather events tend to
be more frequent than before [6], [7]. One type is hurricane
or torrential rain that occurs in summer. For example, the
Zhengzhou 7.20 torrential rain in 2021, China, destroyed over
500 overhead lines. Water treatment plants and pump stations
stopped operation due to power outages [8]. Over 1.2 million
customers lost power and/or water supply for at least one day.
The second is snow and ice coating that occurs in winter, such
as the Texas snowstorm in February 2021, the United States.
Over 3.6 million customers lost power supply in this event. The
power outage also led to water outages in some regions [1], [9].
Although water distribution pipelines are hardly damaged in
those disaster events, severe power outages lead to a cascading
effect in the water distribution network (WDN), which contains
a lot of electrical-driven components [10]. Eventually, the out-
of-service of electricity and water in residential, commercial,
and public-service customers cause a wide social pandemic [11].
Therefore, the resilience assessment of the urban power network
(UPN) should consider electrical-water fault propagation, which
serves as a guideline for utility companies to efﬁciently recover
both electricity and water supply after extreme events.
The vulnerable component identiﬁcation is an essential mea-
sure of the “withstanding” aspect of the system’s resilience. It
means the percentage of system function loss that is caused by
one or several component failures [12]. From the perspective of
complex network theory, a critical node or region of the WDN
canbeidentiﬁedbytheweightedsumofitsstructuralimportance
and functional importance [13], [14]. Reference [15] proposed
a vulnerability assessment method of industrial WDN using
the dynamic inoperability input-output model. Overall, those
works were focused on fault propagation within the WDN by
specifying a faulted branch or faulted node. In disaster scenarios,
however, the large-scale water outage is not caused by damage to
water pipelines, but by a decrease in service water pressure due
to water pump outages [16]. Therefore, the resilience assessment
should be established on a hydraulic model of the WDN.
The operational interdependency of electrical-water coupled
networks was addressed by many studies. The ﬁrst is about the
optimal energy ﬂow [17], [18], [19] and cooperative planning
of an electrical-water coupled network in normal operating
condition [20], [21], [22], [23]. The purpose is to minimize
the operating cost while serving the electrical and water load.
Reference [21] proposed a mixed-integer nonlinear model of
water-energy nexus with the objective of minimizing the oper-
ating cost of water pumps and electrical generators. The study
in [22], [23] co-optimized usual electric loads and water pump
loads considering the baseline demand of electricity and water
customers. An optimization model was developed in [24] to
promote the economic operation of coupled electrical-water
networks considering the water ﬂow dynamics. The second
problem is on the post-disaster restoration of power distribution
system considering electrical-water interdependency. Reference
[10] proposed a restoration strategy of power distribution net-
works by dispatching the power consumption of water pumps
and gas compressors. The study in [25] presents metrics of
infrastructural–operational resilience (IOR) for real-time oper-
ations of WDN and power distribution system under extreme
conditions of serious drought or electric grid failure. Based
on the network interdependency, a set of metrics was intro-
duced to measure the sensitivity of water load loss regarding
electricity loss [12]. In addition to the single-directional fault
propagation, the study in [26] proposed a unit commitment
method of thermoelectric power plants considering the fuel and
water cost under drought events. However, the existing models
in [10], [12], [25], [26] cannot represent the practical fault
propagation mechanism because they simplify the WDN to be a
radial structure or ignore the multi-voltage-level characteristics
of UPN. To our best knowledge, the urban-level electrical-
water fault propagation has not been modeled in existing
literatures.
Considering the fault propagation from UPN to WDN, this
paper proposes a resilience assessment method of UPN. The
method identiﬁes vulnerable components in UPN according to
its cascading effects. The innovations are as follows.
r A tri-stage fault propagation model is proposed to
accurately quantify the impact of the UPN fault on the
transmission function of the WDN. The stages include
the electrical-water network coupling model, the hydraulic
computation model, and the water supply model. Consid-
ering the huge number of nodes, a dimension reduction
method of WDN is proposed to simplify its topological and
energyﬂowmodelsoftheWDNforhydrauliccomputation.
r Based on the fault propagation model, the paper introduces
two UPN resilience metrics in terms of function curve
and sensitivity. Considering customers’ usual demand for
electricity and water supply, the quantity of electrical/water

B. Electrical-Water Fault Propagation
The UPN is more vulnerable to extreme weather events (e.g.,
hurricane, ice coating, torrential rain) because overhead power
lines are more likely to be damaged than underground water
pipelines [27]. Consequently, the WDN operation is affected
by the power outage. On the other hand, although the thermal
power plants consume a pure water, they build the water purify
equipment or supplied by special pipeline instead of absorbing
water from the WDN [28]. Therefore, it is reasonable to study the
single-direction electrical-water fault propagation for resilience
assessment of UPN with the assumption that the WDN is not
damaged by extreme weather.
III. MODELING OF ELECTRICAL-WATER FAULT PROPAGATION
This section proposes a tri-stage model to describe the
electrical-water fault propagation principle.
A. Overview of Electrical-Water Cascading Fault
The UPN fault affects water customers in three stages. First,
the damage in the substation or overhead lines leads to the outage
of coupling elements (WPS and pumps in building). Second,
the absence of a water pump causes a decline in nodal water
head. Finally, customers in buildings lose their water supply.
Hence, the tri-stage electrical-water fault propagation model can
be expressed by three independent functions.

IV. RESILIENCE ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK
This section proposes a resilience assessment method of UPN
based on the electrical-water fault propagation model. The UPN
resilience is measured by its function loss and cascading effect
under a sufﬁcient number of representative fault scenarios.

the zonal average water head differs signiﬁcantly. The result is
analyzed as follows.
r Scenarios with small water head decrease: The similarity in
scenarios 1, 4, 7 and 10 is that the water head only decrease
a small portion in Z1 and Z2. Hence, these two zones are
not sensitive to pumps P1, P2 and P4. In scenarios 1 and
10, the water head of Z3 and Z4 even increase by a little
portion. This is because P2 and P4 mainly boosts the water
head for Z5 and their effect contradict with other WPSs
that are located at the southern part of the WDN.

the average water head of Z4 decreases below 10 m and
P5 quits operation. Thus, there is an obvious correlation
between these two events. The signiﬁcant water head de-
crease occurs for two reasons: 1) the high-elevation zone
Z4 is a receiving end of the WDN, and 2) the water head
of Z4 highly relies on P5 that locates at the main water
delivery path to this zone. Therefore, in the meshed WDN,
some zones are sensitive to one or two WPS due to the
ground elevation and the network topology.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposes an integrated resilience evaluation
method of UPN considering its function correlation with WDN.
The tri-stage fault propagation model is ﬁrst proposed to ac-
curately quantify the impact of multi-dimensional UPN fault
on the horizontal and vertical distribution of affected water
customers. The fault propagation model illustrates the water
customers’ internal dependency on UPN. This is the ﬁrst con-
tribution of the paper. The typical fault scenarios are selected
according to their fragility feature and impacts. Based on the
affected water load and electrical load that are normalized to
be comparable, resilience metrics are proposed to quantify the
time-of-use function curve of UPN and the impact of UPN
fault on WDN. The case study on real-world UPN and WDN
indicatesthesensitivityofWDNinterruptionwiththeUPNfault.
Furthermore, the study extends the concept of power system
resilience considering the correlation between power system and
other infrastructure networks. The resilience assessment result
is an essential guidance for the resilience-oriented planning and
post-extreme-event restoration of UPNs.
With the consideration of the power supply dependency of
WDN, the optimal UPN restoration scheduling will be the future
research direction.
