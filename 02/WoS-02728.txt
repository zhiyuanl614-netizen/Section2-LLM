ABSTRACT Technological advancement plays a crucial role in our daily lives and constantly transforms
the industrial sector. However, these technologies also introduce new security vulnerabilities to Industrial
Control Systems (ICS). Attackers take advantage of these weaknesses to infiltrate the ICS environment. The
size of the targeted industry and the attacker’s knowledge of the internal ICS environment are crucial factors
in determining the degree of impact. Researchers and industry professionals have taken several initiatives to
identify and address security problems in the ICS environment; however, to our knowledge, a comprehensive
survey of this landscape has yet to be conducted. Existing surveys have limitations since they mainly focus on
specific aspects of ICS security rather than covering the security aspects holistically. This paper aims to cover
all aspects of security in ICS by classifying the ICS environment into its components, such as SCADA, PLC,
DCS, RTU, HMI, MTU, etc. The paper then discusses the vulnerabilities in the modern ICS environment,
including those of the specific components. The article also presents a classification of ICS-specific attack
types. Furthermore, the study examines real-world attack scenarios in the industrial critical infrastructure
sectors, including energy, power, water, and wastewater. This study provides an in-depth analysis of ICS
security that empowers researchers and industry practitioners to comprehend the complexities of ICS security
and to strengthen the ICS environment’s resilience proactively.

II. BACKGROUND AND SURVEYS ON THE ICS SECURITY
In this section, we studied the background of ICSs and
current surveys on ICSs. This section highlights previous
research work and current studies on ICS. There was an
isolated environment in traditional ICS that focused on safety.
Each level was secured to stop the process if there was
something wrong. Technological advancements with IoT
devices, internet protocols, and wireless advancements in an
ICS environment reduced isolation from other worlds [23].
However, there were several vulnerabilities because of
insufficient security measures in the ICS environment.
Generally, field sites and control centers are compromised by
an ICS environment. PLCs, sensors, and actuators are in field
sites that control and monitor the industrial procedures [24].
The PLC’s structure is not so complicated; it consists of a
power supply, input and output modules, and memory to store
the control logic program. An ICS state is perceived from the
input module by gaining the sensor’s information or values,
and then signals are transferred to the actuators following
the control logic program to overcome the control physical
process.
There are several security issues with SCADA, PLCs,
and hardware components used in an ICS environment
because these components have no self-security mechanisms.
However, the deployment of IDSs in an ICS environment is
useful. Therefore, there are still issues with ICS protection
at the SCADA, PLC, and hardware stages because of the
feasibility of the structure. Furthermore, Hardware devices
under the PLC’s control can be monitored, limiting the IDS
detection performance deployed on the PLCs [25]. Generally,
IDSs are used at the junction or bypass of the ICS network.
In Industry 4.0, web technologies, Ethernet, and TCP/IP
are compromised in an ICS environment. Authors in [5], [26],

2) SUPERVISORY CONTROL AND DATA ACQUISITION
(SCADA) SYSTEMS
SCADA is an old field that existed before the existence of
transistor wire-based telephone systems [128]. Viscode was
the first SCADA invented by North Electric Company in
1950. With the invention of transistor, SCADA gradually
developed which led to mini and high power computers [128],

SCADA is used to control and monitor equipment or
plants in industries like water and waste control [130],
telecommunications [131], transportation [132], oil and gas
[133] and energy [134] etc. These systems cover data
exchange between a SCADA central host computer and a
number of remote terminal units (RTUs) or PLCs, as well
as between the operator terminals and the central host.
A SCADA system is useful to gather information, transmit
the information to a central site, and warn the station about
leakage [135]. It requires mandatory analysis and control,
such as finding critical leakage and showing the information
organized and logically. Such systems can be very simple,
like one that monitors the environmental condition of a small
building or the entire process of a nuclear power plant or
wastewater treatment plant. In the past, SCADA systems
have used public switched networks for monitoring. But
these days, several systems are monitored by a WAN (wide
area network) or LAN (local area network). For monitoring
purposes, there is wide use of wireless technologies [136],
[137].
SCADA has three key parts: remote terminal units, HMI,
and master terminal units (MTU) [40]. Usually, a SCADA
system consists of remote terminal units (RTUs) and PLCs
that interface to local control switch boxes, actuators, and
sensing devices, as shown in figure 4. A communication
system such as radio, satellite, telephone, cable, etc., is useful
to transfer data between control units, field data interface
devices, and the SCADA central host computer machine.
The central host computer server is also known as the
MTU. A SCADA system also consists of human-machine
interface (HMI) software for operational hold applications to
provide a central host, support communication systems, and
monitor and control remotely available field data [6], [138].
A motivational example of a SCADA security attack is the
Florida Wastewater treatment attack, in which the attacker
increased the NaOH quantity in the plant. This increment was
at the risk level. NaOH is used to balance the PH value of
water, but its large quantity in water can lead to death. In this
attack, the attacker first gained access to the SCADA and then
to the HMI program to disturb the control environment and
vary the NaOH quantity. Another example is the Ukrainian
industrial power grid attack [139], in which the attacker sent
malicious signals from the SCADA host to RTU control,

4) REMOTE TERMINAL UNIT (RTU)
RTU is a part of a distribution automation, or SCADA,
system. It is a unit of a microprocessor or microcontroller that
has a SCADA-based architecture and whose goal is to get data
from sensors installed in plants [145]. Data is transferred to
the remote MTU by communication. This data can be sent
through wireless or wired communication. RTU connects the
objects in the physical world [146], [147] with an automation
system. It permits plant control or monitoring. RTU’s role
is to permit the operators of the network control system
to provide historical data for fault findings and network
planning, operate and manage power systems, and trigger
maintenance. The key difference between RTU and PLC
is that PLCs control machinery by reading input signals
and executing pre-written programs to produce a particular
output. In contrast, RTUs gather data from machines and
transmit it back to a central place for analysis. RTU consumes
less power than PLC, works on varying voltages, and is not
limited to 24V DC and 230V AC. Location is one of the key
advantages of RTU [148].
The hardware of an RTU consists of analog and digital I/O,
a CPU, memory, a power supply, a counter, a digital output
interface, and other tools for supporting RTUs [145], [147].

General functions consist of checking and monitoring

A. ICS VULNERABILITIES BY COMPONENTS
With the advancement of technology and the increasing role
of IT and OT in industry, ICS components seem insecure.
In recent years, most vulnerabilities in ICS components have
been found in HMI, SCADA IEDs, etc. With the rapid
pace of technology, vulnerabilities in ICS are increasing.
Two vulnerabilities that were reported in 1997 were the
first ICS component vulnerabilities that were made publicly
available. Since then, there have been a considerable number
of newly discovered vulnerabilities each year. This indicator
has risen over the past few years. According to [163] and

1) VULNERABILITIES IN SCADA
SCADA is useful in almost all major industries, such as water
and treatment plants, railways, aerospace, power, air traffic
control, etc. Using a SCADA system, one can easily get
real-time process control from actuators, sensors, and other
linked devices. SCADA systems are no exception when it
comes to malware attacks; we can say that they are soft targets
for an attacker in ICS. Targets with specifically developed
malicious code can compromise ICS systems, and malware
can compromise with worms, ransomware, Trojans, etc.
According to [165], about 230k+ people have been affected
by a single SCADA attack in 2015 on Ukraine’s power grid
using BlackEnergy 3 malware [166]. This attack left 230k+
people without power for almost six hours. In this attack, the
SCADA system was left non-functional. Globally, this attack
alarms countries.
As SCADA systems are increasing rapidly in industry
and are linked with IT networks, threats are also increasing
rapidly. If one wants to get control of the overall industrial
system or wants to disturb the industrial production system,
he targets the SCADA system. Through this SCADA system,
the attacker can easily control the production of industries.
Similarly, the key objective of a DDoS attack on an industrial
plant is to disturb the production system. Similarly, in a
SCADA system, one requires high-level authorization to pass

4) RTU VULNERABILITIES
RTUs are SCADA system components that act as the ‘‘eyes,
ears, and hands’’ of the electrical grid. They serve as the
‘‘eyes and ears’’ for the master unit, collecting data from
various pieces of equipment, processing it, and transmitting
it via data transmission protocols. They help the master
unit comprehend how the power system functions. The
RTU, where these control commands are then carried out,
validates the analogy between ‘‘hands’’ on the master unit
and control instructions received from the RTU and passed
to the equipment [197], [198].
In the last few years, SCADA, critical infrastructure
systems, and MODBUS-based systems have been the main
targets of attackers in various ways. The attacker aims
to damage the industry’s physical processes or production
systems. Because of OT and IT, there are a number of vul-
nerabilities in these systems, and such vulnerabilities are soft
targets for attackers. A common application layer protocol
for MTU-to-RTU communication is MODBUS, consisting of
MODBUS/TCP for Ethernet networks and MODBUS RTU
or MODBUS ASCII for serial port networks. As a result,
when SCADA and CIS systems’ foundational MODBUS
protocols are damaged, damaged systems could result in not
only significant harm but even fatalities since these systems
are responsible for crucial functions. Cyberattacks against
industrial system control network applications frequently
take four forms: denial of service, response injection,
command injection, and reconnaissance attacks [199], [200].
The MODBUS serial protocol’s primary issue is the lack
of built-in security protection. There isn’t a built-in command
for finding connected devices or checking their legitimacy.
Additionally, the communications are transmitted in plain-
text form, which may be read without encryption and does
not need to be decoded. In [201], the authors studied false
data injection attacks and focused on RTU-level threats. In an
RTU attack, RTU measurement can be changed to create a
system view and credible state, and the attacker can also
gain access to the system network. Attack classification has
been studied in [202] in which threats against RTU sense
the data that occurs in communication networks or directly
on RTU to overcome the data [203]. A serious security
vulnerability named CVE-2023-2131 was reported to The
US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)
[198], and Radboud University reported this issue to CISA.

Cyber attacks are widely disturbing and interrupting the

Unauthorized access of a third party to a system or network
is known as a cyber attack, while the person who carried
out this third party or attack is known as a hacker/attacker.
Cyber attacks have several negative effects because they
breach data, resulting in data manipulation or loss. Several
industries are losing customer trust because of cyber attacks,
so the term used to overcome cyber attacks is cyber security.
Network, computer, and their component protection from
unwanted digital access is known as cyber security. RQ2
is about attack way, In any ICS or network system, the
attacker find any vulnerability to get access and to control
the system. This vulnerability becomes a leading way for
attackers to overcome the system. There are several reasons
for an attacker to launch an attack, such as gaining control
of the system, financial gain, sabotage, and espionage.
Sometimes, attackers attack to prove their skills or find
network vulnerabilities. RQ3 is about the reason behind the
attacker mindset. There are several positive and negative
aspects behind the attacker mindset. The positive aspect is
this attacker is an expert hired in an industry, and his goal
is to find weak and strong vulnerabilities in his network.
To do this, the attacker can experience himself within his
network to find vulnerabilities and then report them to the
industry. There are several negative aspects, such as getting
control of the industry, industry financial loss, or challenging
opponents’ security. RQ4 is about the results of cyber attacks
and how these attacks happened. In a cyber attack, an attacker
can easily access the ICS and gain computer control, which
is called unauthorized access. Through this unauthorized
access, attackers can modify, steal, or delete the data, which
may cause a huge loss. The second part of RQ4 is for
attack classification, and below is the detailed classification
of attacks in an ICS.
There are numerous ways for a cyber attack to get
control of the system. Attackers may get access through

IX. ATTACK METHODS IN AN ICS ENVIRONMENT
With the progress of technology around the globe, indus-
trial plants’ manufacturing and production themselves have
become linked, and being aware of the risks linked with IIoT
installation is important. Industry containing launching IIoT
or manufacturing start-ups or linking already available tech-
nology with advanced technology like remote and automated
monitoring or remote access will require considering the
attack vectors and potential risks linked with those decisions.
Here, we would like to explain the associated risks with the
ICS environment.
A. DEVICE HIJACKING
Device hijacking happens when an attacker takes control of
an ICS endpoint device or sensor without notifying the owner
about this breach. It depends upon the device quality as to how
smart it is at the endpoint, and this device hijacking differs
from condition to condition as to how many risks it injects.
In the case of sensor or endpoint compromise with malware
or ransomware, attackers may find a feasible way to get
control of the ICS environment at the endpoint. It is possible
only if the end-point device is automatically controlled and
functioning or any internet-connected product function is
linked to that end-point. It usually happens if there is no
timely updating of IIoT devices. It can be the primary point
for an attack that injects into the overall network by initiating
at an endpoint and using that device to control the centralized
network or ICS environment. Several devices in warehouses
or manufacturing plants rely on old technology, which is hard
to update with the advancement of technology. The major
reason for relying on old technology is cost efficiency. Many
industries are unwilling to install new devices because of the
expensive systems and the need to hire experts. A hardware-
based VPN solution is considered the only way to provide
security to data or information, and the ICS component is
compatible with old technology.
B. SIPHONING ATTACK
Data siphoning targets the data transmitted by an industrial
IoT device rather than an end-user, similar to an ‘‘eavesdrop-
ping’’ attack. In this scenario, attackers eavesdrop on network
traffic returning from the endpoint device to the main network
to obtain data they shouldn’t have access to. Sensitive data is
concerning when it is sent in, and the ICS environment may
be an issue if it goes into an attacker’s hands, making it a
concern in industries like healthcare, aerospace, and defense.
If the device transmits data that could give a rival access to
crucial intellectual property, there is also cause for concern.
C. DENIAL OF SERVICE ATTACKS
In IIoT devices, the general risk is distributed denial of service
(DDoS) attack potential across the internet network or all
devices. In this attack, the attacker can use the centralized
network device to get inside access and then overload the
endpoint traffic, which may leave the target incomplete.
Such an attack just renders ICS or IIoT endpoint devices as
beneficial as blocks. It is not feasible for the industries that
rely on such devices to be operational for proper products or
for production to continue in the field.
D. DATA BREACHES
In this attack, attackers usually use the IIoT device as an
entry point into the network, where important and sensitive
data is already available. The large attack surface in IIoT
components makes them a target to attack or to enter
a wide industrial network. Attackers may use them as
a door to enter the network and gain access to secure
data such as client or partner data, personal identification
information, financial data, healthcare data, or trade secrets.
An important way to overcome this attack is to make the
IIoT devices used in an ICS environment more secure and
protective [254].
E. DEVICE THEFT
Theft of physical devices themselves is another concern in an
ICS security environment. This risk is greatest when endpoint
devices store sensitive data that may be problematic if it is in
the wrong hands. One cannot ignore such a serious attack.
Usually, such attacks happen through social engineering
gateways. IIoT device installation makes it secure from this
risk to store important information at the endpoint without
relying on the cloud or network.
F. MAN-IN-THE MIDDLE ATTACK
Such an attack has attacker potential by keeping them
between the ICS environment and representing the device to
send the data. The communication flowing from an endpoint
device should be of particular concern if it could be used
to modify production data or manage a product in the field.
In this case, if an attacker claims to be an IIoT sensor and
sends back wrong information that causes production plants
or machines to vary their collaboration, that might result in a
faulty ICS environment.

General attack models consisting of network access to
the control zone mitigate the attacker’s access to the ICS

network control by exploring vulnerabilities in the devices
interconnected through the internet. Malicious actors can take
various actions and be divided into network attack subclasses
or risks.
B. SECURITY STRATEGY LAYER
Dividing the network into various zones, stages, or levels
is the best solution for a secure ICS environment. IT and
OT are the two major sides of cybersecurity barriers and
threats. An ICS tries to meet and mitigate the security solution
provided in an ICS’s IT and OT environment. For an ICS
strategy, an effective program is layered protection or defense
in depth. Such a method can secure the ICS environment
from any big loss, and in the layering security environment,
attacks will affect only specific layers, not the whole ICS
environment.
1) RISK ASSESMENT
The basic concept of security begins with understanding the
risks, prioritizing them, and forming a consensus among
them. The primary step is to define the security strategy,
but it is not the only action. An industry must have the
scale to review security practices from time to time, allocate
resources, define security requirements, and avail itself of the
required tools in security defense mechanisms. It can be a
difficult and demanding stage. The industry must evaluate
security measurements with minor or major changes on time.
2) TECHNOLOGY CONNECTIVITY
In IIoT and Industry 4.0, more interoperability between
networked components is achieved in the OT than in siloed
networking. Generally, the most sensitive parts of an OT
network can be severely affected by cyberattacks. Isolated
OT networks are safer. While IT and OT overlap, it is
inevitable because of event handling and remote monitoring
fluctuations. For risk minimization, the industry must secure
the IT and OT networks that overlap with the assistance of
tools like unidirectional gateways and firewalls, which ban
communication for event handling and monitoring. For a
time, the IT network should not send operational commands
to sensors in the OT network. There are also suggestions
to divide the from/to input and output OT networks into
individual channels.
C. NETWORK LAYER DEFENSE
Network layer defense refers to a security system that uses
various devices or components to protect operations on
various levels, layers, etc. It is also considered an in-depth
defense. The basic rule of network security should be to
deny all and permit none. Then, ports and devices should
be between the various layers in the environment for secure
communication.
1) NETWORK OR ZONES DIVISION
The key concept for better security in an ICS environment is
to divide the network into zones or stages based on security
needs. It is possible to use a virtual local area network or
subnets within the same network, in which each level shows
an overall individual network. Subdivision limits the attack
power or impact to a single zone, and there are fewer chances

to inject into or enter other zones.
2) NETWORK TRAFFIC MONITORING (NTM)
For a secure and reliable network, it is necessary to ensure the
visibility of internal, outgoing, and incoming network traffic
to detect the threat automatically and to know the impact
and context of the threat. There may be several remote sites
in an ICS environment, making it complicated to overcome
the incident. Ideally, security should be divided into two or
more layers in network monitoring, such as enterprise and
remote site layers. Remote sites cover the applicable primary
security measures, like firewalls, hardening, and antivirus.
Furthermore, there should be a SOC that gathers events
from various sites, develops a real-time picture, fuses them,
and runs an AI-based algorithm based on overall system
performance to detect the cyberattack. A new and developed
method can look for irregularities in various OT network
layers, from HMI and ICS to sensors and networks. Another
technique will be implemented for self-healing processes
in remote sites. Collect data from different security tools
to receive a clean image of network inside activity, threat
vectors, and user accounts and identify that several attacks
cross a number of IT systems.
Gaining this view level might be a bit complicated with
the assistance of old security operators. The industry must
install new technology-based tools that assist in monitoring
network traffic at all times, detect automatic malicious
activity, and either inform the security staff or automatically
respond.
3) SECURITY DEVICES INSTALLATION
Place the maximum firewall in each zone of the ICS
environment. Firewall placement at the network edge is
insufficient. If there is no possibility of deploying the
maximum firewall at each zone, then using built-in firewalls
in routers and switches is suitable. Deployment of cloud
services and anti-DDoS at the network edge is profitable.
Much care is needed to find a suitable place to deploy
load balancers. If load balancers are deployed outside the
demilitarized zone (DMZ), network security may not protect
them well.
4) INTERNET ACCESS VIA PROXY SERVER
Never permit unchecked internet access for network users.
Use a proxy to pass the requests, control them, and
monitor users. Make sure all connections are made by
humans, not robots or automatic machines. To guarantee that
corporate users may only visit websites you have specifically
authorized, whitelist domains.
5) DECEPTION TECHNOLOGY USAGE
It is hard to recommend a network with 100% security and
surety. Attackers have success entering the ICS environment.
To know this, use deception technology that convinces
attackers to attack, creates decoys across the network
environment, and provides a way for you to know their
techniques and strategies. These decoys can be helpful in
attack detection in the overall ICS environment, like network
connections, network files, and identification.
6) ATTACK SURFACE AND THREAT HUNTING MANAGEMENT
To scan the network externally or internally, static network
analysis makes it possible to look for known flaws and
incorrect setups that attackers may exploit. There are various
types of scanners with differing qualities, and out of these,
orchestration is the better approach that can get a real-time
picture of the network and scan it accordingly. Logs and
events from network nodes and security tools should be
continuously gathered, merged, and analyzed to find assaults
for dynamic network analysis.
7) PATCH AND ASSET MANAGEMENT
Usually, an old ICS system consists of vendor relationships
that have existed for a long time. Extension of system
components with modern technology causes security vulner-
abilities. Maintaining the software update list with overall
applications and usage is good practice. This updated list is
useful for identifying sensitive resources, managing patches
and updates, and selecting tools that provide better security
for the network. The list first prioritizes and highlights the
applications and hosts that need to be patched and upgraded.
Ensure there are no infected patches; more than one tool
should be used to scan each tool.
8) BEHAVIOURAL ANALYSIS
Zero-day identification can be done through artificial intel-
ligence [256] algorithms. ML
techniques are useful for
learning users’ common behavior and network entities,
as well as learning common contact between them and
detecting irregularities such as accessing uncommon ways
in a network, off-time activity, using rare protocols, and
downloading large amounts of data.

A. FUTURE TRENDS OF CYBER SECURITY
After COVID-19, most industries and organizations have
preferred to work from home for a long time. There are some
future cyber security trends.
1) WORKING REMOTLY
Several organizations and industries’ environments suddenly
changed after the COVID-19 pandemic when workers started
to work from home. Some organizations closed their physical
offices booked for employees and prefer to facilitate them
to work remotely. And some organizations permit their
employees to work from anywhere, part-time or full-time.
Several organizations transferred to the cloud. It was a
temporary setup during the pandemic, and the basic objective
was to keep running the organization. But this temporary
setup will transform into a permanent setup that works from
anywhere in the world. There is a cyber security challenge
for this organization’s long-term policy. Security control,
security events visibility, and compliance with security needs
are the key challenges from this perspective.
Before moving an organization to remote work, ensure
that employees are well-educated and aware of cyber

2) INDUSTRIAL AUTOMATION
There has been no winning situation for the security teams for
several years. It is complicated for them to be updated with all
kinds of threats [274]. There is an increase in the demand for
cybersecurity professionals in various industries. To adjust
with advanced tools [275], and technology, instead of trusting
human beings, industries and organizations should rely on
automation. Machine learning and artificial intelligence use
security technologies, which do regular data analysis on
monitoring screens, so new threats can be easily detected first

activities which the common human eye cannot do. In the
same way, it is easy to find security vulnerabilities, errors,

Increased automation technology and automation quality
minimize the cyber security burden on the experts. Experts

3) PROVIDING QUICK RESPONSE CAPABILITIES
There is a requirement to enhance the response capabilities
within the organization. There must be secure and trusted
communication between various levels of industry and secu-
rity experts. Generally, organizations are attacked through
ransomware, and attackers demand large ransoms. In another
way, these attackers gather large amounts of sensitive data,
conduct breaches, and ask to pay large amounts to prevent or
sell it.
Industries should have a plan to respond to large-scale
incidents [276] quickly. It shows that any sudden incident
quick response is not just with the security experts but also
with the administration, public affairs [277] and legal counsel
to ensure a quick and smooth response.
4) ZERO TRUST POLICY
Zero Trust is the new name of an old concept [278]; don’t
consider anything or anyone could be trusted. Confirm the
trustworthiness of each service, device, entity, and user before
allowing them, and continuously re-verify the trustworthiness
to confirm that there is no compromise; re-verification can
even be done during access. Provide limited and required
access to each entity to minimize the security risk. This
zero-trust policy can reduce the increase of cyber-attacks.
Zero trust is a part of security technology or control but
is an organizational principle. It depends on the complete
technology infrastructure being built to verify each entity’s
identity and security posture repeatedly and to continuously
watch over the actions each entity is involved in. This
calls for extensive collaboration between security architects
and engineers, system and network administrators, software
developers, and other tech experts. There is growing demand
to implement zero-trust principles as soon as feasible,
yet implementation is nearly always a staged, multiyear
endeavor. You should yield to pressure in this situation.
5) IDENTIFY SUPPLY CHAIN RISKS
We purchase the devices, components, or anything else
required from vendors without any doubt. Organizations
trust the vendors and suppliers and use the devices without
verification. It is called blind trust. Blind trust is dangerous
for any organization. It can be risky for the organization.
In this case, companies breach data, lose customer trust,
and sometimes shut down. It is not so easy to address
this issue, but there are several aspects to overcome the
issues [279]. At this point, it is important for the organization
to acknowledge and recognize threats or risks from the supply
chain [280]. Individual organizations can increase awareness
of these problems and pressure vendors and service providers
to do better by, for example, holding vendors accountable
for poor security practices that result in compromises,
requiring more transparency into vendors’ security practices
before renewing contracts, or adding requirements to new
procurements.
