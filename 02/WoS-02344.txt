Abstract—This article investigates the operation of an
integrated electrical-water system for wind power accom-
modation. The efﬁcient operation can be considered as how
well to handle the binary variables in a water distribution
system (WDS) model. The optimization problem of WDS
is NP-hard, and it often takes hours to ﬁnd the optimal
solution. Since wind power varies in minutes, long com-
putation time may hinder the WDS from being fully used
by power system to deal with the wind power ﬂuctuation
and uncertainties. This article develops a three-step opti-
mization model to answer the challenges of the WDS op-
timization problem. The solution hinges on the calculation
of the binary variable values in advance. The ﬁrst step is
to construct a linear power-WDS co-optimization model to
ﬁnd the optimal electrical demand of WDS. The second
step develops a tank ﬂow allocation based method and a
greedy-based algorithm to determine the binary variable
values of WDS. This allows the WDS optimization model to
be efﬁciently solved by commercial solvers in the third step.
The proposed three-step model converts the NP-hard opti-
mization problem of WDS into a polynomial-time problem,
which can be solved quickly. This allows the wind power
system to leverage the energy ﬂexibility provided by WDS
in the real-time operation. Results of case study show that,
compared with existing methods, the proposed method can
reduce the wind power curtailment and the operation cost
by about 55% and 53%. More importantly, the solution time
is reduced dramatically from hours to seconds, suitable for
practical application.
Index Terms—Binary variable, efﬁcient operation, inte-
grated electrical-water system, power variation range, re-
newable energy.

I. INTRODUCTION
R
ENEWABLE energy has been widely used to build eco-
friendly power system, and is estimated to meet 40% of the
global electricity consumption by 2030 [1]. Renewable energy
such as wind power, however, is uncertain and highly volatile
compared with the power generated by conventional thermal
generating units.
Various models have been developed for wind power forecast-
inganduncertaintyquantiﬁcation,suchastheroughautoencoder
[2], the deep belief network [3], the interval probability distribu-
tion learning [4], and the convolutional graph autoencoder [5].
These models can extract quantitative features from historical
data of wind power output considering the uncertainties of data.
Although accurate wind power forecasting can be obtained by
use of the above models, high penetration of wind power has
brought great challenges to the balance of supply and demand
in the power system [6]. It is in urgent need of ﬁnding effective
means for efﬁcient operation of the wind power system.
Water distribution system (WDS) has appeared as a promising
answer to the challenge mentioned previously. Several reports
and pilot programs have shown that the integration of WDS
with the power system could balance the wind power variations
and reduce the wind power curtailment [7], [8], [9], [10]. WDS
accounts for 5% of the annual total electricity consumption in
China, and up to 7.1% in California, USA [11], [12]. The elec-
trical demand of WDS can be adjusted ﬂexibly by coordinating
the operation of water pumps and tanks in the WDS. The water
pump enables the increase of electricity consumption during the
period of high wind power output and the decrease of electrical
demand when the wind is scarce. The water tank is used to
maintain the water balance in the WDS. More wind power can
be incorporated into the power system through the optimization
of WDS.
The components of WDS can be formulated mathematically
and the cooperative operation model of the WDS and power
system has been developed [13], [14], [15], [16]. The energy
ﬂexibility of WDS was leveraged to minimize the overall op-
eration cost [17], [18]. The optimal bidding strategy for WDS
to participate in the electricity market was also proposed [19],
[20], [21]. The optimization model is generally formulated as a
mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem [14],
[15]. It consists of the binary variables and the continuous vari-
ables. The MINLP problem is NP-hard and unable to obtain the
solutions quickly using the commercial solvers such as Gurobi
[22]. Two factors affect the efﬁciency of solving the MINLP
model of WDS [23]. One is the nonlinear terms in the hydraulic
equations of the water pipe and pump [14]. Another factor is the
binary variables describing the direction of ﬂow in the pipe and
the ON/OFF status of the pump and the valve [7].
Many efforts have been made to tackle the nonlinear terms in
the WDS model with some success, such as the piecewise linear
approximation [24], the second-order cone relaxation [25], the
convex approximation [26], and the quasi-convex hull relaxation
[27]. These methods transform the original MINLP model into
a mixed-integer second-order cone programming model or a
mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model.
The binary variable concern in the MINLP model, however,
remains unresolved. The quasi-convex hull relaxation method in
[27] could remove the binary variables denoting the direction of
ﬂow in the pipe from the model, but not the binary variables
indicating the ON/OFF status of the pump and the valve. In
addition, the quasi-convex hull is not an exact representation of
the original pipe model and may result in the water ﬂow solution
infeasible.

The number of binary variables increases drastically as the
size of WDS expands. They split the feasible region of the
optimization model into many discrete spaces and bring hefty
computational expense. As the number of binary variables ex-
pands, the computation time for solving the MINLP model
increases faster than exponential [23]. It usually take hours
to solve the optimization problem of a WDS [7]. The com-
putation time becomes prohibitive when the size of system
becomes very large [14]. Since the output of wind power varies
rapidly, the optimization model of WDS should be solved in
minutes to cope with the wind power uncertainties. The very
long calculation time caused by the binary variables hinders
the WDS from being fully used to optimize the wind power
system operation. This makes the immediate response to the
real-time variations of wind power impossible and poor handling
of wind power uncertainties. As a result, the amount of wind
power curtailment increases greatly, causing huge economic
loss.
To the best of our knowledge, not many researches regarding
the binary variables have been reported. The existing methods
generally consider the binary variables and continuous variables
in the WDS optimization. This complicates the solution ﬁnding
[24], [25], [26], [27]. Inthis article, thesevariables will betreated
separately. The values of binary variables are calculated in
advance.Withbinaryvariablesknown,theoptimizationproblem
only consists of the continuous variables, which can be solved
efﬁciently by commercial solvers.
This article proposes a three-step optimization approach for
efﬁcient operation of the integrated electrical-water system. The
efﬁciency refers to lower wind power curtailment and shorter
computation time. In speciﬁc, the time to obtain the solution
is signiﬁcantly reduced from hours to seconds. To achieve the
efﬁcient operation goal, the ﬁrst step proposes the power-tank
linear (PTL) constraints to describe the maximum variation
range of the WDS power consumption, and develops a lin-
ear power-WDS co-optimization (LPWCO) model using these
constraints. The LPWCO model is used to obtain the opti-
mal electricity consumption proﬁle of WDS. The second step
proposes a greedy-based algorithm to determine the values of
binary variables, ensuring that the optimized electrical demand
of WDS in the ﬁrst step is satisﬁed as much as possible. A tank
ﬂow allocation based method is also developed to reduce the
computation cost of ﬁnding water ﬂow solutions. The third step
solves the WDS optimization model to obtain the optimal water
ﬂow solutions using the binary variable values in step 2.
The main contributions of this article can be summarized as
follows.
1) Develop a three-step cooperative approach to determine
the binary variable values of WDS in advance so as to
facilitate efﬁcient operation of the integrated electrical-
water system.
2) Provide PTL constraints to describe the maximum varia-
tion range of the electricity consumption proﬁle of WDS.
3) Propose a tank ﬂow allocation based method to obtain the
water ﬂow solutions efﬁciently.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. The proposed
optimization approach is described in Section II. It is composed
of three steps. Section III presents the ﬁrst step and Section IV
describes the other two steps. Case studies are given in Section V
to verify the advantages of the proposed three-step optimization
approach. Finally, Section VI concludes this article.

III. LPWCO MODEL
This section develops the mathematical formulation of the
LPWCO model in Step 1 of the three-step procedure. Prior to the
description of the LPWCO model, the PTL constraints of WDS
are outlined. These constraints aim at using linear expressions
to describe the maximum variation range of the WDS power
consumption.
A. PTL Constraints of WDS
The hydraulic constraints from (1) to (16) are nonlinear and
involve the binary variables. They should not be violated by the
variation of WDS power consumption. These constraints face
two shortcomings. One is the computation time is extremely
large when incorporated into the real-time operation model of
the integrated electrical-water system. Another is that these
constraints do not provide an explicit range in which the WDS
electricity consumption can vary.
To alleviate the abovementioned two concerns, the PTL con-
straintsweredevelopedtodescribethemaximumvariationrange
of WDS power consumption. The PTL constraints do not incur
much computation burden in real time. They represent the upper
and lower bounds of the WDS power consumption as explicit
functions of the water volume in the tank, which is critical in
modeling the power variation range of WDS.
The variation range of the electrical demand of WDS is
affected by constaints (13) and (14). Equation (13) describes
the relationship of water volumes in the tank between two
consecutive time instants, while (14) imposes restrictions on the
water volume in the tank. These two constraints imply that the
water volume in the tank at each time instant would have impact
on the allowable amounts of tank ﬂow during the next several
time periods. For example, when WJm,t = W max
Jm , no more water
can be stored by the tank in the next time instant t + 1. This may
lower the upper bound of the WDS power consumption later,
because WDS is prevented from pumping more water into the
tank for storage.

proposed method includes a tank ﬂow allocation based scheme
to ﬁnd the maximum amount of input and output ﬂows of the

the computations in ﬁnding the appropriate combination of

V. CASE STUDY
The proposed approach for efﬁcient operation of integrated
electrical-water system is evaluated to show its merits: much
lower wind power curtailment and signiﬁcantly shorter com-

uncertainty of wind power. Performance evaluation was carried
out on a small-scale illustrative test system and a large-scale test

resolution is 15 min, corresponding to 96 time steps. The tests

9.0.1 on Python 3.7. This article considers the WDS connected
to the power transmission system to evaluate the impact of WDS
optimization on the entire power transmission system [17].

VI. CONCLUSION
This article presented a three-step optimization approach for
efﬁcient operation of integrated electrical-water system. The
efﬁciency is in terms of signiﬁcantly shorter computation time
and much lower wind power curtailment. The ﬁrst step provide
several linear constraints to model the maximum variation range
of the electrical demand of WDS. The optimal electricity con-
sumption proﬁle of WDS was obtained by solving the LPWCO
model. The binary variable values of WDS were determined in
the second step through the tank ﬂow allocation based method
and the greedy-based algorithm. Then, the optimization model
of WDS can be solved efﬁciently. Data privacy can also be
protected, since only limited information needs to be shared
between WDS and the power system.
Results from case studies demonstrated that the proposed
method offers signiﬁcant beneﬁts on lowering the computation
timeandtheoperationcostincomparisontothepreviousstudies.
The computation time remains nearly unchanged even as the
number of WDS integrated with the power system increases
from 93 to 203.
