Coordinated Energy Scheduling in Interconnected
Power-Water-Gas Networks Under Network Failures
Ahmad Alnakhli
, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Payman Dehghanian
, Senior Member, IEEE,
and Mohannad Alhazmi
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—The rapid growth of interconnections between differ-
ent energy systems has seen emerging developments in recent years.
Electric Power System (EPS), Water System (WS), and Natural
Gas System (NGS) have been traditionally operated independently.
However, a multi-sectoral approach is needed to enhance the re-
silience of critical infrastructure systems and reap the economic
beneﬁts. This becomes crucial as natural disasters with massive
power outages have increased in both frequency and magnitude.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a cooperative
joint framework for the operation of the Power, Water, and Gas
Networks (PWGNs) during emergency operating conditions. The
proposed formulation takes into account the nonlinear nature of
both the hydraulic constraints in WS and the gas ﬂow in the
NGS. To reduce the computational burden of the original problem,
linearization techniques are employed to convert the nonlinear
model into a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) for-
mulation. Case studies with different contingency scenarios are
analyzed in the IEEE 24-bus and IEEE 118-bus test systems,
which are jointly operated with a 12-node and 20-node NGS, and
a 15-node commercial-scale WS. Numerical results demonstrate
that the proposed model effectively enhances the resilience of the
interconnected energy systems and reduces the overall operational
costs during emergency conditions. Additionally, results show that
the proposed joint operation model provides signiﬁcant energy
ﬂexibility and is efﬁciently scalable to large networks.
Index Terms—Contingency analysis, electric power system,
water system, natural gas system, interdependency.

I. INTRODUCTION
P
OWER, water, andgas systems playavital roleinproviding
essential utility services to communities [1]. Community
residents rely on the continuous delivery of electricity, gas,
and water at all times. Traditionally, electricity, water, and gas
systems have been planned and operated independently of one
another, while in reality, the operation of these systems is jointly
interdependent [2], [3]. This interdependence is particularly
evident in urban areas, where hydro systems, which contribute to
a small percentage of the generated power, consume a substantial
amount of energy for water pumping and distribution [4], [5].
Similarly, the interdependence between electricity and natural
gas systems has become increasingly vital in recent years, as
demonstrated by the signiﬁcant increase in natural gas deliveries
to electric power consumers in the US. This trend is exempli-
ﬁed by the power sector’s growing share of total natural gas
demand, which has risen from 22.3% to 35.5% over the last
decade [6]. Therefore, treating each system independently might
be inefﬁcient as the occurrence of a failure, particularly in the
case of High-Impact Low-Probability (HILP) events, in one
system could easily cascade into the other systems resulting in
catastrophic consequences to the society [7], [8], [9]. Further,
besides considering the electrical grid, other infrastructures that
are reliant on or impacted by the power grid must also be consid-
ered when assessing the resilience and the operational planning
of the modern integrated energy infrastructure systems [10].
Given the challenges presented by the interdependence of
power, water, and gas networks (PWGNs), a signiﬁcant body
of research has emerged in recent years that focuses on the
optimization of these networks. The primary focus of these
studies has been to minimize the total operational costs, enhance
the system resilience, reduce greenhouse gas emissions and/or
capture the dynamic behaviours of components in the WS. [11].
For the operation of the water and power systems (WPSs), [12]
presented an uncertainty-informed coordinated day-ahead opti-
mization model for integrated power and water networks con-
sidering the hydraulic constraints of the water system and the
prevailing uncertainty in water demand and renewable energy
sources. Reference [13] investigates the utilization of energy
ﬂexibility enabled through the coordination of power and wa-
ter networks. Reference [14] conducted an economic dispatch
analysis of water and power systems by considering the energy
management of various building applications. A comprehensive
study for the operational resilience of the interlinked water and
power networks, considering the scenarios of limited availability
of water and/or energy, is provided in [15]. Several metrics
for determining the operational resilience of interdependent
WPSs under critical conditions are discussed in [16]. In [17],
a risk-based two-stage stochastic co-optimization framework
that coordinates the operation of a renewable-rich power system
with the operation of grid-connected water desalination plants
is proposed to minimize operational costs while increasing the
utilization of renewables.
As for the coupling between EPS and NGS, several works
studied the optimal operation of power and natural gas systems
under different conditions. In [18], the reliability assessment of
the interconnected gas and power systems is explored consid-
ering power-to-gas and gas storage. Reference [19] constructs
a robust scheduling model for wind-integrated energy systems
that takes into account both gas pipeline and N-1 contingencies
in power transmission. An analytical approach incorporating
multiple variables for determining energy ﬂow in natural gas

II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
A. Proposed Framework
In this paper, we propose a day-ahead dispatch model for the
coupled operation of multi-energy systems, i.e., PWGNs. While
this is not yet approached in the current practice, the focus of this
paper is to explore the potential beneﬁts of such a cooperative
approach economically and during emergency operations. To
simplify the problem, we considered the DC model for the
power network. Further, the cost associated with wind power
generation is neglected. In the natural gas network, the gas
ﬂow dynamic equations can be approximated by accounting
for the energy storage capacity in the form of linepack through
variable inﬂows and outﬂows [27]. In the water network, the
proposed model takes into account the hydraulic constraints in
the WS [12], [28], [29]. The high-level inter-dependencies and
coupling between power, water and gas networks are depicted
in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2.

also maintain the key operational characteristics of the water and
gas systems. The following sections describe the linearization
methods used for these two systems in detail, highlighting
how the nonlinear constraints are addressed while ensuring the
feasibility and scalability of the optimization framework.

V. CONCLUSION
This study proposed a novel operational strategy for in-
terconnected PWGNs under emergency conditions, effectively
addressing system interdependencies through efﬁcient lineariza-
tion techniques. By transforming a mixed-integer nonlinear
programming (MINLP) model into an equivalent mixed-integer
linear programming (MILP) formulation, the framework could
reduce operational costs and minimize load shedding while
maintaining system resilience with an affordable computational
resources. The results across multiple case studies and on two
different-size interconnected networks validate the scalability
and robustness of the proposed approach, providing a practical
and efﬁcient solution for joint PWGN operation under complex
contingency scenarios. By leveraging interdependencies among
infrastructures, the model enhances resilience and cost efﬁ-
ciency, establishing a benchmark for coordinated multi-energy
system optimization.
Future research will focus on enhancing the framework’s
applicability to real-time operations and larger-scale systems
while addressing uncertainties in load proﬁles, renewable gen-
eration, and extreme weather events. Expanding the model to in-
corporate multiple system operators and advanced linearization
