Enabling grid services by water distribution systems: Measurement-based 
modeling and optimized scheduling
Stelios Dimoulias∗iD, Georgios Kryonidis iD, Kyriaki-Nefeli Malamaki iD, Pandelis Biskas iD

H I G H L I G H T S
• Simplified, measurement-based modeling approach for water distribution systems (WDSs).
• WDS optimization for interruptible/curtailable load and balancing service provision.
• Two-stage (day-ahead and intra-day) convex optimization, including service activation.
• Application to a real-world WDS in Greece.
• WDSs are suitable for grid service provision, offering significant economic benefits.

A B S T R A C T
Water distribution systems (WDSs) can be optimally controlled and scheduled to provide grid services, such as 
interruptible/curtailable load and balancing services, to the electric power system, by exploiting the flexibility 
of water pumps and the storage capacity of water tanks. Nonetheless, existing frameworks for modeling and 
optimally scheduling WDSs rely on detailed parameter knowledge, involve complex mathematical formulations, 
and do not account for all time horizons associated with the provision of grid services. To address these issues, this 
paper presents a practical two-fold methodology for enabling WDSs to provide multiple grid services. First, a new 
measurement-based modeling approach for WDSs is proposed to eliminate the need for parameter knowledge. 
The approach correlates the electric power consumption and flow rate of pumps via a convex quadratic function, 
enabling simpler yet more accurate modeling. Second, by leveraging this modeling approach, a comprehensive 
two-stage optimization framework is developed to derive the WDS operating schedule, accommodating multiple 
grid services across both the day-ahead and the intra-day time horizons. The proposed methodology is applied 
and validated on a real-world WDS in Greece. The results demonstrate the accuracy of the proposed modeling 
approach, the effectiveness of the proposed optimization framework, and the suitability of WDSs as flexible loads 
for grid services.
1 . Introduction
1.1 . Motivation
The controllability of water pumps and the storage capacity of water 
tanks render municipal water distribution systems (WDSs) highly flexi­
ble loads [1,2] within the electric power system (EPS). In the context of 
the water-energy nexus and integrated energy systems, this flexibility 
enables WDSs to participate in both implicit and explicit demand-
side flexibility schemes [3], allowing them to provide operational 
benefits to the EPS while receiving economic benefits in return. Implicit 
demand-side flexibility schemes include dynamic electricity pricing pro­
grams, whereas explicit demand-side flexibility schemes involve the 
provision of grid services to the EPS [1], such as interruptible/curtailable 
load (I/C-L) [4,5] and balancing services [6].
In this context, the participation of WDSs in demand-side flexibil­
ity schemes constitutes an optimal scheduling problem. Specifically, the 
WDS operating schedule is optimized to minimize the net operating 
cost, based on the structure of the flexibility schemes involved. When 
providing grid services, the associated remuneration is also considered.

1.2 . Literature review: demand-side flexibility schemes
The participation of WDSs in dynamic electricity pricing programs 
has been thoroughly addressed in the scientific literature [7], with stud­
ies focusing on both fixed-speed pumps (FSPs) [8–10] and variable-speed 
pumps (VSPs) [11–14], [15]. Nevertheless, the challenge of providing 
multiple grid services to the EPS has not been adequately addressed, 
as most studies focus exclusively on either I/C-L [16–19] or balancing 
services [20,21].
Specifically, initial studies on the I/C-L capability of WDSs focused 
solely on FSPs [16–18], thereby substantially limiting controllability and 
flexibility. In [19], the participation of WDSs in I/C-L schemes of the 
French market is examined. Although the market structure is thoroughly 
modeled, the physical modeling of the WDS, and especially of the pumps, 
is not detailed.
Regarding balancing services, they involve two stages: the availabil­
ity stage and the activation stage. The availability stage pertains to 
the reservation of upward and downward balancing capacities through 
day-ahead scheduling, while the activation stage concerns the actual 
delivery of these capacities in the intra-day horizon upon request by 
the EPS operator. Nevertheless, the existing studies do not comprehen­
sively cover both stages. To elaborate, in [20], the authors explore the 
ability of WDSs to provide downward-only balancing services by flex­
ibly increasing their power consumption to absorb surplus renewable 
energy. However, this study focuses solely on the activation stage of 
the service, as the capacity to increase the WDS power consumption is 
assumed to be known in advance. In [21], the provision of short-term 
balancing services is examined. Nonetheless, by considering a single, 
symmetric balancing capacity for both upward and downward service 
provision, and quantifying it on a per-pump basis rather than system-
wide, the study underestimates the capability of the WDS to provide 
balancing services. In addition, [21] does not optimize the intra-day 
schedule of the WDS; instead it relies on the third pump affinity law to 
adjust pump power upon activation of the balancing service. However, 
this relationship is approximate and prone to significant errors [22,23].
Finally, in [24], the authors combine both I/C-L and balancing ser­
vices, with the study extended to desalination plants in [25]. However, 
these works are limited to the availability stage of the balancing service, 
neglecting its actual activation. In turn, this could result in violations of 
the tank operating limits during the activation stage.
1.3 . Literature review: WDS hydraulic modeling
Beyond the above points regarding the accommodation of multiple 
grid services, the primary limitation in the existing literature concerns 
the hydraulic modeling of WDSs, specifically the assumption of full 
parameter availability. As later discussed in detail in Section 2.2, state-
of-the-art works [8–14,16–21,24,25] presuppose accurate knowledge 
of the WDS parameters, particularly regarding pumps and pipelines. 
Nevertheless, in practice, this assumption is difficult to justify due to 
the inherent uncertainties of WDSs, which are further exacerbated by 
phenomena such as pipeline leakage and aging. Furthermore, this as­
sumption leads to less accurate WDS modeling, while also increasing the 
complexity of the associated optimization problems for WDS scheduling 
and grid services provision.
A different approach is adopted in [15]. Specifically, the authors at­
tempt to implicitly handle WDS uncertainties related to pump modeling 
by proposing a robust optimization formulation. Nonetheless, robust 
formulations result in more conservative solutions compared to their 
deterministic counterparts [26].

1.4 . Scope and contribution
The scope of this study is to enable WDSs to participate in both 
implicit and explicit demand-side flexibility schemes and to provide mul­
tiple grid services. To this end, the study addresses the identified gaps in 
both hydraulic modeling and scheduling of WDSs, presenting a two-fold 
contribution:
• Measurement-based decoupling of VSP quantities and prob­
lem simplification: To address the limitation of requiring full 
parameter knowledge for WDSs, this paper proposes a new 
measurement-based modeling approach. Specifically, the paper 
demonstrates that the electric power consumption and the flow rate 
of VSPs can be directly and accurately correlated through a con­
vex quadratic function. This function decouples the flow rate and 
electric power consumption of VSPs from other quantities, such 
as rotational speed and hydraulic head. In turn, this decoupling 
significantly reduces the number of decision variables, thereby sim­
plifying the formulation of the optimization problems for WDS 
scheduling. Consequently, the linearizations and relaxations intro­
duced in the literature [8–14,16–21,24,25], which result from the 
inherent complexity of WDSs, can be avoided, allowing for exact 
solutions. Furthermore, it is highlighted that the proposed model­
ing approach aligns with the research guidelines of the IEEE PES 
Taskforce on Water-Power Systems [27,28], which emphasize the 
development of simple yet accurate models for the integration and 
optimization of WDSs and EPSs.
• Two-stage scheduling for multiple services: Contrary to the ex­
isting literature, the paper proposes a comprehensive framework 
that optimizes the operating schedule of WDSs by jointly consid­
ering all three common demand-side flexibility schemes, i.e., (a) 
dynamic electricity pricing, (b) I/C-L provision, and (c) balancing 
service provision. Furthermore, unlike previous studies focusing 
on balancing services [20,21,24,25], the proposed framework opti­
mizes the WDS schedule for both the availability and the activation 
stages of the balancing service. To this end, it is structured as a 
two-stage problem. The first stage concerns the derivation of the 
day-ahead schedule, aiming to (a) minimize the net operating cost 
of the WDS, (b) provide I/C-L service, and reserve balancing capac­
ity, and (c) ensure that tank operating limits are respected during 
the activation of the balancing service. The second stage is related 
to the intra-day activation of the balancing service upon its re­
quest from the EPS operator. The intra-day schedule ensures (a) 
the optimal allocation of pump setpoints for the balancing ser­
vice activation and (b) the cost-effective adjustment of the WDS 
schedule for the remainder of the day. Moreover, contrary to 
[20,24,25], the proposed formulation accounts for the possibil­
ity of pump shutdown, thus enabling increased flexibility. Finally, 
leveraging the proposed measurement-based modeling approach, 
the scheduling problems are formulated as mixed-integer quadrat­
ically constrained programming (MIQCP) optimization problems 
with convex continuous relaxations, facilitating computational 
tractability.
As an additional strength of the study, it is emphasized that both 
the proposed measurement-based approach for WDS modeling and the 
proposed optimization framework for WDS scheduling are applied and 
validated on a real-world WDS in Greece, as presented in Section 4.
Finally, to provide a clear comparison with the existing literature,

the research gaps it addresses.
The remainder of the manuscript is organized as follows: Section 2 
briefly reviews current practices in the modeling of WDSs and presents 
the proposed measurement-based modeling approach. Section 3 in­
troduces the proposed two-stage optimization framework for WDS 
scheduling. In Section 4, the proposed methodology, including both the 
modeling approach and the optimization framework, is applied to and 
validated on a real-world WDS. Finally, Section 5 concludes the pa­
per, summarizing the main findings. In addition, the application of the 
proposed methodology to a benchmark WDS is presented in Appendix A.
2 . WDS hydraulic modeling
2.1 . Preliminaries
In typical municipal WDSs [2,19,29], pumps in pumping stations ex­
tract water from supply sources (e.g., underground wells or reservoirs) 
and transfer it to storage tanks located at higher elevations. In turn, 
storage tanks define pressure zones [30] and supply consumers by grav­
ity. When additional hydraulic head is required to transfer water to the 
tanks, booster pumps are employed.
In this configuration, the operation of a pump is defined by the hy­
draulic head (𝐻) it generates and the water flow rate (𝑄), hereafter 
referred to as flow, that it delivers. In the case of VSPs, the motors are 
equipped with variable-frequency drives on their electric supply side. 
Thus, the rotational speed (𝑛) of VSPs can be controlled in proportion to 
the input frequency of the drive, thereby allowing greater flexibility in 
terms of the generated head and the delivered flow.

affinity laws and the pump characteristic curve. The pump characteristic

2.2 . Current practices
To model WDSs, existing studies [8–14,16–21,24,25] simultaneously 
employ (1), (3), and (4), for all pumps and pipelines. Nonetheless, (2) 
is omitted and a constant efficiency 𝜂 is instead assumed to simplify the 
model. Likewise, a constant 𝜂m value is assumed for the motor. This 
conventional modeling approach exhibits the following drawbacks:
• Dependence on parameter availability: It heavily relies on the 
availability and accurate knowledge of parameters 𝑐1, 𝑐2, 𝑐3, 𝐾, 
and 𝐻𝑜, for all pumps and pipelines. Nonetheless, this assumption 
is difficult to justify for several reasons. First, WDSs are known to 
suffer significant leakage, with average water losses of around 30% 
[32]. The presence of leaks distorts pipeline characteristics, lead­
ing to significant deviations from the theoretical values. In addition 
to leakages, WDS models, if available, require frequent calibration 
and parameter updates [33], to account for discrepancies due to 
pipeline aging and damage. Moreover, the static head parameter is 
subject to uncertainty [34], with errors of a few meters potentially 
causing significant inaccuracies in the calculations of WDS quan­
tities [35]. Finally, although pump curves are usually provided by 
manufacturers, they have been identified as sources of uncertainty 
[15,36].
• Complex problem formulation: In addition to the assumption of 
accurate knowledge of such an extensive set of uncertain WDS 
parameters, the conventional modeling approaches introduce a 
substantial number of variables into the optimization problem of 
WDS scheduling. More specifically, for each pump, 𝑄, 𝐻, 𝑃, and 
𝑛 are decision variables to be calculated, while 𝜂 is incorrectly as­
sumed to be constant. The increased number of variables, along 
with the significant nonlinearity of (1), (3), and (4), renders such 
problems difficult to solve. For this reason, existing studies resort 
to extensive approximations and relaxations to the original prob­
lem, in order to achieve computationally tractable solutions. As a 
trade-off, such approximations result in errors and discrepancies 
with respect to the actual physical model of the WDS.
• Neglect of efficiency reduction: Pump efficiency decreases at re­
duced speeds [23], with the reduction being more significant in 
systems with substantial static head [37]. Thus, assuming a con­
stant 𝜂 leads to important errors in power calculations. Motor 
efficiency 𝜂m also decreases at reduced speeds due to the reduction 
in power, albeit less significantly.
2.3 . Proposed modeling approach
To avoid reliance on uncertain parameters, this paper proposes a 
new measurement-based modeling approach for WDS, deriving a sim­
plified yet equivalent model. The proposed modeling approach is based 
on the principles stipulated in [38], which are also leveraged in [39,40]. 
Specifically, the aim is to decouple the interactions of interconnected 
pumps, pipelines, and storage tanks by implicitly representing the hy­
draulic behavior of the WDS within an equivalent pump operating 
model. The proposed modeling approach can be applied to WDSs with 
the typical configuration described in Section 2.1, which has been 
highlighted for its potential in demand-side flexibility [1].
To elaborate, an on-site, measurement-based, system-learning pro­
cedure is developed, as shown in Fig. 1(a). For each pump-pipeline 
pair in the WDS, this procedure derives a dedicated 𝑃= 𝑔(𝑄) function 
that correlates the flow delivered by the pump with the corresponding 
electric power consumed by its motor. Specifically, commencing from 
the nominal value (𝑓n), the frequency applied to the VSP is iteratively 
reduced in steps of Δ𝑓. For each input frequency, the corresponding 
flow and electric power operating points are recorded and stored in a 
lookup table. The process is repeated until either the pump operating 
flow or frequency is reduced below the rated minimum value (𝑄min,r
and 𝑓min,r, respectively), as specified by the manufacturer. The step 
size Δ𝑓 can be set to 1 Hz or lower to ensure high resolution and ac­
curate modeling. The derived 𝑃= 𝑔(𝑄) curve is fixed for a specific 
pump-pipeline pair. Therefore, the curves need to be extracted only 
once and updated according to the recommended practices for WDS 
model calibration, typically once every one to five years [41], to ac­
count for pipeline aging and damage. Additionally, since modern WDSs 
are equipped with Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition systems, 
non-scheduled calibrations can be performed whenever the flow values 
stored in the lookup tables do not match the measured values, indicating 
model discrepancies. The proposed learning process:
• achieves simpler yet more accurate WDS modeling, as it relies on 
up-to-date measurements and implicitly accounts for the decrease 
in pump and motor efficiencies at reduced speeds.
• decouples decision variables 𝑃 and 𝑄 from 𝐻, 𝑛, and 𝜂, implicitly 
accounting for their interdependence. As a result, the number of 
variables involved in the optimization problems is reduced by 60% 
per pump.

3 . Proposed optimization framework for WDS scheduling
The proposed optimization framework schedules WDSs to enable 
their participation in both implicit and explicit demand-side flexibil­
ity schemes across distinct time horizons, incorporating the proposed 
modeling approach presented in Section 2.3. The framework formulates 
day-ahead and intra-day scheduling problems, which are solved from 
the perspective of the WDS operator to minimize the net operating cost 
of the WDS.
3.1 . Examined demand-side flexibility schemes
As discussed in Section 1.4, the proposed optimization framework 
accommodates three demand-side flexibility schemes: (a) dynamic elec­
tricity pricing, (b) I/C-L service provision, and (c) balancing service 
provision.
As explained in Section 1.1, dynamic electricity pricing is an implicit 
demand-side flexibility scheme. In this scheme, the WDS is encouraged 
Sustainable Energy, Grids and Networks 46 (2026) 102260 
5 

S. Dimoulias, G. Kryonidis, K.-N. Malamaki et al.
to adjust its energy consumption based on the time variation of elec­
tricity tariffs, thereby implicitly contributing to the EPS operation, e.g., 
through load profile flattening.
In contrast, the I/C-L and balancing services are explicit demand-side 
flexibility schemes. To enhance the generality of the proposed frame­
work, different structures are considered for these two grid services. 
Specifically, the I/C-L service involves an agreement for the WDS to 
reduce its energy consumption during specified, predetermined time in­
tervals, in line with schemes such as [42]. These specified time intervals 
are known in advance, i.e., in the day-ahead stage, as they typically cor­
respond to periods with high load demand for the EPS. The available 
power reduction is calculated in the day-ahead stage and delivered in 
the intra-day stage.
On the other hand, the balancing service is structured as a ramping 
or frequency restoration reserve product [43], representing the abil­
ity of the WDS to provide upward and downward reserve capacity by 
decreasing or increasing its power consumption, respectively, in accor­
dance with the generator convention. Specifically, the balancing service 
involves the day-ahead reservation of balancing capacity, and the po­
tential intra-day activation of this capacity at unknown time intervals, 
if requested by the EPS operator. As a result, the activation is stochastic, 
addressing unforeseen conditions in the EPS that cannot be known in 
advance.
Thus, the optimization framework accommodates the joint provision 
of grid services with both deterministic (I/C-L service) and stochastic 
(balancing service) deliveries in the intra-day stage. The differences 
in the purpose and structure of each service are further analyzed in 
Section 3.2.
3.2 . Day-ahead stage
The day-ahead optimization determines the WDS operating sched­
ule for the following day, aiming to minimize the net daily operating 
cost. The actual operating cost refers to the energy consumption of the 
pumps, which is billed under a dynamic pricing scheme. In addition, two 
revenue sources are considered, corresponding to the provision of I/C-L 
and balancing services.
Let N𝑝 and N𝜏 denote the sets of pumps and tanks of the WDS, re­
spectively. Moreover, for each tank 𝜏 in N𝜏, the sets Nin,𝜏⊆N𝑝 and 
Nout,𝜏⊆N𝑝 represent the pumps at the inlet (upstream) and outlet 
(downstream) of the tank, respectively. The set T denotes the daily time 
horizon. The set TI∕C−L ⊆T denotes the specified time period for the pro­
vision of the I/C-L service, and the set TR ⊆T denotes the time period 
for the provision of the balancing service. Typically, TI∕C−L corresponds 
to periods of high load for the EPS, although its exact definition varies 
across different I/C-L schemes. Furthermore, it is assumed that the two 
services do not overlap; thus TI∕C−L ∩TR = ∅ and TR = T −TI∕C−L.
For a pump 𝑝∈N𝑝, the flow and the electric power consumption are 
represented by two sets of variables. In line with (6), continuous vari­

frequencies, while mixed discrete-continuous variables 𝑄𝑘

3.4 . Discussion on modeling
As discussed in Section 2.3 and demonstrated in Section 3.2 and 
Section 3.3, the proposed modeling approach decouples the physical 
quantities of pump hydraulic head and frequency from the day-ahead 
and intra-day optimization problems. To elaborate, the pump input fre­
quency is implicitly represented by the quadratic 𝑃=𝑔(𝑄) curve and is 
calculated a posteriori for each operating point, using the lookup tables 
derived in Step 2 of Fig. 1(a).
Similarly, the hydraulic head generated by the pump is also implic­
itly represented by the 𝑃=𝑔(𝑄) curve. Specifically, each point on the 
curve represents a different valid operating point of the corresponding 
pump-pipeline pair, and thus a different valid and physically consistent 
operating head, as visualized in Fig. 2(a) and (c).
Regarding the entire WDS, as per the principles established in [30,38,
39,46,47], for well-designed WDSs, any pump operating schedule that 
is feasible with respect to the tank water level constraints also satisfies 
the hydraulic head constraints at all junctions. This principle also ap­
plies to consumer junctions downstream of storage tanks. To elaborate, 
consumer water demands can be delivered at an appropriate pressure 
from the storage tanks even when pumps are not operating, since tanks 
are located at higher elevations [47]. Therefore, junctions downstream 
of tanks can be modeled as an aggregate water demand 𝑄c
𝜏[𝑡] to be sup­
plied by the tanks, since the downstream flow is gravity-fed and does 
not involve pump operation or electricity consumption.
As a result, the physically feasible 𝑃= 𝑔(𝑄) curve ensures that 
pumps generate sufficient head to supply the tanks. In turn, SoC con­
straints (10), (14), and (19) ensure that tank water level limits are 
satisfied. Therefore, the proposed optimization framework implicitly 
ensures that hydraulic head requirements are satisfied throughout the 
WDS, in accordance with the principles of [30,38,39,46,47].

4.7 . Discussion
The results of this section demonstrate both the accuracy of the 
proposed modeling approach and the effectiveness of the proposed opti­
mization framework in enabling WDSs to provide multiple grid services. 
The accuracy of the proposed modeling approach ensures that the sched­
ules derived by the proposed optimization framework are consistent 
with the physical laws governing the WDS. Furthermore, from an eco­
nomic perspective, the results highlight the substantial benefits for WDSs 
and underscore the importance of adjusting the pump schedule during 
the intra-day stage. From a technical perspective, the results emphasize 
the significance of incorporating the reserve activation constraints.
Moreover, it is emphasized that the development of the proposed 
measurement-based modeling approach not only addresses the research 
drawbacks identified in Section 2.2; it also tackles the practical prob­
lem of modeling the examined real-world WDS. Specifically, the system 
curves of the Igoumenitsa WDS are unknown to its operator. As a result, 
conventional methods [8–14,16–21,24,25], which rely on the modeling 
assumptions outlined in Section 2.2, cannot be applied, necessitating the 
development of a measurement-based approach.
Regarding the convex relaxation of constraint (11), as explained in 
Section 3.2, in the vast majority of cases, the structure of the opti­
mization problem ensures that the relaxed constraint (16) is satisfied 
with equality, thereby preserving the equivalence between the origi­
nal and the relaxed problems. Specifically, for the examined cases, the 
numerical discrepancy between the calculated power setpoints and the 
corresponding quadratic flow terms in (16) is of the order on 10-7 kW, 
demonstrating exactness. In turn, this convexification achieves a signif­
icant reduction in the computation time. Indicatively, the day-ahead 
problem of Section 4.3 includes 15,604 constraints and 11,993 vari­
ables, 1152 of which are binary. For this problem, the convex relaxation 
decreases the computation time from 41 s to 5 s, i.e., by 88%. This im­
provement in computation time is particularly important for large-scale 
WDSs comprising numerous pumps and tanks, or for applications where 
a small time step is required.
On the other hand, the main limitation of the study is that water de­
mand uncertainties are not systematically accounted for. As analyzed 
in Section 4.6, for Gaussian forecast errors with 𝑠𝑡𝑑e up to 5%, the 
proposed framework ensures satisfaction of the WDS technical limits. 
Therefore, systematically hedging against higher levels of uncertainty

5 . Conclusions
This paper proposes a practical, two-fold methodology to enable the 
provision of multiple grid services by WDSs. The methodology comprises 
two parts: a new measurement-based approach for modeling WDSs, 
and a new two-stage optimization framework for scheduling WDSs, 
encompassing the day-ahead and the intra-day time horizons.
The proposed measurement-based modeling approach addresses the 
main drawbacks of the current modeling practices for WDSs, partic­
ularly the dependence on parameter knowledge and the complexity 
of the associated optimization problem formulations for scheduling 
WDSs. To elaborate, the proposed modeling approach involves an on-site 
measurement-based process that correlates the electric power con­
sumption and the flow of VSPs. This approach ensures more accurate 
modeling, as it relies on up-to-date measurements, while it decouples 
power and flow from the remaining physical quantities, such as hy­
draulic head, and pump efficiency speed. It is shown that the electric 
power and the flow of a VSP can be accurately correlated through a con­
vex quadratic function, enabling simpler formulations for the associated 
optimization problems.
Subsequently, by leveraging the above-mentioned modeling ap­
proach, the proposed two-stage optimization framework addresses the 
main limitations of the existing frameworks, enabling the provision of 
multiple grid services by WDSs. More specifically, I/C-L and balanc­
ing services are examined, along with dynamic electricity pricing. The 
day-ahead optimization problem derives the initial pump schedule to 
minimize the net daily operating cost of the WDS, ensuring stable power 
reduction for the I/C-L service, as well as upward and downward reserve 
capacities for the balancing service. Moreover, contrary to the major­
ity of the existing literature, the day-ahead problem ensures that the 
reserve activation during the intra-day stage does not violate the tank 
SoC limits. Regarding the intra-day optimization problem, it determines 
the optimal pump setpoints for reserve activation and adjusts the pump 
schedule for the remainder of the day to minimize the operating cost, 
while also ensuring the recovery of the tank SoC. Both the day-ahead and 
the intra-day problems are formulated as MIQCP problems with convex 
continuous relaxations, supporting tractable solutions.
The proposed methodology was applied and validated on a 
real-world WDS located in northwestern Greece. First, the pro­
posed measurement-based modeling approach was performed and the 
quadratic curves for the WDS pumps were derived. Subsequently, the 
proposed optimization framework for scheduling WDSs was validated, 
using real data for water demands. The results demonstrate that the 
proposed methodology unlocks the flexibility of WDSs and enables a 
reduction of nearly 90% in their net daily operating cost, compared to 
the current operational practices.
