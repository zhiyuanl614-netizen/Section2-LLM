A B S T R A C T  
The reliable supply of electricity and freshwater is linked to the living quality of local residents in remote islands. 
A renewable driven standalone combined power and water supply system with cascade electricity and heat 
storage is proposed in this paper. The hybrid wind-solar power system acts as the energy source and the hu­
midification dehumidification (HDH) desalination is employed for freshwater production. The underwater 
compressed air energy storage (UW-CAES) is introduced to smooth the renewable fluctuations, whereas the heat 
storage system for heat addition purpose of HDH desalination is utilized to reduce dumped power during UW- 
CAES charging. The simulated results show that the suggested system can satisfy electricity and freshwater 
demand well. The lower loss of power supply probability (LPSP) or loss of water supply probability (LWSP) 
requires larger renewable installed capacity. At same threshold situation, the renewable configuration of LWSP is 
larger than that of LPSP. The performance comparison among different system structures implies that this system 
has lower renewable configuration and total dumped energy. Moreover, the coefficient of performance of heat 
pump (COP), the heat storage system capacity, the basic freshwater demand, the flexible energy bag volume and 
the humidifier seawater inlet temperature have obvious effect on system performance.   
1. Introduction 
The electrical power and freshwater are the crucial necessities for 
human beings. The supply is still a significant challenge for remote 
islands owing to the long distance from the mainland [1]. Nowadays, the 
electrical power in such places is mainly provided by diesel generators 
(DGs) [2]. However, the deployment of DG emits substantial carbon 
dioxide, causing an adverse environmental impact in the local fragile 
ecological surroundings. Meanwhile, the diesel cost in remote island is 
expensive due to the high expense of fuel transportation. Thus, the prime 
power must change to the utilization of renewables, which is a prom­
ising developing trend throughout the world [3]. On the other hand, 
although the freshwater often can be satisfied via the local self-establish 
desalination system, the most of them need consume a large amount of 
energy from fossil fuel [4]. This fact is also not attractive in current 
decarbonized society. Therefore, the renewable driven desalination 
system is put on the agenda [5]. 
In fact, the renewable energy source potential is abundant in remote 
islands. The most common used forms are wind energy and solar energy. 
In the last decades, in spite of the single type of wind or solar can be used 
as the power supply source, the hybrid wind and solar type standalone 
power system has gained more attention in academic and industrial 
communities, due to the complementary characteristic between them 
[6]. Moreover, in renewable driven standalone power system, the en­
ergy storage device is indispensable owing to the stochastic natures of 
renewables. Hence, the hybrid wind-solar system with energy storage, 
which has been proven its high reliability, is the main stream technical 
form for remote areas in current era [7]. Altun et al. [8] carried out a

energy storage for remote areas. The surplus power from renewable was 
converted to hydrogen via electrolyzer, and later was converted back to 
electricity in a fuel cell stack, achieving a high efficiency and reliability.

Freshwater is produced through the desalination technology, which 
can be recognized as the process to remove the dissolved salts and other 
minerals from salty water to produce freshwater [11]. In principle, the 
desalination can be divided into two main groups. The first is the 
thermal-based method, in which a liquid/gas phase change process oc­
curs to evaporate salty water by heat addition, and then the opposite 
gas/liquid phase change process happens to generate distillate as 
freshwater. This type method mainly includes the humidification 
dehumidification (HDH), the multi-effect distillation (MED) and the 
multi-stage flash (MSF). The other is the membrane-based method, in 
which the pressure difference, the salinity difference or electrical po­
tential difference is utilized, containing the reverse osmosis (RO), the 
capacitive deionization (CDI) and the electro-dialysis reversal (EDR) 
[12].

whether thermal-based method or membrane-based method, has been 
transited to renewable driven. Obviously, since the actual freshwater 
demand and renewable energy supply are both fluctuated, the energy 
storage device also is absolutely necessary in renewable driven desali­
nation system [13]. Lots of works have been conducted focusing on this 
topic. Rezk et al. [14] proposed a brackish water pumping and RO

From the above recalled literatures, it’s worth noting that the 
renewable driven standalone power system or desalination plant is 
attractive and promising in the future. To merge both different func­
tional systems in one framework is more attractive and promising. Thus, 
some renewable driven combined power and water systems have been 
investigated in recent years. P. Das et al. [23] proposed a PV/WT/DG 
powered hybrid system with battery storage to supply electricity and 
freshwater. The excess energy from community load was employed to 
run a RO desalination plant, making a reduced cost of energy and raised 
job creation. Kumar et al. [24] conducted an optimization of PV/bio­
mass based hybrid power system with battery storage for rural electri­
fication and RO desalination. Kiehbadroudinezhad et al. [25] raised 
three standalone renewable driven water/power generation configura­
tions, and then a multi-objective optimization taking lift cycle cost, 
environmental impact on human health and reliability into consider­
ation was carried out. The results showed PV/DG/RO with battery 
storage was the cost-effective system and the PV/WT/DG/RO with 
battery storage was the eco-friendly system. Assareh et al. [26] surveyed 
the thermodynamic-economic optimization of a solar thermal driven 
electricity and freshwater production system. This system was made up 
of a concentrated solar thermal driven Brayton power plant, a steam 
Rankine system, an organic Rankine cycle and RO unit. The optimal 
exergy efficiency was 43.18%. 
However, the research of the combined power and water supply 
system driven by renewables still has some limitations, which can be 
summarized as: 
Firstly, the renewable driven standalone system often requires a high 
capacity to meet load demand with superimposed effect for a higher 
reliability, resulting in a substantial amount of surplus electricity is 
dumped to maintain the power balance [27]. This is a diseconomy 
result. Thus, how to use this great amount of dumped power is vital for 
system efficiency. Meanwhile, the wind-solar mix is a better renewables 
configuration due to the complementary characteristics between them. 
However, some researches only deploy the PV, even the polluting DG. 
Secondly, the desalination type in current existing works is almost 
the RO plant. This membrane-based method needs electricity input. 
Thus, the power load and desalination power load have a superimposed 
effect due to the same variation trends, making a higher peak-to-valley 
ratio and thus a larger renewable installed capacity. Meanwhile, the RO 
technology usually has large-scale and complex maintenance features, 
which is not favorable for the small-scale remote island scenario. 
Finally, in most of renewable systems, the battery energy storage 
(BES) unit is the familiar energy storage device. Some drawbacks are 
behind the BES technology, such as the performance degradation after 
long-term operation or in the low-temperature environment, hard 
disposal of discard battery and complicated heat management. Thus, the 
mechanical energy storage technologies with high reliability, such as 
CAES, may be a good choice in remote island. But, the research in this 
aspect is not adequate. 
To fill this research gap, the main objective of this paper is to carry 
out the performance evaluation of a renewable driven standalone 
combined power and water supply system with cascade electricity and 
heat storage for remote islands. In this proposed system, the PV-WT 
configuration is the system power source and the thermal-based HDH 
desalination is selected to produce freshwater. The UW-CAES system 
acts as the electrical energy storage device to smooth out the power 
fluctuation. Meanwhile, the heat storage system based on CO2 heat 
pump is introduced to provide continuous heat power for HDH desali­
nation plant. 
The main contributions can be concluded as following aspects.  
● This suggested standalone system for remote islands focuses on the 
simultaneous supply of electrical power and freshwater to end-users 
by using renewables with the help of cascade electricity and heat 
storage. From the power supply viewpoint, the power source selects 
the wind-solar mix, leading to a reduced installed capacity of re­
newables and a saving cost. The cascade electricity and heat storage 
devices are employed. The surplus electricity is fed to electrical en­
ergy storage device and heat storage device sequentially, resulting in 
a remarkable reduced dumped power and a boosted energy 
efficiency.  
● On the desalination side, the water-heated HDH unit is employed as 
the desalination system, owing to its simple but reliable features. Its 
heat source is the heat storage system based on CO2 heat pump, 
which can provide continuous heat energy to satisfy the freshwater 
demand.  
● From the electrical energy storage level, the UW-CAES system is 
suitable for fluctuant power management in remote islands, 
achieving a better power supply reliability. Simultaneously, as a 
typical mechanical energy storage technology, the UW-CAES has 
high reliability and lifetime. Moreover, the operation pressure in 
UW-CAES keeps constant all the time, resulting in a high efficiency of 
the related pneumatic machineries. 
2. System structure and operation strategy 
2.1. System structure 
The schematic diagram of the renewable driven standalone com­
bined power and water supply system with cascade electricity and heat 
storage is shown in Fig. 1. Obviously, this suggested system mainly 
consists of five sections, which are the power supply system, the UW- 
CAES system, the heat storage system, the water-heated HDH desali­
nation plant and the end-user section (freshwater demand and electrical 
load). 
The power supply system is the sole power source for this proposed 
system. The electrical load of end-user and the heat load of the HDH 
desalination plant are both satisfied by this power supply system via 
converting the renewable wind and sunlight to electricity. In such sys­
tem, a wind energy conversion system (WECS) composed of several 
wind turbines, a PV array with multiple PV panels, a dump load and 
some power electronic equipment are involved. During operation, the 
electricity generated from WECS and PV array both feeds to direct 
current (DC) bus after passing through the alternating current (AC)/DC 
rectifier and DC/DC converter, respectively. Subsequently, the elec­
tricity on DC bus transfers to AC bus via the DC/AC inverter and finally 
supplies to load. Moreover, the dump load is used to consume the excess 
electricity on AC bus to maintain the system energy balance. 
The water-heated HDH desalination plant with a closed air/open 
water (CAOW) configuration is employed to produce freshwater to end- 
user. This system is made up by a dehumidifier, a humidifier, a seawater 
pump, an air blower and a water-heated heat exchanger. At the begin­
ning, seawater is pumped into the bottom of dehumidifier. Simulta­
neously, the hot humid air with high humidity ratio arrives at the top of 
dehumidifier. Thus, the seawater absorbs the heat in hot humid air and

The UW-CAES system acts the role of energy buffer between the both 
sides of fluctuant end-user and the power supply system. In such system, 
a compressor with after cooler, a turbine with preheater, an energy 
flexible bag, a hot tank, a cold tank, a generator/motor unit and some 
auxiliary devices are included. The energy flexible bag sets below the 
ocean to keep a constant pressure during UW-CAES operation. In charge 
process, the air is pressurized in the compressor by using surplus elec­
tricity from renewables. Meanwhile, the compression heat is absorbed in 
the after cooler by heat storage medium from cold tank 1 and then stored 
in hot tank 1. The cooled air thus is inserted into the flexible energy bag, 
making an expansion of flexible energy bag. In discharge process, the 
stored high-pressure air in flexible energy bag flows into the preheater 
firstly to boost its temperature via the stored heat in hot tank 1, and then 
expands in turbine to produce electricity. At the same time, the flexible 
energy bag shrinks. The cooled heat storage medium out of the preheater 
stores in cold tank 1 again for reuse. 
The heat storage system is employed to store heat for seawater 
desalination in HDH system, which mainly contains a CO2 heat pump, a 
cold tank and a hot tank. In current system, a portion of dumped or 
excess electricity is used to run the CO2 heat pump for heat energy 
generation. Correspondingly, the heat storage medium from cold tank 2 
absorbs the heat to improve its temperature, and then stores in hot tank 
2. When the end-user needs freshwater, the heat storage medium from 
hot tank 2 transfers its heat to seawater in water-heated heat exchanger 
of HDH system, and the related cooled heat storage medium stores in 
cold tank 2 again for reuse. 
2.2. System operation strategy 
The operation strategy of this suggested renewable driven stand­
alone combined power and water supply system with cascade electricity 
and heat storage can be described as follows. 
Initially, the hourly meteorological data of a whole year, such as 
wind speed, solar irradiance and ambient temperature, are selected as 
the inputs for the power supply system. The corresponding hourly wind 
power and PV power are obtained and thereby the total hourly power on 
AC bus is achieved. Meanwhile, the hourly electrical load and the hourly 
freshwater demand are also generated from the electrical load and water 
demand models, respectively. 
Then, according to the total power on AC bus and total electrical 
load, the operation mode of the UW-CAES system can be determined. 
Meanwhile, the status of heat storage system can also be updated. 
While the generated renewable power on AC bus exceeds the elec­
trical load of end-user, the surplus power exists. If the flexible energy 
bag is not full, the UW-CAES system operates in the charge mode. In this 
circumstance, when the total compressed air mass generated by surplus 
power in this hour is lower than the needed air amount to the maximum 
limit of flexible energy bag, all the surplus power is fed to the UW-CAES 
system. Some air is inserted into the flexible bag, resulting in an 
expansile bag volume with more high-pressure air. Otherwise, only a 
portion of surplus power is consumed by UW-CAES system, the other is 
treated as the excess power, making a full flexible energy bag. 
Conversely, if the flexible energy bag is full, all the surplus power is 
considered as the excess power. 
Meanwhile, as for the heat storage system, the excess power is used 
to produce the stored heat energy. If the total amount of hourly gener­
ated heat, stored heat in hot tank 2 and the consumed heat by HDH 
desalination is less than the maximum stored heat amount in hot tank 2, 
all the excess power is converted to heat energy. If not, only a portion of 
excess power is transferred. The remainder power is regarded as the 
dumped power, which is sent to the dump load. 
On the other hand, while the generated renewable power on AC bus 
is insufficient than the electrical load of end-user, the power gap needs 
to be compensated. If the flexible energy bag is empty, the power gap 
cannot be satisfied at all. Conversely, the discharge mode of UW-CAES 
system is activated to fill up the power gap. In this case, if the total air 
mass stored in bag exceeds the needed air amount expanded in turbine, 
the power gap can be compensated completely. If the total air mass in 
bag has a shortage, only a portion of power gap can be satisfied and the 
remaining is regarded as the mismatch power. 
During all simulated periods, the water-heated HDH desalination 
system uses the heat stored in heat storage system to produce freshwater 
to end-user. If the stored heat is sufficient, the freshwater demand can be 
fully satisfied. Otherwise, only a portion of freshwater demand can be 
met, and the rest is recognized as the freshwater mismatch.

5.1. The meteorological condition 
Frankly speaking, to determine the capacity or evaluate the perfor­
mance of any type renewable driven standalone power system, a long- 
term power generation from renewables is indispensable. Because the 
renewables power has great effect by the meteorological condition, the 
hourly wind speed, ambient temperature and solar irradiance in a whole 
year have been selected as the system inputs to generate power. 
In current study, the Xiaowanshan island (21.95◦N, 113.69◦E) 
located near Zhuhai city, Guangdong province in China is selected as the 
case study for testing the proposed system. The hourly mean wind speed, 
ambient temperature and solar irradiance in a whole year at this site are 
gathered to execute the simulation. Fig. 2 gives the collected hourly 
meteorological data in a whole year.

5.3. Design condition performance 
The electricity supply reliability, as well as the freshwater supply 
reliability, is the key performance indicator for current renewable 
driven standalone system. Under the design condition, the maximum 
LPSP, i.e. LPSPmax, is defined as the threshold values for system design 
target. 
At the power supply side, the SWT-20kW type variable pitch wind 
turbine from Senwei Energy and Honey Plus TSM-DD05A(II) type PV 
panel from Trina Solar are employed as the electricity production de­
vices, the detailed information is shown in Table 1 and Table 2, 
respectively. 
In Table 3, the design parameters of the proposed standalone 
renewable driven system under 1% LPSPmax condition are exhibited. The 
corresponding simulated results are given in Table 4. In fact, the 
renewable PV-WT configuration under 1% LPSPmax is (451, 6) with 
2.0737 in DL. Under this configuration, the total electricity generation 
on AC bus from renewables is 585.54 MWh, in which nearly 81.7% is 
contributed by wind. The UW-CAES system stores 55.37 MWh elec­
tricity, whereas it just releases electricity at 35.78 MWh. The heat 
storage system transfers 77.98 MWh electricity to heat for freshwater 
production in HDH desalination system. Unfortunately, 237.98 MWh 
electrical energy is sent to the dump load to maintain the power balance, 
which accounts for more than 40% of the total renewable power gen­
eration, leading to a huge energy waste in such standalone system. The 
power mismatch is 2.524 MWh and the freshwater mismatch is 
43.42ton. In total, the LPSP and LWSP of this suggested system are 
0.9996% and 3.8516%, respectively. The GOR for water-heated HDH 
desalination system is 2.042. 
Based on the renewable resources, the related electrical power pro­
files from PV, WECS and total on AC bus are given in Fig. 6. Obviously,

relatively large value lies in the middle region of plot, which is corre­

highlighted region due to the significant effect from solar energy. 
Fig. 7 gives the profiles of flexible energy bag volume and stored heat

other periods, both energy storage systems are sufficient enough to 
manage the fluctuations between power generation and consumption 
sides. This implies that the long duration energy storage, such as the

5.6. The effect of COP of heat pump cycle 
The function of CO2 heat pump cycle in heat storage system is to 
transfer a certain amount of excess electricity to heat during UW-CAES 
charging. The COP of CO2 heat pump system represents the power to 
heat ability, which also has a significant effect on system performance. 
Fig. 12 plots the effect of heat pump COP on system LPSP, LWSP, DLR 
and DL. Undoubtedly, the LPSP and DL have no relationship with the 
heat pump system. However, a high freshwater supply reliability can be 
achieved with improved COP because more heat can be generated by 
same electricity consumption, resulting in a reduced LWSP. Meanwhile, 
under a fixed heat storage system capacity situation, more renewable 
green electricity is dumped with higher COP due to the less transferred 
excess power via heat pump.

5.9. The effect of flexible energy bag volume 
The flexible energy bag volume reflects the electrical energy storage 
ability of the UW-CAES system. The change of bag volume means the 
total stored air mass in it also varies, as well as the status of UW-CAES 
system. The effect of flexible energy bag volume on system perfor­
mance is shown in Fig. 15. As for a big bag volume, more electricity can 
be stored/released in the charge/discharge process, the electrical power 
supply reliability can be strengthened, and DRL drops correspondingly. 
In such same situation, the available excess power is limited and thus the 
LWSP increases with larger bag volume. Owing to the fixed PV-WT 
configuration and total load, the system DL keeps unvaried. 
5.10. The effect of humidifier seawater inlet temperature 
The seawater inlet temperature of humidifier is a key parameter in 
the water-heated HDH desalination plant, which has remarkable effect 
on the heat and mass transfer characteristics in humification process. 
Fig. 16 depicts the effect of humidifier seawater inlet temperature on 
system performance. As mentioned above, the seawater is heated in the 
WHX by the thermal oil from heat storage system. Thus, the LPSP and DL 
are not related to this parameter, which are unchanged. Furthermore, 
under a fixed temperature heat source condition, the dry air flow rate, 
seawater flow rate and input heat load would decline with increased 
humidifier seawater inlet temperature. Hence, the corresponding pro­
duced freshwater flow rate drops, resulting in a raised LWSP. Mean­
while, the freshwater reduction rate exceeds the reduction rate of input 
heat load, implying a decreased GOR. Moreover, with the improved 
humidifier seawater inlet temperature, same amount of freshwater 
production requires more heat energy. Thus, more excess power is used 
and the corresponding dumped power declines, indicating a decreased 
DLR. 
6. Discussion 
In numerous remote islands and vast coastal areas, the renewable 
driven standalone combined power and water supply system is signifi­
cative and promising for local citizens. In such system, the concerned 
core problem is to obtain a high power/water supply reliability with a 
lower renewable installed capacity. However, due to the inherent nature

(1) The wind-solar mix power source configuration is preferable than

(2) The energy storage device in standalone combined power and 
water supply system must be a hybrid type with more than two

energy storage form needs huge capacity and renewables’ 
installed capacity, resulting in a substantial dumped energy. 
However, the hybrid energy storage can realize the surplus en­
ergy cascade management, the excess power during electrical 
energy storage charging can absorb by the thermal energy storage 
device.  
(3) Corresponding to the hybrid electricity and heat energy storage, 
the simple but reliable thermal-based desalination method may 
be more suitable for the standalone combined power and water 
supply system. 
7. Conclusion 
In most islands or coastal areas, the electricity and freshwater sup­
plies are significant for local residents, which are linked to the living 
quality. In this paper, a renewable driven standalone combined power 
and water supply system with cascade electricity and heat storage for 
remote island is proposed, and then the performance evaluation for a 
case study is conducted. The main conclusions are:  
(1) Required renewables capacity in LWSPmax threshold is larger 
than the same LPSPmax threshold under the design condition. The 
PV-WT configuration for 1% LPSPmax threshold is (451, 6), the 
corresponding LPSP, LWSP and DRL are 0.9996%, 3.8516% and 
40.64%, respectively. On the other hand, for 1% LWSPmax 
threshold situation, the LPSP, LWSP and DRL are 0.4269%, 
0.9976% and 49.35% with a PV-WT configuration at (550, 7). 
Meanwhile, both the electrical load and freshwater demand can 
match well, besides few mismatches in some times.  
(2) Lower LPSP or LWSP performance needs higher renewable 
installed capacity. The DLR increases significantly from 16.58%
